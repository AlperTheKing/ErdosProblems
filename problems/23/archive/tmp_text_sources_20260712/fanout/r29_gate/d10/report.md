## Exact consistency checks

- If those classes really partition all cut edges, the claimed `8422` edges leave `8422-7039 = 1383` non-cut edges. The archive does not define the classes or prove their exhaustiveness/disjointness.

Thus the replacement count is arithmetically consistent only under the unstated requirements that all 680 rows per selector are distinct, exactly one is selected, rigid atoms have exactly one row, and no other atoms/rows exist.

Also `3*6651 = 19953`, consistent with the earlier hub contribution, but the prose says demand includes three HitNeed units without decomposing whether these are already included in the three 6651 terms. - A claimed 2775-vertex active component leaves `2943-2775 = 168` vertices outside it; the archive neither lists them nor rules out hidden active components affecting scope.

## Adversarial findings

No numeric collision was found among the displayed sums. However, every displayed equality is compatible with many non-isomorphic and invalid certificates. In particular:

1. `7039` is only a lower bound witnessed by a cut unless the five-class upper bound is supplied. Names and totals alone do not establish a maximum cut.
2. `Gamma=34575 min` has neither a definition-level expansion nor a comparison certificate. Even `Gamma=34575` is unreplayable; global minimality is wholly unsupported.
3. The histogram does not exclude hidden extra shortest rows. No endpoints, distances, canonical row serialization, or multiplicity convention are provided.
4. The universal replacement assertion depends on definition-sensitive claims: `Q\P != empty`, “previously in exactly one row,” diagonal collision `n(v,v)=2`, and persistence of every positive-score vertex. None has a witness table. The phrase “at least 18 of 26 lock arms per leaf survive” does not itself prove activity under an available formal definition.
5. “Every nontrivial replacement is a selector replacement” requires all 707 rigid atoms to have exactly one admissible row; the prose calls them rigid but supplies no enumeration certificate.
6. The archive explicitly calls the global selector-trade minimum an open question. Consequently **no claim that 30811 is a global minimum is present or supportable**. Any downstream use as a global-minimum counterexample is malformed.
7. Hash provenance fails. The claimed `00186166...` prefix is mentioned, but no filename, complete digest, byte serialization, or matching artifact is supplied. It is not the SHA-256 of the local R29 archive.
