# G7 — Minimum-degree structure theory for Erdős #23, made exact and machine-readable

Label: **G7**.  All arithmetic exact (Python `Fraction` / `int`, C++ `int64`).
Every numeric claim below is reproduced by a script in this directory and was
cross-checked by a second, independently written implementation.

Notation throughout: `psi(H,x) = min over cuts S of H of sum_{uv monochromatic} x_u x_v`
on the standard simplex; `M(H,q) = max over a in Z_{>=0}^{V(H)}, sum a = q, of bip(H[a])`
(zeros allowed).  By accepted facts 1–2, `bip(H[a]) = q^2 psi(H, a/q)` and
`max_x psi(H,x) = sup_q M(H,q)/q^2`, so the conjecture restricted to blow-ups of `H`
is exactly `25 M(H,q) <= q^2` for all `q`.

## (a) The five theorems, stated exactly, with sources and discrepancy flags

Primary source read in full: `round2/brandt_thomasse_vega11.pdf`
(S. Brandt, S. Thomassé, *Dense triangle-free graphs are four-colorable: A solution
to the Erdős–Simonovits problem*), extracted to `round3/G7_bt_raw.txt`; page numbers
below are the paper's own page numbers, which coincide with the PDF page numbers.
Secondary source read in full: `round2/andrasfai_bipartification_0907.3928.pdf`
(P. Heinig, arXiv:0907.3928v3), extracted to `round3/G7_and_raw.txt`.
Third source read: J. Wang, W. Yang, F. Zhao, *Triangle-free Graphs with Large
Minimum Common Degree*, arXiv:2408.05547v1, §1.

### A1. Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós

**Statement.** Every triangle-free graph `G` on `n` vertices with `delta(G) > 2n/5`
is bipartite.  Sharp: the balanced blow-up of `C5` has `delta = 2n/5`.

* General form (AES 1974): for every graph `F` and every `r >= 3`, at most two of
  (i) `F` is `K_r`-free, (ii) `delta(F) > (3r-7)/(3r-4)|F|`, (iii) `chi(F) >= r`
  can hold.  `r = 3` gives `(3r-7)/(3r-4) = 2/5`.
* Citation as given by Wang–Yang–Zhao, arXiv:2408.05547, Theorem 1.2, attributed to
  B. Andrásfai, P. Erdős, V. T. Sós, *On the connection between chromatic number,
  maximal clique and minimal degree of a graph*, Discrete Math. **8** (1974) 205–218.

**⚑ DISCREPANCY (attribution).** Brandt–Thomassé p.2 attribute the `2n/5` bipartiteness
theorem to **Andrásfai alone**, reference [1] = B. Andrásfai, *Graphentheoretische
Extremalprobleme*, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. **15** (1964) 413–438 — not to the
1974 AES paper.  Both attributions occur widely.  The mathematical content is
identical; only the citation differs.  Brandt–Thomassé also note there
that the result is "sharp because of the 5-cycle".

### A2. Häggkvist 1982

**Statement (homomorphism form).** If `G` is triangle-free on `n` vertices and
`delta(G) > floor(3n/8)` then `G` is homomorphic to `C5`.
Source: Wang–Yang–Zhao, arXiv:2408.05547, Theorem 1.3, attributed to
R. Häggkvist, *Odd cycles of specified length in nonbipartite graphs*,
Graph theory (Cambridge 1981), North-Holland Math. Stud. **62**, 89–99.
They add that the balanced blow-up of the **Möbius ladder on 8 vertices**
(= Wagner graph `V8` = `And(3)` = `Gamma_3`) shows the threshold is sharp.

**⚑ DISCREPANCY (strength).** Brandt–Thomassé p.2 record only the weaker
3-colourability form ("every triangle-free graph with minimum degree greater than
`3n/8` is 3-colorable"), with no mention of `C5`.  The homomorphism form is strictly
stronger and is the one accepted fact 7 uses.  Both forms are attributed to the same
1982 paper; I did not obtain the original, so the homomorphism form rests on
Wang–Yang–Zhao (and on the identical statement in several other surveys).

**Sharpness verified here (exact, `G7_chain.py`):** `And(3) = Gamma_3` is
triangle-free, `3`-regular on `8` vertices (`delta/n = 3/8` exactly) and an exhaustive
backtracking search proves **there is no homomorphism `And(3) -> C5`**.  So `3n/8`
cannot be relaxed to `>= 3n/8`.

### A3. Jin 1995

**Statement (the correct one).** If `G` is triangle-free on `n` vertices and
`delta(G) > 10n/29` then `chi(G) <= 3`.  Sharp: Häggkvist's 10-regular 4-chromatic
graph on 29 vertices (a weighted Grötzsch blow-up) has `delta = 10n/29` exactly.
Source: G. Jin, *Triangle-free four-chromatic graphs*, Discrete Math. **145** (1995)
151–170; as recorded by Brandt–Thomassé p.2 ("Jin proved that `3n/8` could be
replaced by `10n/29`, achieving the exact value") and p.18 (`c in ]10/29, 2/5]` gives
`chi_c = 3`).

**⚑ REFUTED (this report): the parenthetical form "Jin 1995: `delta > 10n/29`
implies homomorphic to `C5`" is FALSE.**  See §REFUTED below for the exact falsifier.

**⚑ DISCREPANCY (authorship).** Brandt–Thomassé attribute the `10n/29` 3-colourability
threshold to **Jin [11] (1995)**, and attribute to Chen–Jin–Koh [6] (1997) the
different `n/3` dichotomy theorem.  The 2025 survey "A Dense Neighborhood Lemma"
(arXiv:2504.02992, §6.1) instead attributes the `10n/29` threshold to
**Chen, Jin and Koh (1997)**.  Brandt–Thomassé is the closer source and is
self-consistent (their §"The very end" lists Jin for `]10/29, 2/5]`); I take theirs.

### A4. Chen–Jin–Koh 1997

**Statement, as translated into weighted graphs by Brandt–Thomassé (their Theorem 1,
p.3).**  A weighted maximal triangle-free, twin-free graph with minimum weighted
degree `delta > 1/3` is either isomorphic to a graph `Gamma_i` for some `i >= 1`
(hence 3-colourable), or it contains the Grötzsch graph as an induced subgraph
(hence `chi >= 4`).
Original: C. C. Chen, G. Jin, K. M. Koh, *Triangle-free graphs with large degree*,
Combin. Probab. Comput. **6** (1997) 381–396.

**⚑ GAP FLAG.** Brandt–Thomassé state this as a translation of Chen–Jin–Koh into
"the language of weighted graphs" and give no proof of the translation.  I have not
seen the Chen–Jin–Koh original.  Everything in §(c) below depends on this
translation being correct.

### A5. Brandt–Thomassé (the main theorem)

Definitions, from Brandt–Thomassé:

* p.3: `Gamma_i` is the graph on `{1,2,...,3i-1}` in which vertex `j` has neighbours
  `j+i, j+i+1, ..., j+2i-1`, taken modulo `3i-1`.
* p.2 (weighted graphs): a weight function is `omega : V -> [0,1]` with
  `sum omega = 1`; the degree of `v` is `omega(N_v)`; `delta(G,omega)` is the minimum.
  A **good weighted graph** is a pair `(G,omega)` with `G` maximal triangle-free,
  twin-free, `chi(G) >= 4`, and `delta > 1/3`; `G` is **good** if some `omega` makes
  `(G,omega)` good.
* p.4 (Vega graphs, verbatim construction): for `i >= 2`, take `Gamma_i` on
  `{1,...,3i-1}`, add an edge `xy` and an induced 6-cycle `(a,v,c,u,b,w)` with
  `x` joined to `a,b,c` and `y` joined to `u,v,w`; the neighbours of `a` and of `u`
  in `Gamma_i` are `{1,...,i}`; the neighbours of `b` and of `v` are `{i+1,...,2i}`;
  the neighbours of `c` and of `w` are `{2i+1,...,3i-1}`.  This graph on `3i+7`
  vertices is `Upsilon_i`.  The other Vega graphs are `Upsilon_i - {y}` and
  `Upsilon_i - {2i}` (both on `3i+6` vertices) and `Upsilon_i - {y,2i}`
  (on `3i+5` vertices).  `Upsilon_2 - {y,4}` is the Grötzsch graph.

**Theorem 4 (p.4).** A graph is good if and only if it is a Vega graph.

**Corollary 4.1 (p.4).** The twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with
`delta > 1/3` are the 3-colourable graphs `Gamma_i`, `i >= 1`, and the 4-chromatic
Vega graphs.

**Corollary 4.2 (p.5).** Every triangle-free graph with `delta > n/3` is 4-colourable.

**Corollary 4.3(3) (p.5).** Every triangle-free graph with `delta > in/(3i-1)` is
homomorphic to `Upsilon_{i-1}`, for `i >= 3`.

**Corollary 4.3(4) (p.5).** Every maximal triangle-free, twin-free weighted graph
with `delta > i/(3i-1)` has at most `3i-4` vertices, for `i >= 2`.

**⚑ TYPO IN THE SOURCE (flagged, and shown harmless).** The proof of Corollary 4.3(4)
on p.5 reads "the graphs under consideration are the graphs `Gamma_j`, `j < i`, with
at most `3i-4` vertices and **Vega graphs with at most `i` vertices**".  The italicised
clause is wrong as written (the smallest Vega graph already has 11 vertices, so for
`i < 11` the clause would be vacuous while the conclusion is not).  The intended
clause is "Vega graphs with at most `3i-4` vertices", and this is correct: with the
regular degrees of Theorem 3 one computes exactly
`delta(Upsilon_j) > i/(3i-1) <=> i >= 9j-5`, `delta(Upsilon_j - y) > i/(3i-1) <=> i >= 9j-6`,
`delta(Upsilon_j - {y,2j}) > i/(3i-1) <=> i >= 9j-7`, and in each case the order of
the Vega graph is `<= 3i-4` for all `j >= 2`.  The statement of Corollary 4.3(4) is
therefore correct as stated; only its one-line proof is mis-typed.

**⚑ SECOND FLAG (p.4, parenthetical).** Brandt–Thomassé write that
`Upsilon_i - {2i}` "does not occur in [5], perhaps due to the fact that for `i = 2`
both graphs are isomorphic".  **Verified exactly** (`G7_patterns.py`):
`Upsilon_2 - {y} ≅ Upsilon_2 - {4}`, while `Upsilon_i - {y} ≇ Upsilon_i - {2i}`
for `i = 3, 4, 5`.

### The list is INFINITE and it COLLAPSES to one chain

**Theorem B1 (collapse, proof by construction).**  For every `i >= 2`,
`Gamma_i`, `Upsilon_i - {y}`, `Upsilon_i - {2i}` and `Upsilon_i - {y,2i}` are
**induced subgraphs of `Upsilon_i`** (the last three by definition — vertex deletion;
`Gamma_i` because `Upsilon_i` restricted to `{1,...,3i-1}` is `Gamma_i` by
construction), and `Gamma_1 = K_2` is an induced subgraph of `Upsilon_2`.
By accepted fact 3 (`max psi` is monotone under induced subgraphs),
`max_x psi(H,x) <= max_x psi(Upsilon_i, x)` for every `H` in the list with index `i`.

**Theorem B2 (chain).**  For every `m >= 3`, put `D = {m, 2m, 3m-1}` inside
`V(Gamma_m) = {1,...,3m-1}` (note `3m-1 ≡ 0`, so `D = {0, m, 2m}` as residues).
The order-preserving relabelling `psi_D(j) = j - #{d in D : d < j}` is an isomorphism
`Gamma_m - D -> Gamma_{m-1}` mapping `X_m\{m\}` onto `X_{m-1}`, `Y_m\{2m\}` onto
`Y_{m-1}`, `Z_m\{3m-1\}` onto `Z_{m-1}`.  Extending it by the identity on
`{x,y,a,b,c,u,v,w}` gives an isomorphism `Upsilon_m - D -> Upsilon_{m-1}`.
Equivalently, in the index `i = m-1`:

> **`Upsilon_i ≅ Upsilon_{i+1} - {i+1, 2i+2, 3i+2}`, an induced subgraph.**

*Proof.*  Use the circular-distance form: `j ~ k` in `Gamma_m` iff
`dist_{3m-1}(j,k) >= m`.  Let `j < k` be non-deleted, `p = k-j`, `p' = 3m-1-p`,
and let `delta, delta'` be the numbers of elements of `D` in the two open arcs, so
`delta + delta' = 3` and `psi_D(k) - psi_D(j) = p - delta`, `3m-4-(p-delta) = p'-delta'`.
We must show `min(p,p') >= m` iff `min(p-delta, p'-delta') >= m-1`.
The cyclic gaps between consecutive elements of `D` are `m, m, m-1`.
(⇐) If `delta = 0` then `j,k` lie strictly inside one gap, so `p <= m-2` and
`p - delta <= m-2 < m-1`; hence `delta >= 1` and `p >= (m-1) + delta >= m`; same for `p'`.
(⇒) If `delta = 1`, `p >= m > m-1+1-1`, fine.  If `delta = 2`, the two `D`-points in
the arc span at least `m-1`, plus one step at each end, so `p >= m+1 = (m-1)+2`.
If `delta = 3`, the three `D`-points span at least `2m-1`, so `p >= 2m+1 >= (m-1)+3`.
Symmetrically for `p', delta'`.  ∎

Verified **entry by entry** (every pair of vertices, both for `Gamma` and for the full
`Upsilon`) for `m = 3..24` by `G7_psi_map.py`.

**Consequence.**  `max_x psi(Upsilon_2, x) <= max_x psi(Upsilon_3, x) <= ...`, and
the whole Brandt–Thomassé pattern list is dominated by the single chain
`{Upsilon_i : i >= 2}`.  The reduction target is therefore **one infinite chain of
graphs on `3i+7` vertices**, not four families plus the Andrásfai graphs.

## (c) The reduction, with complete proof and an exact gap analysis

Write `A_H` for the adjacency matrix of `H` and, for `x` in the simplex,
`delta(H,x) = min_v (A_H x)_v`.

### Lemma R0 (regular weightings are optimal)
If `H` admits `w* >= 0`, `sum w* = 1`, with `A_H w* = d* 1`, then
`max { delta(H,w) : w >= 0, sum w = 1 } = d*`.
*Proof.* Let `w >= 0`, `sum w = 1`, `d = delta(H,w)`, so `A_H w >= d·1`.  Then
`d* = d*(1^T w) = (A_H w*)^T w = w*^T (A_H w) >= d (w*^T 1) = d.` ∎
(Brandt–Thomassé assert the same on p.5 via LP duality.)
Hence `delta_reg(Gamma_i) = i/(3i-1)`, `delta_reg(Upsilon_i) = (9i-6)/(27i-19)`,
`delta_reg(Upsilon_i - y) = delta_reg(Upsilon_i - 2i) = (9i-7)/(27i-22)`,
`delta_reg(Upsilon_i - \{y,2i\}) = (9i-8)/(27i-25)` (Theorem 3 weights, verified in (b)).

### Theorem R1 (the reduction as requested)
> Let `L = {Gamma_i : i >= 1} ∪ {Upsilon_i, Upsilon_i - y, Upsilon_i - 2i,
> Upsilon_i - {y,2i} : i >= 2}` (equivalently, by Theorem B1, `L' = {Upsilon_i : i >= 2}`).
> If `max_x psi(H,x) <= 1/25` for every `H in L'`, then `bip(G) <= N^2/25` for every
> triangle-free `G` on `N` vertices with `delta(G) > N/3`.

*Proof.*
1. **Completion.** Let `Ghat` be any maximal triangle-free graph on the same vertex
   set containing `G` (add missing edges greedily while triangle-freeness holds).
   By accepted fact 6, `bip(G) <= bip(Ghat)`, and `delta(Ghat) >= delta(G) > N/3`,
   with `|V(Ghat)| = N` unchanged.  So it suffices to bound `bip(Ghat)`.
2. **Twin quotient.** Twins (equal neighbourhoods) are non-adjacent, so each twin
   class is independent; let `H` be the quotient of `Ghat` by twin classes and let
   `a_v >= 1` be the class sizes, `sum a = N`.  Then `Ghat = H[a]` exactly.
   `H` is triangle-free (a triangle in `H` lifts to a triangle in `Ghat`);
   `H` is twin-free by construction; `H` is maximal triangle-free, because two
   non-adjacent classes `C, C'` have representatives with a common neighbour `w` in
   `Ghat`, and the class of `w` is adjacent to both and equal to neither.
3. **Degrees.** For a class `v`, `(A_H a)_v` is the `Ghat`-degree of any member of `v`,
   so `delta(H, a/N) = delta(Ghat)/N > 1/3`.  All weights `a_v/N` are `> 0`.
4. **Classification.** By Brandt–Thomassé Corollary 4.1, `H in L`.
5. **Bound.** By accepted fact 1, `bip(Ghat) = bip(H[a]) = N^2 psi(H, a/N)
   <= N^2 max_x psi(H,x) <= N^2/25` (using Theorem B1 to pass from `L` to `L'`). ∎

### The four points the assignment asks about

**(i) Does the maximal-triangle-free completion preserve the degree hypothesis?**
Yes, and strictly for free: adding edges only increases degrees and does not change
`N`, so `delta(Ghat) >= delta(G) > N/3`.  `bip` is monotone under edge addition
(accepted fact 6), so nothing is lost.  Note the completion is *not* unique in general
(uniqueness for `delta > n/3` is Brandt's result quoted on p.17 of Brandt–Thomassé);
the proof does not need uniqueness.

**(ii) Does Brandt–Thomassé need `G` connected?**  No connectivity hypothesis appears,
and none is needed: a maximal triangle-free graph on `>= 2` vertices is automatically
connected, since adding an edge between two components can never create a triangle.
(Brandt–Thomassé p.2: maximal triangle-free ⟺ diameter `<= 2`.)  Step 2 above produces
a maximal triangle-free `H`, hence connected.

**(iii) Is the Vega list finite or infinite?**  **INFINITE**, and irreducibly so for
the band `delta > N/3`: `delta_reg(Upsilon_i) = (9i-6)/(27i-19) > 1/3` for every
`i >= 2`, decreasing to `1/3`.  So no finite sub-list suffices for `delta > N/3`.
Theorem B1+B2 compress the list to the single chain `Upsilon_2 ⊂ Upsilon_3 ⊂ ...`,
but the chain is still infinite and `max_x psi(Upsilon_i,x)` is non-decreasing in `i`,
so what Theorem R1 really needs is
`lim_{i -> infinity} max_x psi(Upsilon_i, x) <= 1/25`.
For any **fixed** threshold `c > 1/3` the list is finite — see Theorem R3.

**(iv) Does the target inherit any hypothesis?**  There is **no homomorphism** in the
proof: step 2 is an exact blow-up identity `Ghat = H[a]`, which is strictly stronger.
`H` does inherit a weighted degree condition, `delta(H, a/N) > 1/3` — but the quantity
bounded in step 5, `max_x psi(H,x)`, ranges over the **whole** simplex and therefore
throws that information away.  Keeping it gives the sharper Theorem R2.

### Theorem R2 (degree-restricted reduction — sharper, same proof)
> For a pattern `H` set
> `Psi(H,c) = sup { psi(H,x) : x in simplex, (A_H x)_v > c for all v }`.
> If `Psi(H, 1/3) <= 1/25` for every `H in L`, then `bip(G) <= N^2/25` for every
> triangle-free `G` with `delta(G) > N/3`.

*Proof.* Identical to R1; in step 5 note `x = a/N` satisfies `(A_H x)_v > 1/3` by step 3. ∎

This is strictly stronger information because the feasible polytope
`P_H(1/3) = {x : (A_H x)_v >= 1/3}` is small.  Two exact consequences, both computed
below in (d):

* On `C5` the restriction changes nothing: `Psi(C5, 1/3) = 1/25`, attained at the
  uniform point.  So the constant `1/25` in R2 is still tight and cannot be improved.
* On `Gamma_3 = And(3) = Wagner` the restriction bites hard.  The `C5` blow-up point
  is **not** feasible: for the induced `C5` on `{0,2,4,5,7}` of `Gamma_3` the vertex
  `6` has `N(6) = {1,2,3}`, so its weighted degree is `1/5 < 1/3`.  The exhaustive
  degree-restricted maximum of `25 M_deg(Gamma_3,q)/q^2` over `q <= 64` is `25/28`,
  strictly below `1`, whereas the unrestricted maximum is exactly `1`.
* **⚑ CAVEAT.** Accepted fact 3 (induced-subgraph monotonicity) **fails** for
  `Psi(H,c)`: the proof extends `x` by zeros, which violates the degree constraint.
  So Theorems B1/B2 do *not* collapse the list for R2; every pattern must be treated
  separately in the R2 formulation.

### Theorem R3 (the exact ladder — an unconditional band-shrinking scheme)
By Lemma R0 the largest weighted min-degree a pattern can carry is `delta_reg`, and
a direct computation (`G7_ladder.py`) shows that **every Vega regular degree equals an
Andrásfai regular degree**:
`delta_reg(Upsilon_j) = t/(3t-1)` with `t = 9j-6`;
`delta_reg(Upsilon_j - y) = delta_reg(Upsilon_j - 2j) = t/(3t-1)` with `t = 9j-7`;
`delta_reg(Upsilon_j - {y,2j}) = t/(3t-1)` with `t = 9j-8`.
Hence the set of attainable thresholds is exactly `{t/(3t-1) : t >= 1}`, strictly
decreasing to `1/3`.  Therefore, for every integer `t >= 1`:

> **If `max_x psi(H,x) <= 1/25` for every `H` in the FINITE set
> `L_t = { H in L : delta_reg(H) > t/(3t-1) }`, then `bip(G) <= N^2/25` for every
> triangle-free `G` with `delta(G) > tN/(3t-1)`.**

*Proof.* Run R1; in step 4, `delta_reg(H) >= delta(H,a/N) = delta(Ghat)/N > t/(3t-1)`,
so `H in L_t`. ∎  (Same statement holds with `Psi(H, t/(3t-1))` in place of
`max_x psi`, by R2.)

The Brandt–Thomassé route for the whole band `delta > N/3` terminates at:

> **(BL1)  `lim_{i -> infinity} max_x psi(Upsilon_i, x) <= 1/25`,**

This is an infinite family and no finite computation discharges it; the sequence is
monotone by Theorem B2, so it is exactly a limit statement.  BL1 is **not** of strength
at least the conjecture (it concerns one explicit chain, not all triangle-free graphs),
so the route is not dead — but it cannot be closed by enumeration.

The individual rungs of Theorem R3 terminate at explicit finite lemmas.  The first
open one is

> **(BL2)  `max_x psi(Gamma_3, x) <= 1/25`, where `Gamma_3 = And(3)` is the Wagner
> graph `V8` (8 vertices, 12 edges, circulant `Z_8` with connection set `{3,4,5}`).**

A second, weaker but possibly easier target is the degree-restricted form:
