- Its only publication is: J. Balogh, F. C. Clemen, B. Lidický, *Max cuts in triangle-free

- The bound is **n²/23.5 = 2n²/47 = 0.04255319… n²**, *not* 0.0409 n². Note

- Confirmed as still current by the same authors 5 years later: J. Balogh, C. Chen,

**Corrected record: the best published unconditional bound is `bip(G) ≤ n²/23.5 = 2n²/47`

**0.2 The ψ-plateau (campaign fact 3) makes every ψ table below a lower bound only.**
Every graph in every family discussed here (Andrásfai, Vega, Grötzsch, Wagner, Higman–Sims,
Clebsch) has odd girth 5, hence `max_x ψ(H,x) ≥ 1/25` automatically. Any computed value
below 1/25 is a non-maximal point, not evidence. The correct target for these families is
the **equality** `max_x ψ(H,x) = 1/25`, i.e. that the C5-induced point is a *global* maximum.

## (a) Full history and every published upper bound

### a.1 Origin of the problem

The `n²/25` conjecture is **Erdős's**, stated at the 5th British Combinatorial Conference:
P. Erdős, *Problems and results in graph theory and combinatorial analysis*, Proc. Fifth
British Combinatorial Conf. (Aberdeen 1975), Congressus Numerantium XV, 169–192, 1976; and
repeated in P. Erdős, *Some old and new problems in various branches of combinatorics*,
Discrete Math. 165/166 (1997) 227–231. The 1988 paper of Erdős–Faudree–Pach–Spencer is where
it is first attacked; the paper attributes the conjecture to Erdős, verbatim (p. 87):

> "According to a long-standing conjecture of Erdős (see [3, 4, 8]), in the last assertion,
> (1/18 − ε) n² can be replaced by n²/25. This bound, if valid, would be best possible. (It is
> also conjectured that a K₄-free graph with n vertices can be made bipartite by the omission
> of (1/9 + o(1))·n² edges. The complete tripartite graph with n/3 vertices in each class shows
> that this conjecture, if true, is also the best possible.)"

The 5n-vertex phrasing ("every triangle-free graph on 5n vertices can be made bipartite by
deleting at most n² edges") is how the problem is recorded on the UCSD Erdős problems page.

### a.2 Erdős, Faudree, Pach, Spencer 1988 — the source of `1/18`

P. Erdős, R. Faudree, J. Pach, J. Spencer, **"How to make a graph bipartite"**,
*J. Combin. Theory Ser. B* **45** (1988) 86–98, doi:10.1016/0095-8956(88)90057-3.
(Received 5 June 1986; dedicated to Turán.) Verbatim statements:

> **THEOREM 1.** *Every triangle-free graph G with n vertices and m edges can be made
> bipartite by the omission of at most*
> `min{ m/2 − 2m(2m² − n³)/(n²(n² − 2m)) ,  m − 4m²/n² }`
> *edges.*

> **THEOREM 2.** *There is a (calculatable) constant ε > 0 such that every triangle-free graph
> G with n vertices can be made bipartite by the omission of at most (1/18 − ε + o(1)) n² edges.*

> **THEOREM 3.** *For every forbidden graph F and for every c > 0 there is a constant
> ε(F, c) > 0 such that any F-free graph G with n vertices and m ≥ cn² edges can be made
> bipartite by the omission of at most (m/2) − ε(F, c) n² edges.*

> **COROLLARY 2.6.** *Every triangle-free graph G with n vertices can be made bipartite by the
> omission of at most n²/18 + n/2 edges.*

Supporting lemmas that are actually usable (all verbatim):

> **LEMMA 2.1.** *Every triangle-free graph G has a vertex x such that
> |E(G[Γ̄(x)])| ≤ |E(G)| − 4|E(G)|²/|V(G)|².*
> (Here Γ̄(x) = V(G) − ({x} ∪ Γ(x)).)

> **PROPOSITION 2.5.** *Let G be a graph and W ⊆ V(G) a set of vertices such that the subgraph
> G[W] induced by W can be made bipartite by the omission of δ edges. Then G can be made
> bipartite by the omission of at most |E(G)|/2 − |E(G[W])|/2 + δ edges.*

> **LEMMA 2.4.** *Let G be a graph with n vertices and m edges. Then
> (i) G has an edge which is contained in at least 8m³/n⁴ − 6m/n quadrilaterals.
> (ii) If, in addition, G is triangle-free then it has an edge contained in at least
> 4m(2m² − n³)/(n²(n² − 2m)) quadrilaterals.*

> **THEOREM 4.** *Let f = f(n, m) denote the maximum integer satisfying the condition that every
> triangle-free graph with n vertices and at least m edges contains an induced bipartite subgraph
> with at least f edges. Then
> (i) ½m^{1/3} − 1 ≤ f(n, m) ≤ c m^{1/3} log² m  if m < n^{3/2},
> (ii) 4m²/n⁴ ≤ f(n, m) ≤ c (m³/n⁴) log³(n²/m)  if m ≥ n^{3/2}.*

The paper's last section generalises Theorem 3 to p-partite (Theorem 3′) and answers a
question of Füredi (Theorem 5.3). It contains **no** minimum-degree result.

**Exact density consequence of Theorem 1 (verified in `G11_verify_andrasfai.py`, part E):**
put `m = c n²`. The second term is `(c − 4c²)n²`, and `c − 4c² ≤ 1/25` iff `c ≤ 1/20` or
`c ≥ 1/5`, with **equality at both endpoints**. So EFPS Theorem 1 already proves the
conjecture for `m ≤ n²/20` and for `m ≥ n²/5`, tightly at both ends. (BCL quote the same
ranges as "edge density ≤ 0.172 and ≥ 0.4", using density `= m/binom(n,2) ≈ 2m/n²`; the
lower end 0.172 comes from the *first* term of Theorem 1, which beats `m − 4m²/n²` there.)

### a.3 Erdős, Győri, Simonovits 1992 — reduction to C5 blow-ups at high density

P. Erdős, E. Győri, M. Simonovits, **"How many edges should be deleted to make a
triangle-free graph bipartite?"**, in *Sets, graphs and numbers (Budapest, 1991)*, Colloq.
Math. Soc. János Bolyai **60**, North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1992, 239–263.
(This volume is not online; I could not obtain the original text. What follows is the
statement as quoted by Balogh–Clemen–Lidický, who use it as an input.)

BCL, arXiv:2103.14179, verbatim:

> **Theorem 3 (Erdős, Győri and Simonovits [5]).** *Let G be a K₃-free graph on n vertices with
> at least n²/5 edges. Then there exists an unbalanced blow-up of C5 H such that D2(G) ≤ D2(H).*

and, for the blow-up monotonicity (their eq. (8)), citing **[5, Theorem 7]**:

> `D2(G′) / (⌈n₀/n⌉ n)² ≥ D2(G) / n²`, where `G′` is the balanced blow-up of `G` with
> parts of size `⌈n₀/n⌉`.

**This EGS Theorem 7 is the source of campaign facts 1 and 4.** It is *published*; the
campaign should cite EGS 1992 Theorem 7 rather than re-deriving it.

The UCSD Erdős-problems page summarises: *"Erdös, Györi and Simonovits proved the conjecture
holds for graphs containing at least 5n² edges"* (i.e. n²/5 in the 5n-vertex normalisation),
*"the general case remains unresolved for graphs with edge counts e satisfying 2n² < e ≤ 5n²."*

EGS is also the source of a canonical-edge-deletion theorem used elsewhere: BCL ("10
problems", §2.1) write verbatim: *"A result of Erdős, Győri and Simonovits [10, Theorem 7]
states that there is a canonical 'edge deletion' achieving the minimum of D₃(H)."*

### a.4 Poljak–Tuza — **not** about this problem

S. Poljak, Zs. Tuza, *Bipartite subgraphs of triangle-free graphs*, **SIAM J. Discrete Math.
7 (1994) 307–313**. Their theorem is about the *m*-parametrised error term, not `n²`:
they improve Erdős–Lovász to `f(m) ≥ m/2 + c (m log m)^{2/3}` (largest bipartite subgraph of a
triangle-free graph with m edges), with a polynomial-time algorithm where the earlier proof
was probabilistic. **This is `m/2 + o(m)` and therefore worthless for the `n²/25` constant**
(campaign rules of evidence, clause 3). The sharper `m/2 + c m^{4/5}` line is Alon–Bollobás–
Krivelevich–Sudakov, *Maximum cuts and judicious partitions in graphs without short cycles*,
J. Combin. Theory Ser. B **88** (2003) 329–346. Same verdict.

### a.5 Balogh, Clemen, Lidický 2021 — the current record

J. Balogh, F. C. Clemen, B. Lidický, **"Max cuts in triangle-free graphs"**, Extended
Abstracts EuroComb 2021, Trends in Mathematics **14**, Springer, 509–514, 2021,
doi:10.1007/978-3-030-83823-2_82; arXiv:2103.14179. Verbatim:

> **Theorem 2.** *Let G be a triangle-free graph on n vertices. Then, for n large enough,*
> *(a) D2(G) ≤ n²/23.5 ,*
> *(b) D2(G) ≤ n²/25 when |E(G)| ≥ 0.3197·binom(n,2),*
> *(c) D2(G) ≤ n²/25 when |E(G)| ≤ 0.2486·binom(n,2).*

Their Theorem 4 is the "just below density 2/5" case:

> **Theorem 4.** *There exists n₀ such that for all n ≥ n₀ the following holds. Let G be an
> n-vertex triangle-free graph with |E(G)| ≥ (0.2 − ε)n² edges, where ε = 10⁻⁸. Then
> D2(G) ≤ n²/25.*

with the key deletion lemma

> **Lemma 1.** `D2(G) ≤ (3/32)(n² − m² + n − m) + D2(G_m)`

where `G_m` is the graph left after iteratively deleting minimum-degree vertices until
`δ(G_i) > (3/8) i` or `⌊5·10⁻⁷ n⌋` rounds have passed. They then split on Häggkvist (early
stop) vs. EGS Theorem 3 (late stop).

**Summary table of every published unconditional upper bound on `bip(G)` for triangle-free G.**

## (b) Is the conjecture known under a minimum-degree hypothesis?

**Answer: yes for `δ > ⌊3n/8⌋` only (via Häggkvist ⇒ hom. to C5), plus a strictly stronger
2024 minimum-*common*-degree version. Nothing is proved at `δ > n/3` or `δ > 10n/29`;
the `δ > n/3` case reduces exactly to two explicit graph families and is open.**

### b.1 `δ > ⌊3n/8⌋` — settled (this is campaign fact 7, and here is the verbatim source)

Häggkvist 1982, as stated verbatim in J. Wang, W. Yang, F. Zhao, *Triangle-free graphs with
large minimum common degree*, arXiv:2408.05547 (10 Aug 2024), Theorem 1.3:

> **Theorem 1.3 ([2]).** *If G is a triangle-free graph on n vertices with δ(G) > ⌊3n/8⌋, then
> G is homomorphic to C5.*

with sharpness recorded verbatim in the same paper:

> "Define the Möbius ladder H_M to be the graph obtained from a cycle of length 8 by adding 4
> chords joining vertices of distance 4 on the cycle. A balanced H_M-blow-up on n vertices
> shows that Theorem 1.3 is sharp."

BCL's phrasing (arXiv:2103.14179 §2.2) is the "subgraph of a blow-up" form:
*"a result by Häggkvist [6] who proved that every triangle-free graph on n vertices with
minimum degree more than 3n/8 is a subgraph of a C5-blow-up."*

**Verified here:** the Möbius ladder `H_M` is exactly the Andrásfai graph `And_3 = Γ_3` =
Wagner graph `V8` (`G11_verify_andrasfai.py`, part D, brute-force isomorphism), and
`bip(V8) = 2`, `bip/n² = 1/32 = 0.03125`.

### b.2 **NEW (2024): `δ₂ > ⌊n/8⌋` ⇒ hom. to C5 — strictly stronger than Häggkvist**

J. Wang, W. Yang, F. Zhao, *Triangle-free Graphs with Large Minimum Common Degree*,
arXiv:2408.05547, 2024. Here `δ₂(G) := min{|N(x) ∩ N(y)| : xy ∉ E(G)}`. Verbatim:

> **Theorem 1.4.** *Let G be a triangle-free graph on n vertices. Then (i) and (ii) hold.*
> *(i) If δ₂(G) > ⌊n/5⌋ and n ≥ 5, then G is bipartite.*
> *(ii) If δ₂(G) > ⌊n/8⌋ and n ≥ 8, then G is homomorphic to C5.*

with the free conversion tool (their Lemma 1.5, due to Liu–Shangguan–Skokan–Xu):

> **Lemma 1.5 ([4]).** *If G is a maximal triangle-free graph on n vertices with
> δ(G) > (1/3 + α)n, then δ₂(G) > 3αn.*

and the verbatim remark that this is a **strict** strengthening:

> "Let G₂ be a C5-blow-up with vertices of C5 being replaced by independent sets of sizes
> n/7, 2n/7, n/7, n/7 and 2n/7 consecutively. It is easy to check that … δ₂(G₂) = n/7 > n/8
> but … δ(G₂) = 2n/7 < 3n/8. Thus the condition δ₂(G) > 3αn is strictly stronger than
> δ(G) > (1/3 + α)n."

**Consequence for this campaign (immediate, no new mathematics):** since `G → C5` implies
`bip(G) ≤ n²/25` (campaign fact 7, AM-GM twice), the conjecture is **unconditionally true for
every triangle-free G with `δ₂(G) > ⌊n/8⌋`**, a region strictly containing `δ(G) > ⌊3n/8⌋`.
A minimal counterexample is maximal triangle-free (fact 6), so `δ₂ ≥ 1` for free, and the
open region is `1 ≤ δ₂(G) ≤ ⌊n/8⌋`.

### b.3 `δ > n/3`: reduces exactly to Andrásfai + Vega graphs, and is OPEN

Chain of three published theorems.

**(i) Chen–Jin–Koh 1997.** C. C. Chen, G. P. Jin, K. M. Koh, *Triangle-free graphs with large
degree*, *Combin. Probab. Comput.* **6** (1997) 381–396. As stated verbatim by
Brandt–Thomassé (Theorem 1), in weighted form:

> **Theorem 1** *A weighted maximal triangle-free, twin-free graph with minimum degree
> δ > 1/3 is either isomorphic to a graph Γ_i, for some i ≥ 1, and therefore 3-colorable, or it
> contains the Grötzsch graph as an induced subgraph and therefore its chromatic number is at
> least 4.*

where (Brandt–Thomassé, verbatim) *"we denote by Γ_i, for every integer i ≥ 1, the graph on
vertex set {1, 2, …, 3i − 1} where the vertex j has neighbors j + i, …, j + 2i − 1, these
values taken modulo 3i − 1"* — i.e. `Γ_i` is the **Andrásfai graph** `And_i` (verified
isomorphic to Heinig's definition for `i = 2..7` in `G11_verify_andrasfai.py`, part D).

The quantitative form used by Łuczak–Polcyn–Reiher (arXiv:2002.01498, Thm 1.3) and quoted in
the literature is: *triangle-free, χ(G) ≤ 3 and δ(G) > ((k+1)/(3k+2))·n ⇒ G → And_k*.
Thresholds: `k=2 : 3/8`, `k=3 : 4/11`, `k=4 : 5/14`, `k=5 : 6/17`, … → `1/3`.

**(ii) Jin 1995.** G. Jin, *Triangle-free four-chromatic graphs*, *Discrete Math.* **145**
(1995) 151–170: every triangle-free `G` with `δ(G) > 10n/29` has `χ(G) ≤ 3`. Sharp: Häggkvist's
10-regular triangle-free 4-chromatic graph on 29 vertices (obtained by weighting the Grötzsch
graph). Brandt–Thomassé, verbatim: *"Using a suitable weight function on the set of vertices of
the Grötzsch graph, Häggkvist [10] gave a counterexample to this question with minimum degree
10n/29. He proved also that every triangle-free graph with minimum degree greater than 3n/8 is
3-colorable. Later on, Jin [11] proved that 3n/8 could be replaced by 10n/29, achieving the
exact value."*

Combining (i)+(ii): **`δ(G) > 10n/29` ⇒ `G` is homomorphic to some Andrásfai graph `And_k`.**

**(iii) Brandt–Thomassé.** S. Brandt, S. Thomassé, *Dense triangle-free graphs are
four-colorable: a solution to the Erdős–Simonovits problem* (manuscript, "to appear in
J. Combin. Theory Ser. B"; the file `vega11.pdf` on Thomassé's page is the one I read).
Verbatim:

> **Theorem 4** *A graph is good if and only if it is a Vega graph.*
>
> **Corollary 4.1** *The twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with δ > 1/3 are the
> 3-colorable graphs Γ_i for i ≥ 1 and the 4-chromatic Vega graphs.*
>
> **Corollary 4.2** *Every triangle-free graph with minimum degree > n/3 is 4-colorable.*
>
> **Corollary 4.3** *(3) Every triangle-free graph with minimum degree > in/(3i − 1) is
> homomorphic to Υ_{i−1}, for i ≥ 3.*

("good weighted graph" = maximal triangle-free, twin-free, χ ≥ 4, with a weight function of
minimum weighted degree > 1/3.) **Since twin-identification and maximal-triangle-free
completion are both homomorphisms that do not decrease δ, Corollary 4.1 says exactly:
every triangle-free `G` with `δ(G) > n/3` is homomorphic to some `And_i` or to some Vega
graph.** With campaign fact 2 this is an *exact reduction*, not a relaxation.

**Vega graphs, verbatim construction (Brandt–Thomassé §1):**

> "For some integer i ≥ 2, start with a graph Γ_i on vertex set {1, …, 3i − 1} and add an edge
> xy and an induced 6-cycle (a, v, c, u, b, w) such that x is joined to a, b, c and y is joined
> to u, v, w. The set of neighbors of a, u on the Γ_i graph is {1, …, i}. The set of neighbors
> of b, v on the Γ_i graph is {i + 1, …, 2i}. The set of neighbors of c, w on the Γ_i graph is
> {2i + 1, …, 3i − 1}. This is the sole Vega graph on 3i + 7 vertices. We denote it by Υ_i.
> There are two Vega graphs on 3i + 6 vertices, obtained from Υ_i by a simple vertex deletion.
> The first one is Υ_i − {y}, the second Υ_i − {2i} … Finally, the last Vega graph, on 3i + 5
> vertices, is Υ_i − {y, 2i}. Observe that the Vega graph Υ_2 − {y, 4} is exactly the Grötzsch
> graph."

> **Theorem 3** *Every Vega graph is a regular good weighted graph with positive weight function.*
> "Υ_i: Assign weight 1 to the vertices x, y, 1, 2i, weight 3i − 3 to c, w, weight 3i − 2 to
> u, v, a, b and weight 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i − 6, and the total weight
> is 27i − 19." (+ three analogous weightings for the deleted variants.)

**All of this is machine-verified in `G11_verify_vega.py`** for `i = 2..6`: each of the four
Vega families is triangle-free, maximal triangle-free, twin-free; the stated weights are
regular with exactly the stated degree `9i−6` / `9i−7` / `9i−7` / `9i−8` and total weight
`27i−19` / `27i−22` / `27i−22` / `27i−25`; `Υ_2 − {y,4}` has 11 vertices, 20 edges, χ = 4
(Grötzsch); and `δ = (9i−6)/(27i−19) > 1/3` by exactly 1 in the numerator
(`27i−18 > 27i−19`).

**Status of the `δ > n/3` case: OPEN.** The only published fragment is:

P. Heinig, *The Erdős bipartification conjecture is true in the special case of Andrásfai
graphs*, arXiv:0907.3928v3 (2009). Verbatim:

> **Theorem 3.** *For every integer k ≥ 2 the set of edges F_k := U_k^(1) ∪ U_k^(2) … is a
> minimal bipartification of And_k in the stronger sense that omitting an element from it
> creates a 5-cycle. … Moreover, the set F_k consists of exactly ⌊k²/4⌋ = ⌊(1/36)(|And_k| + 1)²⌋
> edges.*
>
> **Conjecture 5.** *The set F_k defined in Theorem 3 is a minimum bipartification for And_k.*
>
> **Conjecture 6.** *By considering several copies of the bipartification F_k, and then
> optimizing a system of quadratic inequalities, it is possible to prove that an arbitrary
> homomorphic preimage H of And_k can be made bipartite by deleting at most (1/25)|H|² edges.*

**Verified here (`G11_verify_andrasfai.py`):** `F_k` really is a bipartification of `And_k` and
`|F_k| = ⌊k²/4⌋ = ⌊(3k)²/36⌋` for `k = 2..8`; `⌊(n+1)²/36⌋ ≤ n²/25` for `n = 3k−1`,
`k = 2..14`, with **equality only at k = 2 (C5)**; and by exhaustive max-cut
`bip(And_k) = ⌊k²/4⌋` for `k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6` — i.e. **Heinig's Conjecture 5 is confirmed for
k ≤ 6** (a small new fact, exact, reproducible).

Heinig's Conjecture 6 is *precisely* `max_x ψ(And_k, x) ≤ 1/25` in the campaign's language,
and it is **still open in 2026** — I found no paper proving it. (A web-search summary asserted
"Erdős' conjecture holds for every triangle-free graph G with δ(G) > 10n/29"; I could locate
**no source** for that claim and treat it as an unsupported synthesis. It would follow from
Jin + Chen–Jin–Koh + Heinig's Conjecture 6, which is exactly the open step.)

Heinig also spells out the resulting dichotomy, verbatim:

> "(1) The minimum degree of the graph G is at most (1/3)|G|. … (2) The minimum degree of the
> graph G is strictly larger than (1/3)|G|, the chromatic number of G is exactly four, and the
> graph G contains a Petersen graph with one edge contracted, a Wagner graph, and a Grötzsch
> graph as subgraphs."

### b.4 What is *not* known

- **`δ > 10n/29`**: not proved. Needs Heinig Conj. 6 (= `ψ(And_k) ≤ 1/25`).
- **`δ > n/3`**: not proved. Needs `ψ(And_k) ≤ 1/25` **and** `ψ(Vega) ≤ 1/25`.
- **`δ ≥ (1/3 + ε)n`**: reduces to a *finite* check by Łuczak's theorem (see (c)), but no
  finite check has been carried out in the literature.
- **`δ ≤ n/3`**: hopeless by structure alone — Hajnal's construction gives triangle-free
  graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number with `δ > (1/3 − ε)n`, so no bounded
  homomorphic target exists below `1/3`. This is the true wall, and it sits at `0.333…`,
  well above the campaign's `0.16` floor from fact 7.

## (c) Exact statements and provenance of the named theorems

**Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós (1974).** B. Andrásfai, P. Erdős, V. T. Sós, *On the connection between
chromatic number, maximal clique and minimal degree of a graph*, *Discrete Math.* **8** (1974)
205–218. General form: *Let G_n be a graph on n vertices with chromatic number r which
contains no complete graph on r vertices. Then G_n contains a vertex of degree not exceeding
n(3r − 7)/(3r − 4). The result is essentially best possible.* At `r = 3` this is
`2n/5`, i.e. as stated verbatim in arXiv:2408.05547 Theorem 1.2:

> **Theorem 1.2 ([1]).** *If G is a triangle-free graph on n vertices with δ(G) > 2n/5, then G
> is bipartite.*

Brandt–Thomassé attribute the triangle case to Andrásfai alone: B. Andrásfai,
*Graphentheoretische Extremalprobleme*, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. **15** (1964) 413–438.

**Häggkvist (1982).** R. Häggkvist, *Odd cycles of specified length in nonbipartite graphs*,
in *Graph theory (Cambridge 1981)*, North-Holland Math. Stud. **62**, 89–99, 1982.
Two results are attributed to this paper: (α) `δ(G) > ⌊3n/8⌋ ⇒ G → C5` (verbatim quote in
b.1); (β) the 10-regular triangle-free 4-chromatic graph on 29 vertices, built by weighting
the Grötzsch graph, refuting Erdős–Simonovits's `δ > n/3 ⇒ χ ≤ 3`.

**Jin (1995).** G. Jin, *Triangle-free four-chromatic graphs*, *Discrete Math.* **145** (1995)
151–170. `δ(G) > 10n/29 ⇒ χ(G) ≤ 3`; sharp by Häggkvist's 29-vertex graph. Brandt–Thomassé's
summary of the chromatic profile, verbatim:

> "• For c ∈ ]0, 1/3[, Hajnal proved χ_c = +∞ • For c ∈ ]1/3, 10/29[, the bound is χ_c = 4
> • For c ∈ ]10/29, 2/5], Jin proved χ_c = 3 • For c ∈ ]2/5, 1/2], Andrásfai, Erdős and Sós
> proved χ_c = 2"

**Chen–Jin–Koh (1997).** *Triangle-free graphs with large degree*, Combin. Probab. Comput. **6**
(1997) 381–396. Verbatim statement quoted in b.3(i). Simpler proof: S. Brandt, *On the
structure of dense triangle-free graphs*, Combin. Probab. Comput. **8** (1999) 237–245.

**Brandt–Thomassé (Vega graphs).** Verbatim statements in b.3(iii). Companion facts:
J. Pach, *Graphs whose every independent set has a common neighbour*, Discrete Math. **37**
(1981) 217–228 — verbatim as quoted by BT: *"**Theorem 2** The graphs Γ_i are just the
triangle-free, twin-free graphs, where every maximal stable set is the neighborhood of a
vertex."* Vega graphs were introduced in S. Brandt, T. Pisanski, *Another infinite sequence of
dense triangle-free graphs*, Electron. J. Combin. **5** (1998).

**Łuczak (2006).** T. Łuczak, *On the structure of triangle-free graphs of large minimum
degree*, **Combinatorica 26 (2006) 489–493**: *for every ε > 0 there exists L = L(ε) such that
every triangle-free graph on n vertices with minimum degree at least (1/3 + ε)n is homomorphic
to a triangle-free graph on at most L vertices.* Brandt–Thomassé's phrasing, verbatim:
*"Using the Regularity Lemma, Łuczak [14] recently proved that not only the chromatic number
is bounded, but there exists a finite graph G_ε such that every triangle-free graph with
minimum degree at least (1/3 + ε)n has a homomorphism into G_ε."*
Predecessor: C. Thomassen, *On the chromatic number of triangle-free graphs of large minimum
degree*, Combinatorica **22** (2002) 591–596.

**Liu–Shangguan–Skokan–Xu / Beyond chromatic threshold (2024).** As quoted verbatim in
arXiv:2408.05547: *"Liu, Shangguan, Skokan and Xu [4] proved that every maximal triangle-free
graph with δ₂(G) ≥ εn is a blow-up of some triangle-free graph with at most 2^{O((1/ε³)log(1/ε))}
vertices."* (arXiv:2403.17910, *Beyond chromatic threshold via the (p,q)-theorem, and a sharp
blow-up phenomenon*; SoCG 2024.) This is an explicit, single-exponential quantitative version
of Łuczak's theorem, and it is *stated for the minimum common degree*, which is the parameter
that a maximal triangle-free graph has for free.

## (d) Flag algebras: what BCL computed, exactness machinery, precedents

### d.1 Exactly what BCL computed (verbatim, arXiv:2103.14179 §2.1)

The objective is *not* a subgraph density; they encode **cuts** as flag inequalities. Setup,
verbatim:

> "Pick a copy of a labeled graph H on k vertices in G. This will partition the rest of V(G)
> into classes X₁, …, X_{2^k} based on the adjacencies to the fixed k vertices. Now we construct
> a bipartition of V(G) into sets A and B. For each class X_i fix p_i ∈ [0, 1] and for each
> vertex in X_i we put it to A with probability p_i and to put it to B otherwise, i.e., with
> probability (1 − p_i)."

> "We can include all cuts rooted on at most 4 vertices and C5.
> 1. |V(H)| ≤ 2 and p_i ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}, gives 10 cuts,
> 2. |V(H)| ≤ 3 and p_i ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}, gives 108 cuts,
> 3. |V(H)| = 4 and p_i ∈ {0, 1}, gives 953 cuts,
> 4. H = C5, and p_i ∈ {0, 1}, gives 125 cuts.
> However, for k ≥ 6, there are more possible inequalities than computers can reasonably
> handle."

Two hand-picked extra cut families, verbatim:

> "Norin and Ru Sun [10] observed that the Clebsch graph, see Figure 1, is particularly
> unfriendly when applying local cuts. We add cuts that are specially designed to cut the
> Clebsch graph. The root is a 4-cycle v₀v₁v₂v₃v₀ and two additional vertices v₄ and v₅ with
> edges v₄v₀ and v₁v₅."

> "Another inequality that made a big difference is an extension of (5). … we pick another K₂
> in the non-neighborhood and do the same partition once more. This results in rooting on
> K₂ ∪ K₂ ∪ K₂."

Their own diagnosis of the ceiling, verbatim (§2.3):

> "We believe Theorem 2 can be improved by adding more cuts to the calculation and possibly
> lead to the proof of Conjecture 1. Adding more cuts lead to marginal improvements so far. We
> are looking at other cuts as well but the time needed to perform the calculations grows
> quickly and it may take a while until a significant improvement is obtained."

**So: flag order ≈ 6–7 vertices; the "types" are the roots listed above (≤4 vertices, C5, the
6-vertex Clebsch cutter, K₂∪K₂∪K₂); the certificate form is a standard flag-algebra SDP
(PSD matrices per type + a linear combination of the ~1200 cut inequalities), computed
numerically, no exact rational certificate is published.**

### d.2 Why a flag-algebra bound of *this* shape is structurally not exact

Two distinct reasons, both documented:

1. **The relaxation is not `D2`.** Each "cut" family gives an upper bound on the *minimum*
   over cuts. A finite family `C` of locally-defined random cuts yields
   `D2(G) ≤ min_{c ∈ C} value(c)`, and the flag algebra bounds
   `max_G min_{c∈C} value(c)`, which is `≥ max_G D2(G)`. Exactness would require some finite
   family of cuts defined by bounded-size roots to be optimal on the worst graph. **Norin–Sun's
   Clebsch observation is the recorded witness that this fails**: on the Clebsch graph the
   locally-defined cuts are strictly worse than the global optimum. Razborov independently
   pins the same constant: in *More about sparse halves in triangle-free graphs* he writes
   verbatim *"The number 27/1024 here is not arbitrary, it reflects what can be achieved with
   a certain class of methods, and the Clebsch graph is an extremal example for the resulting
   extremal problem."* — i.e. **the Clebsch graph is the *exact* obstruction that saturates the
   local-cut method in the sibling problem.**
2. **No Positivstellensatz for graph algebras.** H. Hatami, S. Norin, *Undecidability of linear
   inequalities in graph homomorphism densities*, **J. Amer. Math. Soc. 24 (2011) 547–565**:
   there are valid linear inequalities in homomorphism densities that are **not** expressible
   as a finite sum of squares of labelled quantum graphs (answering Lovász's question and a
   question of Razborov negatively), and validity is undecidable. So a finite-level flag
   certificate is not guaranteed to exist even when the inequality is true. This is the
   general-theory reason a level of the hierarchy can stall strictly above the truth.

### d.3 Stability / exactness machinery — has it been applied here?

- **O. Pikhurko, J. Sliačan, K. Tyros**, *Strong forms of stability from flag algebra
  calculations*, **J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 135 (2019) 129–178**, arXiv:1706.02612. Provides
  a computer-checkable sufficient condition for "perfect B-stability": *there is a constant C
  such that every admissible graph G of order n ≥ C can be made into a blow-up of B by changing
  at most C(λ(n,G) − λ(G))·binom(n,2) adjacencies.* Applied by the authors to Turán-type
  problems for `K₄⁻`, `K_{3,2}`, `K_{2,2,1}`, `P₃`, `C₄`, `C₅`, the paw.
  **Not applied to the max-cut / bipartition problem.** Its hypothesis requires `λ` to depend
  *linearly on order-κ subgraph statistics*; `D2` is a min over cuts and is **not** of that
  form, so the machinery does not apply verbatim to `D2`. It would apply to a fixed cut family
  — but then it certifies stability for the *relaxation*, not for `D2`.
- **Razborov's tight-limit / exactness style** has been applied to the **sibling** problem:
  A. A. Razborov, *More about sparse halves in triangle-free graphs*, **Sbornik: Mathematics
  213 (2022) 109–128**, arXiv:2104.09406. Verbatim abstract: *"we establish the new bound
  (27/1024)n² on the number of edges in general case. We completely prove the conjecture for
  graphs of girth ≥ 5, for graphs with independence number ≥ 2n/5 and for strongly regular
  graphs. Each of these three classes includes both known (conjectured) extremal
  configurations, the 5-cycle and the Petersen graph."*
  **Note the sibling problem has TWO extremal configurations (C5 and Petersen).** Any
  stability/exactness argument for our problem must not silently assume a unique extremal
  family.

### d.4 The precedent: a flag-algebra bound *pushed to the exact conjectured constant*, with the
balanced C5 blow-up as the unique extremal example

**Yes, this exists, and it is the closest possible precedent.** The Erdős pentagon problem
("maximise the number of C5's in a triangle-free graph") was solved *exactly* by flag algebras,
twice independently:

- A. Grzesik, *On the maximum number of five-cycles in a triangle-free graph*, **J. Combin.
  Theory Ser. B 102 (2012) 1061–1066**;
- H. Hatami, J. Hladký, D. Kráľ, S. Norin, A. Razborov, *On the number of pentagons in
  triangle-free graphs*, **J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 120 (2013) 722–732**, arXiv:1102.1634.

Result: every triangle-free graph on `n` vertices has at most `(n/5)^5` copies of `C5`, with
**equality iff `5 | n` and `G` is the balanced blow-up of the pentagon**. The non-divisible
cases and the extremal characterisation were completed by B. Lidický, F. Pfender, *Pentagons
in triangle-free graphs*, arXiv:1712.08869 (European J. Combin.). Caution: a "sporadic
counterexample to a characterization of equality" for the 5-cycle problem is on record
(ResearchGate title *"Cycles of length 5 in triangle-free graphs: a sporadic counterexample to
a characterization of equality"*), so equality characterisations in this family have already
been wrong once.

**Why the precedent does not transfer for free:** the pentagon objective *is* a subgraph
density (linear in order-5 statistics), so it lives natively inside the flag algebra. `D2` is
a min over an exponential family of cuts and does not. This is the single structural
difference between the solved pentagon problem and the open bipartition problem, and it is
exactly the gap that BCL's "cut encoding" tries, and fails, to bridge.

## (e) Lower-bound constructions: anything beating or approaching `1/25`?

**No. I found no published construction with `bip/n² > 1/25`, and none other than C5
blow-ups attaining `1/25`.** Everything on record:

- **C5 blow-ups**: `bip(C5[a₁..a₅]) = min over the 16 cuts`; balanced gives `n²/25`. Uniquely
  conjectured extremal. BCL: *"This would be sharp as the balanced blow-up of C5 with class
  sizes n/5 needs at least n²/25 edges removed to be made bipartite."*
- **Andrásfai graphs `And_k`** (`n = 3k−1`): `bip = ⌊k²/4⌋ = ⌊(n+1)²/36⌋`, ratio `→ 1/36`;
  equality with `n²/25` only at `k = 2` (C5). Verified exactly here for `k ≤ 14` (ratio) and
  `k ≤ 6` (exhaustive max-cut). `1/36 = 0.0277… < 1/25`.
- **Wagner / Möbius ladder `V8 = And_3`**: `bip = 2`, ratio `1/32 = 0.03125`.
- **Vega graphs** (all four families, `i = 2..5`): computed here exactly.
  Largest `bip/n²` seen is `Υ_3 − {2i}`: `bip = 8`, `n = 15`, `8/225 = 0.035556`.
  All `< 1/25`. `ψ` at the Brandt–Thomassé regular weight vector ranges `0.0314 … 0.0345`,
  monotonically decreasing in `i`. **(Per §0.2 these are lower bounds only; the true
  `max_x ψ` is `≥ 1/25` for every one of them since they all contain an induced C5.)**
- **Strongly regular graphs** (campaign fact 8): Clebsch `8/256 = 0.03125`, Hoffman–Singleton
  `50/2500 = 0.02`, Gewirtz `84/3136 = 0.0268`, Higman–Sims `350/10000 = 0.035`.
- **Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp local-density family**: `D₂^b`-style analogues where the
  balanced C5 blow-up *is* extremal in one regime — BCL "10 problems" verbatim:
  *"The complete balanced bipartite graph achieves equality in the first case and the balanced
  C5-blowup achieves equality in the second case."*

**Conjectured extremal characterisation.** No paper states a proved characterisation. The
belief in the literature is "balanced C5 blow-up, uniquely". BCL "10 problems" Question 2.2
is the only place I found a hedged version, verbatim: *"What is the maximum of D₂^b(G) over
all n-vertex triangle-free regular graphs? The balanced C5-blow-up seems to be a reasonable
candidate for the extremal example when 10 divides n."*

**IMPORTANT WARNING (the one near-miss in this family).** In the *closely related* local
density problem the C5-blow-up guess is **false**, and the falsifier is a sporadic strongly
regular graph. BCL "10 problems" §1, verbatim:

> "Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [9] proved Conjecture 1.2 for α ≥ 0.647 and Krivelevich
> [16] for α ≥ 0.6, note that 17/30 ≈ 0.5666 and 53/120 ≈ 0.4416. **Brandt [4] disproved
> Conjecture 1.2 for α < 0.474. He [4] observed that the Higman-Sims graph is a counterexample
> in this range.** Yet, the conjecture is believed to be true for a wide range of α."

([4] = S. Brandt, *The local density of triangle-free graphs*, Discrete Math. **183** (1998)
17–25.) Likewise Razborov's sparse-half problem has **two** extremal configurations (C5 *and*
Petersen). Any campaign step that assumes "the extremal object is a C5 blow-up" is assuming
something that has already failed twice in the neighbourhood.

## (f) Odd-cycle edge transversal vs. edge packing

**The one directly relevant published theorem** is a packing/covering inequality, and it is
exact and unconditional:

S. Norin, Y. R. Sun, *Triangle-independent sets vs. cuts*, arXiv:1602.04370 (2016).
Verbatim:

> "A set of edges T in a graph G is triangle-independent if T contains at most one edge from
> each triangle in G. Let α₁(G) denote the maximum size of the triangle-independent set in G,
> and let τ_B(G) denote minimum size of a set F ⊆ E(G) such that G \ F is bipartite. We prove
> that α₁(G) + τ_B(G) ≤ |V(G)|²/4, verifying a conjecture due to Lehel, and independently
> Puleo, and a slightly weaker conjecture of Erdős, Gallai and Tuza. Further, we characterize
> the graphs which attain the equality."
>
> **Theorem 4.** *For every graph G, α₁(G) + τ_B(G) ≤ |V(G)|²/4. Moreover the equality holds
> if and only if G is a join of a collection of complete balanced bipartite graphs.*

**For triangle-free G, `α₁(G) = |E(G)|` trivially, so this is the exact statement
`|E(G)| + bip(G) ≤ n²/4`, valid for every triangle-free graph and every n.** Sanity check on
the extremal object: `C5[n]` has `|E| = N²/5` and `bip = N²/25`, so LHS `= 6N²/25 = 0.24N²`
`< 0.25N²` — not tight, gap `N²/100`. Combined with the trivial `bip ≤ m/2` and EFPS Thm 1
the max of `min{c/2, c − 4c², 1/4 − c}` over `c` is `1/16 = 0.0625` at `c = 1/8`, so
Norin–Sun does not by itself improve `n²/18`; it does give the conjecture for
`m ≥ 0.21 n²` (weaker than EGS's `0.2 n²`). **It appears not to have been used by this
campaign; it is a free exact global inequality.**

Related history in the same paper (verbatim): *"Puleo proved that (3) holds for triangle-free
graphs [7], and that α₁(G) + τ_B(G) ≤ 5n²/16 holds for every graph G on n vertices [8]. Even
more recently, Xu [12] improved this bound to α₁(G) + τ_B(G) ≤ 4403n²/15000."*
The Erdős–Gallai–Tuza conjecture is the weakening with `τ₁` (minimum triangle edge
transversal) in place of `τ_B`.

**Erdős–Pósa for odd cycles — negative for our purposes.**
- Odd cycles do **not** have the Erdős–Pósa property (vertex or edge version) in general.
- B. Reed, *Mangoes and blueberries*, **Combinatorica 19 (1999) 267–296**: odd cycles have the
  **half-integral** vertex Erdős–Pósa property (either `k` odd cycles pairwise meeting each
  vertex at most twice, or a vertex set of size `f(k)` whose removal leaves the graph
  bipartite).
- Planar edge version: S. Fiorini, N. Hardy, B. Reed, A. Vetta, *Approximate min-max relations
  for odd cycles in planar graphs*, Math. Programming **110** (2007) 71–91: `τ(G) ≤ 10 ν(G)`
  for planar graphs (later improved; also D. Král, H.-J. Voss, *Edge-disjoint odd cycles in
  planar graphs*, JCTB **90** (2004) 107–120, `τ ≤ 2ν`).
- K. Kawarabayashi, Y. Kobayashi, *Edge-disjoint odd cycles in 4-edge-connected graphs*, JCTB
  **119** (2016): the edge Erdős–Pósa property holds for odd cycles **under 4-edge-connectivity**.
- **None of these gives a `c·n²` bound**, because all `f(k)` are functions of `k` only and the
  planar/connectivity hypotheses are exactly the "convenient special class" the campaign's
  rules of evidence forbid. **Verdict: (f) yields exactly one usable object, Norin–Sun.**

Observation worth recording (elementary, not from the literature): in `C5[n]` the
edge-disjoint-odd-cycle packing number equals `bip` (`|E| = 5n²`, and `n²` edge-disjoint
`C5`s saturate the bound `bip ≥ ν_odd`), so the conjectured extremal object has a *perfect*
packing/covering duality for odd cycles. A `τ ≤ ν`-style theorem restricted to triangle-free
graphs would therefore be *at least as strong as the conjecture* on the extremal object —
see BLOCKED below.

## THE THREE RESULTS MOST LIKELY TO BE EXPLOITABLE BY THIS CAMPAIGN

### 1. Brandt–Thomassé Corollary 4.1 + Chen–Jin–Koh Theorem 1 — the `δ > n/3` band collapses to two explicit families

> **Corollary 4.1** *The twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with δ > 1/3 are the
> 3-colorable graphs Γ_i for i ≥ 1 and the 4-chromatic Vega graphs.*

**Why exploitable.** With campaign fact 2 this is an **exact reduction, not a
reformulation**: `bip(G) ≤ n²/25` for all triangle-free `G` with `δ(G) > n/3`
**⟺** `max_x ψ(H, x) ≤ 1/25` for `H ∈ {And_i : i ≥ 1} ∪ {Vega graphs}`. Both families are
two-parameter and completely explicit; I have written the Vega construction out in
`G11_verify_vega.py` and machine-verified every structural claim of Brandt–Thomassé
Theorem 3 on it. By fact 3 the target is **equality at `1/25`**, i.e. "the induced-C5 point is
the global maximum of `ψ` on `And_i` and on every Vega graph". This is Heinig's Conjecture 6
plus a Vega analogue, and it is the *only* place in the literature where the whole problem on
an explicit degree range is reduced to a named, finitely-described family. An unconditional
theorem on `δ > n/3` would shrink the campaign's open band from `(0.16, 0.375]` to
`(0.16, 0.3333…]`, and is precisely the deliverable the rules of evidence call valuable.
Risk: `And_i` and Vega graphs both blow up in size as `δ ↓ 1/3`, so the family is infinite and
a uniform-in-`i` argument is needed; the campaign's G1 already found the `And_i` side
numerically well-behaved, and my Vega table (`ψ_BT` decreasing from `0.0345` at `i = 2` to
`0.0314` at `i = 5`) suggests the same, but neither is a proof.

### 2. Wang–Yang–Zhao 2024, Theorem 1.4(ii) — an immediate, free enlargement of the settled region

> **Theorem 1.4(ii).** *If δ₂(G) > ⌊n/8⌋ and n ≥ 8, then G is homomorphic to C5.*

**Why exploitable.** Zero new mathematics is needed: `G → C5` gives `bip(G) ≤ n²/25` by
campaign fact 7. So the conjecture is **unconditionally true whenever the minimum common
degree exceeds `⌊n/8⌋`**, a region *strictly* larger than `δ > ⌊3n/8⌋` (their `G₂` witness:
a `C5` blow-up with parts `n/7, 2n/7, n/7, n/7, 2n/7` has `δ₂ = n/7 > n/8` but
`δ = 2n/7 < 3n/8`). Since a minimal counterexample is maximal triangle-free (fact 6),
`δ₂ ≥ 1` for free, and the campaign can now assume `δ₂(G) ≤ ⌊n/8⌋` — a *pair*-level structural
constraint that the campaign has never used, and which is much more restrictive than a
degree bound: it forces some non-adjacent pair with a tiny common neighbourhood, i.e. a
localised "thin" spot in the graph. Companion tool for free: their Lemma 1.5
(`maximal triangle-free, δ > (1/3 + α)n ⟹ δ₂ > 3αn`).

### 3. Łuczak (Combinatorica 26 (2006) 489–493) + Liu–Shangguan–Skokan–Xu (SoCG 2024) — the `δ ≥ (1/3+ε)n` band is a *finite* computation

> Łuczak: *for every ε > 0 there is L(ε) such that every triangle-free graph with
> δ ≥ (1/3 + ε)n is homomorphic to a triangle-free graph on at most L(ε) vertices.*
> LSSX (quantitative, common-degree form): *every maximal triangle-free graph with δ₂(G) ≥ εn
> is a blow-up of some triangle-free graph with at most `2^{O((1/ε³)log(1/ε))}` vertices.*

**Why exploitable.** Combined with fact 2 this converts the entire super-`n/3` regime into a
*decidable* statement: verify `max_x ψ(H, x) ≤ 1/25` for every triangle-free `H` on at most
`L` vertices. That is a finite (astronomically large, but finite) certificate problem of
exactly the type this campaign already builds machinery for, and — unlike every relaxation
tried so far — it is **not** a weakening: it is equivalent on that range. The practical route
is to use it as a *soundness backstop* for statement 1: any uniform-in-`i` argument for
`{And_i} ∪ {Vega}` automatically covers the whole `δ > n/3` band, and LSSX certifies that no
other target graph can appear.

**Honourable mention (cheap, unused): Norin–Sun Theorem 4**, giving the exact unconditional
inequality `|E(G)| + bip(G) ≤ n²/4` for every triangle-free `G`, with equality characterised:
the equality graphs are exactly the joins of collections of complete balanced bipartite
graphs, and a join of **two or more** of those contains a triangle, so among triangle-free
graphs equality holds **only** for `K_{n/2,n/2}` (where `bip = 0`). Hence for every
**non-bipartite** triangle-free `G` the inequality `|E(G)| + bip(G) < n²/4` is *strict*.

## BLOCKED — routes that terminate at a lemma at least as strong as the conjecture

- **"Prove the conjecture for graphs homomorphic to an Andrásfai graph."** Blocking lemma,
  verbatim (Heinig, arXiv:0907.3928, Conjecture 6): *"By considering several copies of the
  bipartification F_k, and then optimizing a system of quadratic inequalities, it is possible
  to prove that an arbitrary homomorphic preimage H of And_k can be made bipartite by deleting
  at most (1/25)|H|² edges."* This is `max_x ψ(And_k, x) ≤ 1/25`, open since 2009. It is NOT
  implied by Heinig's Theorem 3 (which bounds `bip(And_k)` itself, ratio `→ 1/36`).
- **"Push the BCL flag-algebra SDP to `1/25`."** Blocking lemma, verbatim (BCL, arXiv:2103.14179
  §2.3): *"Adding more cuts lead to marginal improvements so far. … the time needed to perform
  the calculations grows quickly and it may take a while until a significant improvement is
  obtained."* Structural blocker, verbatim (BCL §2.1): *"Norin and Ru Sun [10] observed that
  the Clebsch graph … is particularly unfriendly when applying local cuts."* and (Razborov,
  arXiv:2104.09406) *"The number 27/1024 here is not arbitrary, it reflects what can be
  achieved with a certain class of methods, and the Clebsch graph is an extremal example for
  the resulting extremal problem."*
- **"Import the Pikhurko–Sliačan–Tyros perfect-stability machinery."** Blocked by hypothesis:
  the method requires `λ(G)` to depend **linearly on the statistics of order-κ subgraphs**;
  `D2(G)` is a minimum over `2^{n−1}` cuts and is not of that form. Applying it to a fixed
  finite cut family certifies stability for the relaxation, whose optimum is `≥ n²/23.5`.
- **"Get `1/25` from an Erdős–Pósa / odd-cycle packing–covering theorem."** Blocking lemma:
  one would need `τ_odd-edge(G) ≤ c n²` with `c = 1/25` for triangle-free `G`, and on the
  extremal object `C5[n]` the packing number equals `bip` exactly, so any `τ ≤ f(ν)` statement
  strong enough must have `f(ν) = ν` there — i.e. it is at least as strong as the conjecture
  on the extremal object. All published Erdős–Pósa statements for odd cycles have `f` a
  function of `k` alone (Reed, half-integral) or require planarity / 4-edge-connectivity.
- **"Improve the constant via `m/2 + c·m^{4/5}`-type max-cut bounds (Poljak–Tuza,
  Alon–Bollobás–Krivelevich–Sudakov)."** Blocked by rules of evidence clause 3: these are
  `m/2 + o(m)` and give nothing at `m = Θ(n²)`.
