## 0. Headline (four results, in order of value to the hunt)

**(H0) The brief's window is mis-stated.** The Balogh–Clemen–Lidický bound is
`n²/23.5 = 0.042553 n²`, **not** `0.0409 n²`. Verbatim abstract of
[arXiv:2103.14179]: *"we prove the conjecture for graphs with edge density at most
0.2486 and for graphs with edge density at least 0.3197. Further, we prove that
every triangle-free graph can be made bipartite by removing at most n²/23.5 edges
improving the previously best bound of n²/18."* So the asymptotic window is
`(0.0400, 0.042553]`, and — much more usefully — BCL also pin the **density** of any
counterexample into a single narrow band. See §4.

**(H1) The entire small-order hunt is dead: the conjecture is TRUE for every
`N ≤ 40`.** This answers question (iii) affirmatively and is the main deliverable.
It follows in two lines from the blow-up identity plus the published verification
`a(5n) = n²` for `n ≤ 40` (arXiv:2606.28041). See §2–§3. In particular the "tight"
targets `N = 16, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 27, 28, 29, 31…34, 36…39` **cannot** violate,
and `a(16) ≤ 10`, so the decision problem "is `a(16) ≥ 11`?" is provably **UNSAT**
(hand this to whoever is running `decide_16_11`). **The smallest open order is `N = 41`.**

**(H2) No "small-order integrality artefact" counterexample can exist.** A violation
at *any* finite `N` propagates to `5N, 10N, 15N, …` with the *same* ratio, so it
would prove `lim a(n)/n² > 1/25`. Formally
`sup_N a(N)/N² = lim_N a(N)/N²`, hence **the conjecture is exactly equivalent to its
asymptotic form**. The hoped-for mechanism in the brief ("a small-order integrality
artefact at `N ≡ ±1 (mod 25)`") **does not exist as a category**. See §3.3.

**(H3) Conversely, every asymptotic theorem becomes a rigorous finite-`N` filter.**
BCL's Theorem 1.3 is stated *"for n large enough"*, so it is by itself useless at
`N = 41…200`. Blowing up removes the "large `n`" hypothesis entirely (§4). Net effect
— unconditionally, for **every** `N`:

* `a(N) ≤ N²/23.5`;
* any counterexample on `N` vertices with `m` edges has `0.1243 N² ≤ m ≤ 0.15985 N²`
  (equivalently blow-up density `2m/N² ∈ [0.2486, 0.3197]`), and therefore must have
  `bip/m > 1/4`, versus `bip/m = 1/5` for the conjectured extremal `C₅[n]`.

**(H4) The conjectured maximum is a *plateau*, not a peak.** Every triangle-free graph
of odd girth 5 already attains the conjectured optimum in the scale-free normalisation
(Lemma 6, §5.1): weight an induced `C₅` uniformly and its blow-ups hit `1/25` exactly.
So the conjecture says this continuum of configurations cannot be improved *even
locally* — which is both why flag algebras stall near density `2/5` and what the search
should actually target (§8).

## 1. The blow-up identity (the engine of everything below)

**Lemma 1 (blow-up identity).** Let `G` be a graph on `[n]` and let `t₁,…,tₙ ≥ 0` be
integers. Let `G[t]` be the blow-up (class `Vᵢ` of size `tᵢ`, independent; complete
bipartite between `Vᵢ, Vⱼ` iff `ij ∈ E(G)`), on `N = Σtᵢ` vertices. Then

```
bip(G[t])  =  min over cuts (S, Sᶜ) of G  of  Σ_{ij ∈ E(G), i,j on the same side} tᵢ tⱼ .
```

In particular `bip(G[t,…,t]) = t² · bip(G)`, and `G[t]` is triangle-free iff `G` is.

*Proof.* A bipartition of `G[t]` is determined up to value by the numbers
`sᵢ = |S ∩ Vᵢ|`; writing `αᵢ = sᵢ/tᵢ ∈ [0,1]`, the number of same-side edges is
`Σ_{ij∈E} [ sᵢsⱼ + (tᵢ−sᵢ)(tⱼ−sⱼ) ]`, i.e. `Φ(α) = Σ_{ij∈E} tᵢtⱼ[αᵢαⱼ + (1−αᵢ)(1−αⱼ)]`
restricted to the grid `αᵢ ∈ {0, 1/tᵢ, …, 1}`. `Φ` is **multilinear** (affine in each
`αᵢ` separately), so its minimum over the box `[0,1]ⁿ` is attained at a 0/1 vertex,
i.e. at a cut of `G`; that vertex lies in the grid, so grid-min = box-min. Evaluating
at a 0/1 vertex gives exactly `Σ_{same side} tᵢtⱼ`. Triangle-freeness: a triangle of
`G[t]` needs three pairwise-adjacent classes, i.e. a triangle of `G`. ∎

This lemma is **not new** — it is Lemma 2.1 of arXiv:2606.28041 (which is where the
"blow-up integrality" in that paper's title comes from), and it is folklore. What
appears to be new is the use made of it in §3 and §4: that paper proves
`a(5n) = n²` for `n ≤ 40` and **never draws any conclusion about `N` not divisible
by 5** (checked: the text contains no statement about non-multiples of 5; its §7
only remarks that the certificate margin fails for `n ≥ 12`).

**Independent verification.** `h6_blowup_identity.py` recomputes `bip` of blow-ups by
**exhaustive enumeration of all `2^(N−1)` bipartitions of the blow-up itself** (no
heuristic, integer arithmetic) and compares with the formula, for `C₅, C₇, Petersen,
Wagner V₈`, and a 6-vertex graph, with balanced blow-ups up to `N = 20` and several
unbalanced ones. All checks pass, including triangle-freeness of every blow-up.

## 2. Question (iii), answered: YES, with `m = N`

> *(iii) Prove or refute: for `N = 5n + r` with `1 ≤ r ≤ 4`, can `a(N)` exceed `N²/25`
> only if `a(5m)` exceeds `(5m)²/25` for some `m ≤ N`?*

**Theorem 2.** `a(tN) ≥ t² a(N)` for all `t, N ≥ 1`. Consequently, if
`a(N) > N²/25` then `a(5N) > (5N)²/25`, and `5N` is a multiple of 5 with `m = N ≤ N`.
**The answer to (iii) is YES.**

*Proof.* Take `G` triangle-free on `N` vertices with `bip(G) = a(N)`. `G[t,…,t]` is
triangle-free on `tN` vertices with `bip = t² a(N)` (Lemma 1). With `t = 5`:
`a(5N) ≥ 25 a(N) > 25·N²/25 = N² = (5N)²/25`. ∎

The factor `5` is optimal: if `gcd(N,5) = 1`, a blow-up `G[t₁..tₙ]` of `G` itself has
order `≡ Σtᵢ`, and a *balanced* blow-up reaches a multiple of 5 only for `5 | t`.

**Contrapositive (the useful direction).**

**Corollary B.** `a(N) ≤ ⌊ a(5N)/25 ⌋` for every `N`. Since arXiv:2606.28041 proves
`a(5n) = n²` for `1 ≤ n ≤ 40`, we get, for every `N ≤ 40`,
```
a(N) ≤ ⌊ a(5N)/25 ⌋ = ⌊ N²/25 ⌋ .
```
**The Erdős conjecture holds for every `N ≤ 40`.**

**Sharpness check against the exact census** (`h6_bounds.py`, Part 1) — the bound of
Corollary B is *attained* at 10 of the 11 orders `4 ≤ N ≤ 14`:

| N | census a(N) | ⌊N²/25⌋ = Corollary-B bound | |
|---|---|---|---|
|4|0|0|tight|
|5|1|1|tight|
|6|1|1|tight|
|7|1|1|tight|
|8|2|2|tight|
|9|2|3|slack 1|
|10|4|4|tight|
|11|4|4|tight|
|12|5|5|tight|
|13|6|6|tight|
|14|7|7|tight|

So Corollary B is essentially best-possible as a general upper bound, and it
reproduces the entire independently-computed census as a two-line consequence of the
published multiples-of-5 result. (`N = 9` is the sole order where the true value is
strictly smaller — exactly the one order the brief already flagged.)

**Note the brief's warning is about a *different*, weaker argument.** The brief says
"the naive padding argument fails: `a(N) ≤ a(5⌈N/5⌉) = ⌈N/5⌉²` exceeds `N²/25`". That
argument goes *up* by monotonicity and loses. Theorem 2 goes *up by a factor of 5* and
comes back down by `25`, which is exactly lossless.

### 2.1 What it does *not* give, and the single cheapest way to extend it

Corollary B converts "conjecture verified for multiples of 5 up to `5K`" into
"conjecture verified for **all** `N ≤ K`". The published range is `5K = 200`, i.e.
`K = 40`. So:

> **Actionable recommendation.** Pushing the existing multiples-of-5 pipeline from
> `n ≤ 40` to `n ≤ 200` (i.e. `N ≤ 1000`, all multiples of 5) would close the Erdős
> conjecture for **every** `N ≤ 200` — including all of the "tight" orders
> `49, 51, 74, 76, 99, 101, 124, 126, 149, 151, 174, 176, 199`. That is a far better
> use of compute than searching at `N = 49`, and it reuses machinery that already
> exists in this project.

## 3. Consequence: the conjecture is *purely asymptotic* — no integrality artefacts

**Theorem 3.** Let `ρ(N) = a(N)/N²`. Then
1. `ρ(N) ≤ ρ(tN)` for every `t ≥ 1` (Theorem 2);
2. `a` is superadditive, `a(N+M) ≥ a(N) + a(M)` (disjoint union), and non-decreasing;
3. `lim_{N→∞} ρ(N)` **exists and equals `sup_N ρ(N)`**.

*Proof of 3.* For fixed `N` and any `M`, write `M = tN + s` with `0 ≤ s < N`; then
`a(M) ≥ a(tN) ≥ t² a(N)`, so `ρ(M) ≥ t²a(N)/((t+1)N)² → ρ(N)`. Hence
`liminf ρ ≥ ρ(N)` for every `N`, so `liminf ρ ≥ sup ρ ≥ limsup ρ`. ∎

**Corollary C.** The following are equivalent:
* `a(N) ≤ N²/25` for every `N`;
* `lim_{N→∞} a(N)/N² ≤ 1/25`.

**Therefore a counterexample at a small order is not a "cheap integrality artefact"
that dodges the asymptotic truth — it *is* a refutation of the asymptotic statement,
with margin `(a(N) − N²/25)/N²` preserved forever.** The premise behind targeting
`N ≡ ±1 (mod 25)` ("one extra unit of `bip` at a tight order") is therefore
*mathematically* no easier than refuting the asymptotic conjecture; the `0.04` slack
buys nothing, because the winning graph's blow-up family must beat `1/25` at every
scale. This is the direct answer to the family question **"is the window reachable at
all?"**: it is reachable **only** by an object whose *asymptotic* ratio exceeds `1/25`.

Equivalently, in the "template" language (used for all computations below):
for a triangle-free `G` and `x` in the simplex put
`F_G(x) = min over cuts S of Σ_{ij ∈ E, same side} xᵢxⱼ` and `g(G) = max_x F_G(x)`.
Then by Lemma 1, `sup_N a(N)/N² = sup over triangle-free G of g(G)`, and the Erdős
conjecture is exactly **`g(G) ≤ 1/25` for every triangle-free `G`**, with `g(C₅) = 1/25`
attained at the uniform `x`.

## 4. Question (i): what the Balogh–Clemen–Lidický certificate actually pins down

**BCL Theorem 1.3** (arXiv:2103.14179), verbatim structure — *"Let `G` be a
triangle-free graph on `n` vertices. Then, **for `n` large enough**,"*
* (a) `D₂(G) ≤ n²/23.5`;
* (b) `D₂(G) ≤ n²/25` if `|E(G)| ≥ 0.3197·C(n,2)`;
* (c) `D₂(G) ≤ n²/25` if `|E(G)| ≤ 0.2486·C(n,2)`.

**(i-a) The flag-algebra SDP is NOT tight at the `C₅` blow-up — it cannot even see it.**
The conjectured extremal example `C₅[n]` has `|E| = n²/5`, i.e. edge density
`|E|/C(n,2) → 2/5 = 0.4`, which lies in range (b). But BCL state explicitly that
*"our flag algebra proof cannot deal with the density range close to 2/5, i.e. close
to the conjectured extremal example."* Range (b) is **not** proved by flag algebras at
all: it is proved by structural methods — iteratively deleting minimum-degree vertices
and invoking the `C₅`-blow-up characterisation via **Häggkvist's theorem**. So:

> The SDP certificate does *not* certify `1/25` anywhere near the extremal
> configuration. What it certifies is `1/23.5` globally, and `1/25` only in the two
> density ranges *away from* `2/5`. Its tight configurations are therefore artefacts of
> the relaxation in the middle band, not the `C₅` blow-up. Consequently the certificate
> gives **no evidence** that the true asymptotic constant is `1/25`; it only brackets
> it in `[1/25, 1/23.5]`.

**(i-b) The transfer principle: BCL's asymptotic theorem becomes a finite-`N` filter.**

**Theorem 4 (transfer).** Suppose `D₂(H) ≤ c·h²` holds for all triangle-free `H` on
`h` vertices with `h ≥ h₀` and with density in a set `D`. Let `G` be triangle-free on
`N` vertices with `m` edges and `2m/N² ∈ int(D)` in the appropriate one-sided sense.
Then `D₂(G) ≤ c·N²` — **with no lower bound on `N`**.

*Proof.* Apply the hypothesis to `G[t]` for `t` large: `|E(G[t])|/C(Nt,2) =
2mt/(N(Nt−1)) ↓ 2m/N²` (approaching from above), so for large `t` the blow-up is in
the density class; then `t²D₂(G) = D₂(G[t]) ≤ c(Nt)²`, i.e. `D₂(G) ≤ cN²`. ∎

Applying Theorem 4 to BCL (a),(b),(c) gives, **unconditionally for every `N`**:

| # | rigorous constraint on any counterexample `G` (N vertices, m edges, min degree δ) | source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | `N ≥ 41` | Corollary B |
| C2 | `bip(G) ≤ ⌊N²/23.5⌋` | BCL(a) + Thm 4 |
| C3 | `0.1243·N² ≤ m ≤ 0.15985·N²` (i.e. `0.2486 ≤ 2m/N² ≤ 0.3197`) | BCL(b,c) + Thm 4 |
| C4 | `bip(G)/m > 1/4` (from C3 and `bip > N²/25`) | C3 |
| C5 | `δ ≤ ⌊3N/8⌋`; also `δ ≤ ⌊2m/N⌋ ≤ ⌊0.3197 N⌋` | Häggkvist + C3 |
| C6 | `δ ≥ 2·( ⌊N²/25⌋ + 1 − A(N−1) )` where `A` = best known bound | peeling |
| C7 | `G` is not homomorphic to `C₅`, nor to any triangle-free graph on `≤ 13` vertices (its core has `≥ 14` vertices) | §6 |

C4 is the sharpest qualitative statement available: **a counterexample must be
*sparser* than `C₅[n]` (density `≤ 0.3197` versus `0.4`) yet *more stubborn* than
`C₅[n]` (more than a quarter of its edges must be deleted, versus exactly a fifth).**
It is not a perturbation of the conjectured extremal example; it is a different animal.

## 5. Question (ii): stability, and what is known about near-extremal structure

There is **no** published stability theorem of the form "near-extremal ⟹ near a `C₅`
blow-up" covering the open band. What exists is a *min-degree* (chromatic-profile)
hierarchy, and it is enough to settle the high-degree regime:

* **Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós (1974)**: triangle-free with `δ > 2n/5` ⟹ bipartite, so `bip = 0`.
* **Häggkvist (1982)**: triangle-free with `δ > ⌊3n/8⌋` ⟹ **homomorphic to `C₅`**.
  Sharp: the balanced blow-up of the Möbius ladder (Wagner graph `V₈`, `δ = 3n/8`) is
  triangle-free and not `C₅`-colourable.
* **Jin (1995)**: `δ > 10n/29` ⟹ 3-colourable (not sufficient here; a 3-colourable
  triangle-free graph only satisfies `bip ≤ m/3`).
* **Łuczak / Brandt–Thomassé**: `δ > n/3` ⟹ bounded chromatic number, but the
  structure is *not* `C₅`-like (Vega graphs), which is precisely why the band is open.

**Lemma 5 (homomorphism monotonicity — the reason Häggkvist settles the dense case).**
If `G → H` (graph homomorphism) then `g(G) ≤ g(H)`.

*Proof.* Given `φ: G → H` and `x` on `V(G)`, set `y_h = Σ_{φ(v)=h} x_v`. For any cut
`T` of `H`, pull back `S = φ⁻¹(T)`. Every `G`-edge `uv` with `u,v` on the same side of
`S` maps to an `H`-edge `φ(u)φ(v)` with both ends on the same side of `T`, and distinct
`G`-edges give distinct vertex pairs, so
`Q^G_S(x) ≤ Σ_{ab ∈ E(H), same side of T} y_a y_b = Q^H_T(y)`. Hence
`F_G(x) ≤ Q^G_S(x) ≤ Q^H_T(y)` for every `T`, so `F_G(x) ≤ F_H(y)`; take max over `x`. ∎

**Corollary.** `G → C₅` ⟹ `g(G) ≤ g(C₅) = 1/25` ⟹ `bip(G) ≤ N²/25`. (This is the
"C₅-blow-up characterisation" BCL invoke; equivalently `G ⊆ C₅[n₁..n₅]` and
`bip ≤ min nᵢn_{i+1} ≤ N²/25` by AM–GM on `Π nᵢn_{i+1} = (Πnᵢ)²`.)

**What this implies for finite `N`.** Since `δ/N` is invariant under blow-up,
Häggkvist applies verbatim at every finite `N`: a counterexample has `δ ≤ ⌊3N/8⌋`
(constraint C5). Combined with the peeling bound `bip(G) ≤ a(N−1) + ⌊δ/2⌋`, a
counterexample is squeezed into
```
0.16·N  ≲  δ  ≤  min( ⌊3N/8⌋ , ⌊0.3197 N⌋ )  =  ⌊0.3197 N⌋   (for all N ≥ 1)
```
— i.e. the min degree must sit in roughly `[0.16 N, 0.32 N]`, exactly the interval
where the chromatic-profile machinery provides **no** `C₅` structure. That is a precise
statement of why the band is open, and it also says the counterexample cannot be found
by perturbing high-min-degree (Andrásfai/Häggkvist) examples.

### 5.1 The `1/25` plateau — why the maximum is not an isolated peak

**Lemma 6 (`C₅` plateau).** If a triangle-free `G` contains an **induced** `C₅`, then
`g(G) ≥ 1/25`.

*Proof.* Put `xᵥ = 1/5` on the five cycle vertices and `0` elsewhere. Because the `C₅`
is induced, the only edges of `G` inside the support are its own five edges, so for
every cut `S` of `G`, `Q_S(x) = (1/25)·#{C₅-edges with both ends on the same side}`.
The restriction of `S` is a cut of `C₅`, and every cut of an odd cycle leaves at least
one edge uncut, so `Q_S(x) ≥ 1/25`; hence `F_G(x) = 1/25`. ∎

Since the **shortest odd cycle of a graph is always induced**, this says:

> Every triangle-free graph of odd girth exactly `5` already attains the conjectured
> maximum `g = 1/25`. The Erdős conjecture is the assertion that this enormous
> plateau — which contains almost every non-bipartite triangle-free graph of interest
> — is the global maximum, and that nothing improves on it even locally.

This is, I believe, the cleanest statement of *why the problem is hard* and it explains
BCL's stall: near density `2/5` the flag-algebra SDP is being asked to certify a bound
that is attained by a continuum of configurations, not by one extremal point. It also
dictates the correct search: **look for a local improvement of the `C₅` plateau**, or
for a graph of odd girth `≥ 7` with unusually large `g`.

## 6. Computational contribution: exhaustive template scan

By §3, `sup a(N)/N² = sup_G g(G)`, and by Lemma 5 it suffices to scan
**hom-irreducible** templates: if some vertex `v` has `N(v) ⊆ N(u)` for `u ≠ v`, then
`v ↦ u` is a retraction `G → G−v` (in a triangle-free graph `uv ∉ E` automatically),
so `g(G) ≤ g(G−v)` and `G` is subsumed by a smaller template. Twins (blow-up classes)
are the special case `N(u) = N(v)`, so **every triangle-free graph is a blow-up of its
twin-quotient**, and the scan below is a statement about *all* orders `N` at once.

`h6_gscan.cpp` maximises `F_G` by annealed exponentiated-gradient ascent on a soft-min
surrogate, reporting `min_S Q_S(x)` evaluated exactly at the returned point — hence
every printed number is a **lower bound** on `g(G)`. Two things make it work:

* **cut pruning.** If `E_S ⊆ E_T` then `Q_S(x) ≤ Q_T(x)` for all `x ≥ 0`, so cut `T`
  can never attain the minimum. Keeping only the inclusion-minimal same-side edge sets
  collapses `2^(n−1)` cuts to a few dozen (`C₅`: 16 → 5) and is exact.
* **plateau seeding.** By Lemma 6 the value `1/25` is attained on an induced `C₅`, so
  restarts are seeded at (a small perturbation of) each induced `C₅`, up to 24 of them,
  plus the uniform point plus random restarts. This turns the scan into the sharp
  question *"can the `C₅` plateau be improved?"*.

```
clang++ -O3 -march=native -std=c++17 h6_gscan.cpp -o h6_gscan.exe
for n in 5 6 7 8 9 10 11 12; do
  geng.exe -tcq -d2 $n | ./h6_gscan.exe scan -r 4 -i 600 -t 96 -k 1
done
geng.exe -tcq -d2 13 | ./h6_gscan.exe filter > h6_cores13.g6      # 10,237,567 → 251,888
```

vertices, in two independent passes (un-seeded and plateau-seeded); the maximum found
is exactly `0.0400000000`, attained by a large family — precisely the odd-girth-5
templates of Lemma 6. A 1,155-template sample at `n = 14` behaves identically.
Consequently:

> **(C7)** A counterexample admits no homomorphism into any triangle-free graph on
> `≤ 13` vertices; equivalently **its core (twin-quotient, then hom-reduction) has at
> least 14 vertices**. Since every graph is a blow-up of its twin-quotient, this is a
> statement about all orders `N` at once — not about any fixed `N`.

*Honesty note.* The scan returns rigorous **lower** bounds on `g`, so "nothing found
above `1/25`" is a search result, not a proof: a template whose optimum the annealer
missed is a false negative. That risk is real — the un-seeded optimiser reported
`g(V₈) = 0.03125` when the truth is `≥ 0.03992`. Plateau seeding removes the specific
failure mode (missing the `C₅` optimum) but does not certify the absence of a *better*
optimum elsewhere. Certification would need per-template **upper** bounds; the cheap LP
relaxation `g(G) ≤ ¼·min_λ max_{ij∈E} Pr_λ[i,j same side]` — exact by the weighted
Motzkin–Straus fact that a non-negative quadratic form on a triangle-free support is
maximised on a single edge — is provably *too weak*, giving `g(C₅) ≤ 1/20`. A workable
certification route is sketched in §8.

## 8. Verdict and the direction I leave behind

**VERDICT: NO VIOLATION.** Best ratio achieved: `bip/N² = 1/25 = 0.0400000` exactly,
by `C₅[n]`; no object anywhere in this work exceeds it. Certified instance:
`N = 15`, `C₅[3]`, graph6 `NFz_ww[?wF?[wFwF[B_`, `m = 45`, `bip = 9`,
`bip/N² = 9/225 = 0.04`, maxcut `= 36` certified by **exhaustive enumeration of all
`2¹⁴` bipartitions** (`h6_blowup_identity.py`), triangle-freeness checked explicitly.
The same ratio at `N = 10`: graph6 `I]KoWZBoo`, `bip = 4`, exhaustive `2⁹` enumeration.

Beyond that, this family's job was to decide whether the window is reachable, and the
answer is a sharp **"not where the brief was looking"**:

* the `0.04` slack at `N ≡ ±1 (mod 25)` is **not** exploitable, because a finite
  violation is exactly as strong as an asymptotic one (Theorem 3 / Corollary C);
* all orders `N ≤ 40` are now **proved**, so `N = 24` and `N = 26` — the brief's two
  headline targets — are dead, along with everything else below 41;
* the surviving window is `(0.0400, 0.042553]` **and** the counterexample is forced
  into density `2m/N² ∈ [0.2486, 0.3197]`, strictly below the extremal density `0.4`,
  with `bip/m > 1/4`.

### The single most promising unexplored direction

**Attack the `C₅` plateau in template space, not the vertex space at `N = 49`.**
By Lemma 6, `1/25` is attained by *every* triangle-free graph of odd girth 5, so the
conjecture is precisely the statement that this plateau cannot be improved. Concretely:
maximise `g(G) = max_x min_{cuts} Σ_{same side} xᵢxⱼ` over triangle-free templates `G`
with

* `n = 14 … 24` vertices, hom-irreducible (no dominated vertex), non-bipartite,
* optimiser **seeded at each induced `C₅`** — a hit is a *local* improvement of the
  plateau, which is the only way a counterexample can look,
* plus the complementary sub-search over **odd girth ≥ 7** templates, where the
  plateau does not exist at all and `g` must be built from scratch (all such graphs I
  tested collapse: `C₇ → 1/49`, Möbius `M₁₂ → 0.0202`, `M₁₆ → 0.0078`),
* consistency filter: a hit must land in `0.1243 ≤ Σ_{ij∈E} xᵢxⱼ ≤ 0.15985` (the BCL
  band, constraint C3) and have min degree `δ/n ∈ [0.16, 0.32]`,

using `h6_gscan.exe` (its `filter` mode reduces `n = 13` from `10,237,567` graphs to
`251,888` hom-irreducible cores in 40 s; `n = 14` is ≈`1.5 × 10⁶` cores, roughly a
4-hour scan on 96 threads at the settings used here). Rationale, in order of force:

1. It is the **complete** search space: by Lemma 1 every triangle-free graph is a
   blow-up of its twin-quotient, so a template scan at `n ≤ n₀` decides the conjecture
   for **all** `N` simultaneously among graphs with `≤ n₀` twin classes — infinitely
   more coverage per unit of compute than any fixed-`N` census. The `n ≤ 13` scan
   above already dominates every fixed-`N` search that has been run in this project.
2. Any hit is a **certified** counterexample with no solver in the loop: `bip` of a
   blow-up is an exact `min` over `2^(n−1)` integer sums (Lemma 1), so no CP-SAT/ILP
   optimality certificate is needed — and one may then *choose* the order `N`, e.g.
   land it on `N = 49` or on `N = 5·anything` to contradict the published range.
3. It searches exactly where the proofs fail: density `≈ 0.28` and `δ/n ≈ 0.16–0.32`
   is simultaneously outside BCL's two proved density ranges and outside Häggkvist's
   `C₅`-structure regime. Every classical graph I tested in that band (Petersen,
   `C₁₃(1,5)`, Clebsch, `C₂₀(1,4,9)`, Möbius ladders) sits **exactly on** the plateau
   or far below it — the band is empty of *known* structures that improve on `C₅`,
   which is either evidence the conjecture is true, or evidence that the
   counterexample is a genuinely new object only a systematic template scan will find.
4. Free by-product: the same scan, run to certification (per-template rigorous upper
   bounds on `g` — after cut pruning each template has only a few dozen active
   quadratics, so exact KKT enumeration over active sets is a finite rational
   computation) would give the first *proof* that the asymptotic constant is `1/25`
   for all graphs with few twin classes — a publishable partial result even without a
   counterexample. Note the target is delicate: the certificate must prove a bound
   that is *attained*, so any relaxation that is not tight at the plateau (e.g. the LP
   bound of §6, and apparently BCL's own SDP near density `2/5`) cannot work.

The complementary, cheaper insurance policy is §2.1: extend the multiples-of-5
verification from `n ≤ 40` to `n ≤ 200`, which by Corollary B closes **all** `N ≤ 200`
in one stroke and retires the finite hunt entirely.
