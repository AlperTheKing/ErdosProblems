## (a) blow-up identity — CONFIRMED  (`P4_a_blowup.py`)

Re-derived: for a blow-up `G = H[n]` there is always an optimal cut that respects the blobs
(each blob is independent, so a blob's contribution is linear and all its vertices are
interchangeable; move the whole blob to its better side — this weakly decreases the monochromatic
count and reduces the number of split blobs by one, so it terminates). Hence

```
        bip(H[n])  =  N² · ψ(H, n/N)      exactly,    N = Σ n_v .
```

Machine-checked over 115 random triangle-free `H` (3–6 vertices) with random blob sizes, comparing
brute force over **all** `2^{N−1}` cuts of the blow-up against `N²ψ(H,x)`:

* blow-up triangle-free — 115/115 (a triangle would need two vertices in one blob, impossible);
* optimum attained blob-respecting — 115/115;
* `bip(H[n]) = N²ψ(H,n/N)` — 115/115;
* `C5[q]`: `bip = q²` exactly for `q = 1..4`, i.e. `= N²/25`;
* reverse direction `ψ(G, uniform) = bip(G)/N²` — 40/40 random graphs.

Both directions of the equivalence therefore hold (rational `x` dense in the simplex, `ψ` continuous
as a min of finitely many quadratics).

**Convention forced.** The circle adjacency must be **strict**, `d(x,y) > 1/3`: with `d ≥ 1/3` the
points `0, 1/3, 2/3` are pairwise adjacent and the circle graph contains a triangle. Every
downstream object inherits this, and it is exactly what breaks the Fourier identity in (e).

## (b) `And(k) = Γ_{3k−1}` — CONFIRMED  (`P4_b_andrasfai.py`)

`And(k) = Cay(Z_{3k−1}, {1,4,…,3k−2})` (the standard definition) and
`Γ_{3k−1} = Cay(Z_{3k−1}, {k,…,2k−1})` (the circle graph). Since `3jk ≡ j (mod 3k−1)`,

```
        k·(3j+1) ≡ k + j (mod 3k−1),   j = 0..k−1        ⟹   k·S_And = S_Γ  exactly,
```

and `gcd(k, 3k−1) = 1`, so `v ↦ k·v` is an isomorphism. Verified elementwise and as a full
adjacency check for `k = 2..8`, plus an independent brute-force isomorphism search over all `m!`
relabelings for `k ≤ 4`; both graphs `k`-regular and triangle-free in every case. The source
(Brandt–Thomassé, Thm 1) defines `Γ_i` on `{1..3i−1}` with `j ~ j+i,…,j+2i−1` — literally the same
connection set, so the campaign's `Γ_{3k−1}` and the literature's `Γ_k` agree.

**Side observation worth recording:** `δ(Γ_m) > m/3` **iff** `m ≡ 2 (mod 3)`. Circle graphs with
`m ≢ 2 (mod 3)` (e.g. Γ₁₈, Γ₂₀·uniform, Γ₇ — several of the recorded witnesses) are legitimate
triangle-free test objects but are **not** Andrásfai graphs and do not lie in the class the
structure theorem produces.

## (d) `∫m dμ = W − ∫g²dμ` and `m(b) = value of the cut N(b)` — CONFIRMED  (`P4_cdf.py`)

`N(b)` is the open arc `(b+1/3, b+2/3)`, of length exactly `1/3`, hence independent (any two of its
points are at distance `< 1/3`), which is where triangle-freeness enters. So

```
        mono(N(b)) = W − e(N(b), N(b)^c) = W − ∫_{N(b)} g dμ = m(b),
        ∫ m dμ = W − ∫∫ 1[d(b,y)>1/3] g(y) dμ(y)dμ(b) = W − ∫ g² dμ   (since y ∈ N(b) ⟺ b ∈ N(y)).
```

Machine-checked exactly on 400 random measures: identity 400/400; `m(b)` computed three independent
ways (direct monochromatic sum over the cut, the `W − Σ_{N(b)} x g` formula, and the sweep) agree in
every case; `N(b)` independent in every case.

**The pairing hazard is real.** On Γ₁₁ with support `{0, 2/11, 4/11, 7/11, 9/11}` and weights
`(1,2,3,2,1)/9`, attaching to the `b`-th atom the arc that *starts* at the `b`-th atom instead of
`N(b)` gives a different value at 4 of the 5 atoms (e.g. `m(b) = 2/81` vs `1/27` at `b = 0`). Any
future implementation must construct `N(b)` from the distance predicate, never from an index offset.

## (e) the Fourier identity — coefficients right, four surrounding claims wrong  (`P4_e_fourier.py`, `P4_e2_threefold.py`)

**CONFIRMED:** `ψ̂(0) = 1/36` and
`ψ̂(n) = (−1)ⁿ[sin(πn/3)/(6πn) + (cos(πn/3)−1)/(2π²n²)]`, re-derived symbolically with sympy from
`2∫_{1/3}^{1/2}(1/2−t)cos(2πnt)dt` and matched for `n = 1..12`. Multiples of 6 vanish.

**REFUTED (round5 `R5K10_FOURIER.md`), four separate claims:**

1. *"ψ̂(n) = O(1/n²)"* — false. The `sin(πn/3)/(6πn)` term does not vanish unless `3 | n`, so
   `|ψ̂(n)| ~ √3/(12πn) = 0.0459/n`. Numerically `n·ψ̂(n) → ±0.0459` (n = 500: `+0.04579`;
   n = 3001: `−0.04594`), not `→ 0`.
2. *"the sum converges absolutely for every measure, atomic or not"* — false.
   `Σ_{n≤2·10⁶}|ψ̂(n)| = 0.4532` and grows like `0.0306·ln N`; and for an atomic measure `|μ̂(n)|²`
   does not decay (`μ = δ₀` has `|μ̂(n)| ≡ 1`). Convergence is only conditional (symmetric partial
   sums, Dirichlet–Jordan), which forbids rearrangement or tail truncation.
3. *"the only positive coefficients of any size are n = 3 and n = 5"*, and
   *"Σ_{ψ̂(n)>0} 2ψ̂(n) = 0.2277"* — false. `ψ̂(n) > 0` for **every** `n ≡ 2, 5 (mod 6)` and every odd
   multiple of 3; the positive part sums to `+∞` (`0.3688` by `n = 2·10⁵`). The quoted `0.2277` is a
   truncation, not a value. Consequence: **no bound on `A` can be obtained by keeping finitely many
   frequencies and discarding the rest.**
4. *The identity itself* — false for any measure carrying a positive mass of pairs at distance
   **exactly 1/3**. The kernel jumps there (`0 → 1/6`) and the series converges to the midpoint
   `1/12`; the exact defect is

   ```
        Σ_n ψ̂(n)|μ̂(n)|²  −  A  =  (1/12) · (μ⊗μ){ (x,y) : d(x,y) = 1/3 }   ≥ 0 .
   ```

   Exact falsifiers (each verified against the direct double sum):

   | measure | true `A` | spectral series | gap |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | uniform on Γ₁₈ *(a recorded round-5 witness)* | `1/54 = 0.018519` | `1/36 = 0.027778` | `1/108` |
   | uniform on Γ₉ | `0.012346` | `0.030864` | `0.018519` |
   | `(δ₀+δ_{1/3}+δ_{2/3})/3` | `0` | `1/18 = 0.055556` | `1/18` |
   | two atoms at distance exactly `1/3` | `0` | `0.041667` | `1/24` |

   The five tie-free measures the round-5 note tested (C5, C7, continuous uniform, the perturbed
   near-path, a random six-atom measure) — plus W1 and W8 here — all agree with the series to
   `3·10⁻⁷`, which is why the defect was never seen. **Direction:** the series always
   *over*-estimates `A`, so statements of
   the form "`A ≤ spectral expression`" survive; the equality does not, and neither does any
   argument that reads the true `A` off the spectrum.

**Also refuted, item 6's last sentence** (`P4_e2_threefold.py`): *"a purely 3-fold measure has `g`
constant and no adjacent pairs at all"*. For a `1/3`-rotation-invariant `μ`, `g(x) = 1/3 − μ({x})`
exactly (the three arcs have equal mass and miss only the three points `x, x+1/3, x+2/3`). So the
atomless 3-fold measure has `g ≡ 1/3` and `W = 1/6` — **plenty** of adjacent pairs — while only the

## (f) `bound_k` — CONFIRMED, and it is the proximate cause of the item-7 failure  (`P4_cdf.py`)

`bound_k` is a convex combination of the `m(b)` with weights `x_b g(b)^k ≥ 0`, hence

```
        ARCBOUND  ≤  min_b m(b)  ≤  bound_k        for every k ≥ 0 ,
```

so it is a valid upper bound on `ARCBOUND` (and on `ψ`) for every `k`. Verified on 600 random
measures for `k = 0..6`, no violations. Degenerate cases behave: with `0⁰ = 1`, `bound_0` is the
plain average; atoms with `g(b) = 0` simply drop out for `k ≥ 1` (harmless for an upper bound); if
**all** `g(b) = 0` the denominator vanishes and `bound_k` is undefined for `k ≥ 1`, but then `W = 0`
and `ARCBOUND = 0` anyway. `bound_0 = W − ∫g²dμ ≤ W − 4W²` by Jensen, and
`4W² − W + 1/25 = 4(W−1/20)(W−1/5)` — the quoted roots `1/20, 1/5` are right, as is
`A ≤ W/3 ≤ 1/25 ⟺ W ≤ 3/25`, so the open window `(0.12, 0.2)` is correctly derived.

The other listed bound, `A`, is also CONFIRMED as an *averaged* bound (`P4_f_halfarc.py`): by exact
integration over the rotation parameter (the cut is piecewise constant in `a`, breakpoints at the
atoms and their antipodes), `A = W − 2T` **equals** the mean of `mono([a, a+1/2))` over uniform `a`
— on all nine test measures and 200/200 random ones. Hence `ARCBOUND ≤ A`, as claimed.

**But** the same chain of inequalities is the refutation: `bound_k ≥ min_b m(b)`, so a measure whose
neighbourhood cuts are *all* worse than `1/25` defeats the entire hierarchy at once.

## 7. THE OPEN STEP — REFUTED  (`P4_search.py`, `P4_verify.py`, `P4_witness_W8.py`, `P4_slide.py`)

## (g) the Chen–Jin–Koh / Brandt–Thomassé reduction — class CONFIRMED, "reduction" UNSUPPORTED

Checked against the source, not the brief: Brandt & Thomassé, *Dense triangle-free graphs are
four-colorable: a solution to the Erdős–Simonovits problem*
(`perso.ens-lyon.fr/stephan.thomasse/liste/vega11.pdf`), pp. 3–4.

* **Thm 1** (their translation of Chen–Jin–Koh into weighted language): *a weighted **maximal
  triangle-free, twin-free** graph with **minimum degree δ > 1/3** is either isomorphic to some `Γ_i`
  (hence 3-colourable) or contains the Grötzsch graph as an induced subgraph (χ ≥ 4).*
* **Cor 4.1**: *the twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with `δ > 1/3` are the
  3-colourable `Γ_i`, `i ≥ 1`, and the 4-chromatic **Vega** graphs.*
* Their `Γ_i` is defined on `{1..3i−1}` with `j ~ j+i,…,j+2i−1` — **exactly** the campaign's circle
  graph (verified in (b)).
* Their weighted-graph dictionary is exactly the campaign's blow-up dictionary: a rational weighting
  corresponds to replacing `v` by `ω(v)n` twins, and *"the minimum degree of `H` is `cn` if and only
  if the minimum degree of `(G,ω)` is `c`"*.

So the **class** `{Andrásfai} ∪ {Vega}` in item 2 is correct. Three things the brief omits, all of
which matter:

1. **The two completion steps are fine and I verified their direction.** `ψ` is monotone under
   adding edges (`ψ(G,x) ≤ ψ(G',x)` for `G ⊆ G'`), so passing to a maximal triangle-free completion
   is safe; and `ψ` is exactly invariant under twin identification with weights (that is (a)). Both
   preserve `δ > N/3`. No error here.
2. **The Vega branch is untouched by items 3–7.** Vega graphs are 4-chromatic, hence *not* circle
   graphs, so no statement about measures on the circle says anything about them. The smallest Vega
   graph is the Grötzsch graph (their `Υ₂ − {y,4}`), which they weight with total 29 and degree 10
   (`δ = 10/29 > 1/3` — Häggkvist's example); I verified it is triangle-free, 4-chromatic, twin-free
   and maximal triangle-free (diameter 2). Its `max_x ψ` is **exactly `1/25`** (attained on its
   induced `C5`, `δ = 2/5`): the Vega branch is **tight**, not a formality.
3. **The hypothesis is on the weighting you are actually using.** `δ > 1/3` must hold for the `x`
   whose `ψ` you are bounding (equivalently for the graph induced on `supp x`). Nothing in the chain
   establishes that a `ψ`-maximiser can be taken that way. The free WLOG from vertex deletion is
   far weaker: deleting a vertex of weighted degree `d` costs `d`, and `(N−1)²/25 + d ≤ N²/25` needs
   `d ≤ (2N−1)/25 ≈ 0.08N`, i.e. one may only assume `δ > 2N/25`, not `δ > N/3`.
   **`C7` is the clean case the reduction cannot see:** it has no induced `C5`, its
   `max_x ψ = 4/225 > 0` is attained at min support degree `4/15 ≤ 1/3`, its best full-support
   weighting only reaches `δ = 2/7 < 1/3`, and every proper induced subgraph of `C7` is a forest
   (`ψ = 0`). So inside the `δ > 1/3` class `C7` contributes only `ψ = 0` while its true value is
   positive — the structure theorem is simply blind to it. (`P4_gh_scope.log`)

Verdict: the **structure theorem** is quoted correctly; calling it a *reduction of Erdős 23* is
**UNSUPPORTED**. It reduces the `δ > N/3` case, which is a special case, and even there it needs the
Vega branch.

## (h) circularity

No step literally assumes the conjecture. Four things do deserve to be flagged:

1. **"Reduces to"** in item 2 is wrong in the way described above: items 3–7, even if completed,
   would prove Erdős 23 only for weighted graphs with `δ > 1/3` (and only on the Andrásfai branch).
2. **The only visible route to "WLOG `δ > 1/3`" is circular.** Measured, not asserted: for all of
   Γ₅, Γ₈ (Wagner), Γ₁₀, Γ₁₁, Γ₁₃, Petersen and Grötzsch, `max_x ψ = 1/25` and the maximiser found is
   the **induced-`C5`** weighting, whose support-induced graph is `C5` with `δ = 2/5 > 1/3`. So the
   hypothesis is available — but *only* through the conjecture's own extremal configuration:
   the reason a maximiser has `δ > 1/3` is that it sits on a `C5`, which is what one is trying to
   prove. *Without* the conjecture there is no argument on file. The missing lemma is:
   "if `ψ(H,x) > 1/25` for some triangle-free `H`, then `ψ(H',x') > 1/25` for some `(H',x')` with
   `min_{supp} g > 1/3`". It should be stated explicitly as an open sub-goal.
3. **`sup ARCBOUND ≤ 1/25` (item 3) is strictly stronger than the conjecture on circle graphs**
   (because `ARCBOUND ≥ ψ`), so it cannot be justified by believing Erdős 23; adopting it is a
   deliberate strengthening and should be labelled as one.
4. **Prior art.** The *unweighted* Andrásfai case is already published: P. C. Heinig,
   *The Erdős bipartification conjecture is true in the special case of Andrásfai graphs*
   (arXiv:0907.3928, 2009) — `bip(And_k) = ⌊k²/4⌋ ≈ (1/36)n²`, and the paper explicitly does **not**
   cover blow-ups. So the campaign's target (the weighted/blow-up version, which is what reaches
   `1/25`) is genuinely stronger than the published result — no duplication, but the weighted
   qualifier must stay in every statement of the goal.
