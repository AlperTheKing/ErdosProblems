## 2. The refutation

### 2.1 Collapse of the hierarchy on flat measures (proof)

> **Proposition.** If `g` is constant (`= c`) on `supp mu`, then `c = 2W`,
> `m(b) = W - c^2 = W - 4W^2` for every `b in supp mu`, and therefore
> `bound_k = W - 4W^2` for every `k >= 0` — as does `min_b m(b)` and the harmonic mean
> `1/E[1/m]`.

*Proof.* `int g dmu = 2W` gives `c = 2W`.  `m(b) = W - int_{N(b)} g dmu = W - c mu(N(b))
= W - c g(b) = W - c^2`.  Any weighted average, minimum, or harmonic mean of a constant is that
constant. []

Since `W - 4W^2 > 1/25` exactly for `W in (1/20, 1/5)`, *every* flat measure with
`W in (1/20,1/5)` has the entire hierarchy pinned above `1/25`.  Item 7 can therefore only be
true if no flat measure satisfies its other two hypotheses.  It does.

### 2.2 The witness

```
mu = weight 1/8 at  {0, 1, 6, 7, 12, 13, 14, 19} / 20
```
The far-graph is 3-regular (`g == 3/8`): with the atoms labelled `v_0..v_7` in circular order,
`v_i ~ v_{i+3}, v_{i+4}` — the Wagner graph `V8`, drawn on the circle with the eight vertices
unequally spaced so that the 12 edges have lengths `7/20` (x7), `8/20` (x4), `9/20` (x1)
instead of the `3/8, 1/2` of the equally spaced `Gamma_8`.  That is the whole trick: the
unequal spacing pushes the edge lengths down towards `1/3`, which inflates `A`, while the
3-regularity keeps `Var(g) = 0`, which paralyses `bound_k`.

### 2.3 Quantitative version (why "more levels" cannot help)

`bound_k - bound_0 = Cov_mu(g^k, m)/E[g^k]` and `m = W - Kg`.  Writing `g = 2W + ghat` with

## 3. The real obstruction: the arc length cannot be fixed

Every bound in the chain fixes the arc length: `bound_k` and `B` use length `1/3`
(the neighbourhood arcs), `A` uses length `1/2` (and averaging over positions at any *fixed*
length `l` is worse — see below).  On the witness the optimal cut is the arc `[0, 7/20]`,
carrying `v_0..v_3`, with exactly one edge inside each side.  Its length is strictly between
`1/3` and `1/2`, and both endpoint families miss it.

For fixed length `l` and *uniform* position, the average cut value is
`V(l) = W(1-2l) + 2 int (l-d)_+ dmu_pair`, whose derivative is
`V'(l) = -2W + 2 mu_pair([1/3, l)) <= 0`.  So `V` is non-increasing on `[1/3,1/2]`:
*among all uniform-position averages the best is `l = 1/2`, i.e. exactly `A`.*  This closes
the "average at some other fixed length" idea permanently: it can never beat `A`, and `A` fails.
Hence any repaired certificate must let the arc length depend on the measure.

## 4. Proved: the sigma-family of arc-cut bounds (and its exact limit)

> **Theorem 1.** Let `sigma` be any non-atomic probability measure on `R/Z`.  For an edge
> `e = {x,y}` let `D_sigma(e) = min(sigma(I), sigma(I'))` where `I, I'` are the two arcs
> bounded by `x` and `y`.  Then
>
>   `ARCBOUND(mu) <= A_sigma(mu) := sum_{e} w_e (1 - 2 D_sigma(e))`,  `w_e = x_i x_j`.

*Proof.* Let `Q` be the `sigma`-quantile map (`sigma` non-atomic, so `sigma(Q([0,t])) = t`).
For `t` uniform in `[0,1)` let `I_t` be the arc from `Q(t)` to `Q(t+1/2)`: an arc cut.  A pair
`x,y` is separated by `I_t` iff exactly one of the two quantiles `t, t+1/2` falls in the
quantile window of one of the two arcs bounded by `x,y`; that window is an interval of length
`D = D_sigma(e) <= 1/2`, and for an antipodal pair of points the probability that exactly one
lands in an interval of length `D <= 1/2` is `2D`.  So `E_t[mono mass] = sum_e w_e (1-2D_sigma(e))`,
and the minimum over arc cuts is at most this average. []

* `sigma = Lebesgue` gives `D = d` and recovers `A = W - 2T` — item 5 of the chain.
* `sigma = mu` gives the **mass-coordinate bound** `Atilde = W - 2 sum_e w_e D_mu(e)`, where
  `D_mu(e)` is the `mu`-mass of the arc between the endpoints counting each endpoint atom half.
  On the item-7 witness `Atilde = 1/32 <= 1/25` — it closes it.  (`sigma = mu` is legitimate:
  it is the limit of the non-atomic `mu * Unif[0,eta]`.)
* For an atomic `mu` with atoms `p_0 < ... < p_{n-1}`, `A_sigma` depends on `sigma` only through
  the `n` gap masses, and `sigma = mu` is the gap vector `s_i = (x_i + x_{i+1})/2`.
  **Any rational gap vector is an exact certificate**, so a floating-point LP may be used to
  *search* while the acceptance stays exact.

> **Theorem 2 (why this is only a reformulation).** `inf_sigma A_sigma(mu) = ARCBOUND(mu)`.

*Proof.* `>=` is Theorem 1.  For `<=`, given an arc cut with endpoints `a,b` chosen in gaps of
`supp mu`, take `sigma_eps` = uniform of mass `1/2` on `[a,a+eps)` and `1/2` on `[b,b+eps)`
(smoothed to be non-atomic).  Every edge separated by that cut has one blob in each of its two
arcs, so `D = 1/2` and it contributes `0`; every unseparated edge has both blobs on one side, so
`D = 0` and it contributes `w_e`.  Hence `A_{sigma_eps} -> ` the value of that cut. []

So `A_sigma` is a *certificate device*, not a reduction: it converts "minimum over a
2-parameter family of arcs" into "exhibit one `sigma`", and the corresponding
`max_sigma sum_e w_e min(S_e, 1-S_e)` is a concave maximisation (an LP).  Empirically the LP
optimum equals `ARCBOUND` on all 13 test measures (`P1_sigma.py`), as Theorem 2 predicts.
**Do not spend a round trying to prove `inf_sigma A_sigma <= 1/25`; that statement is
literally the arc-cut conjecture.**  What is worth having is a *rule* `mu -> sigma(mu)`;
neither `sigma = Leb` nor `sigma = mu` is such a rule (`Leb` fails on the witness of §2,
`mu` fails on W7 where `Atilde = 0.046 > 1/25`).

## 5. Proved: the PENTAGON LEMMA

Call `mu` **pentagonal** if `supp(mu)` can be cut into five cyclically consecutive blocks
`B_1,...,B_5` (arcs of the circle, possibly empty) such that for each `i` the set
`B_i u B_{i+1}` contains no edge, i.e. all its points are pairwise at distance `<= 1/3`.
(Equivalently: the map "atom `->` index of its block" is a graph homomorphism from the far-graph
into the pentagram graph on `Z_5` — vertices `0..4`, edges the distance-2 pairs, which is `C5` —
and it respects the cyclic order of the circle.  So pentagonal = "cyclically `C5`-colourable".)
Put `q_i = mu(B_i)`.

> **Theorem 3 (pentagon lemma).** If `mu` is pentagonal then
>
>   `ARCBOUND(mu) <= min_i e(B_{i+2}, B_{i+4}) <= min_i q_{i+2} q_{i+4}
>                 <= (prod_i q_i)^{2/5} <= (5^{-5})^{2/5} = 1/25`,
>
> and `ARCBOUND(mu) = 1/25` forces `q_i = 1/5` for all `i` and every distance-2 pair of blocks
> to be completely joined, i.e. `mu` is a balanced blow-up of `C5`.

*Proof.*  `S_i := B_i u B_{i+1}` is an arc, hence an arc cut, with value
`e(S_i) + e(S_i^c)`.  `e(S_i) = 0` by hypothesis.  `S_i^c = B_{i+2} u B_{i+3} u B_{i+4}`, in
which the pairs `(B_{i+2},B_{i+3})` and `(B_{i+3},B_{i+4})` are consecutive and each block is
internally edge-free, so `e(S_i^c) = e(B_{i+2},B_{i+4}) <= q_{i+2} q_{i+4}`.  The five numbers
`q_{i+2}q_{i+4}` have product `(prod_i q_i)^2`, so their minimum is at most `(prod q_i)^{2/5}`,
and `prod q_i <= 5^{-5}` by AM-GM on `sum q_i = 1`.  Equality forces equality in AM-GM
(`q_i = 1/5`), in `min <= geometric mean` (all five products equal) and in
`e(B_a,B_b) <= q_a q_b` (complete joins). []

Remarks.

* The lemma is *sharp and exactly tight at the extremal object*: for `C5` (and each of its
  balanced blow-ups) it returns `1/25` and nothing smaller.
* It needs no hypothesis on `W`, on `rho`, or on `Var(g)`.  In particular it settles cases with
  `W > 1/5` (W7 has `W = 0.21`), which the `bound_0` argument cannot touch.
* Verified exactly on every test measure (`P1_pentagon.py`).  Where it applies it does not just
  give `<= 1/25`, it **equals** `ARCBOUND`:

```
W1 (1,1,2,2,1)/G8        pentagonal q=(1,1,2,2,1)/7      bound 1/49    = ARCBOUND
W1' on G11, W1'' on G16  same                             bound 1/49    = ARCBOUND
W2 = C5                  q=(1,1,1,1,1)/5                  bound 1/25    = ARCBOUND   <-- tight
W5 three-atom path       q=(1/3,1/3,0,0,1/3)              bound 0       = ARCBOUND
W6 Gamma_7 uniform       q=(1,1,2,1,2)/7                  bound 1/49    = ARCBOUND
W7 unequal five-atom     q=(1/5,1/10,3/10,1/10,3/10)      bound 1/100   = ARCBOUND
R1 Gamma_11 residual     q=(3/17,9/34,3/17,5/34,4/17)     bound 15/578  = ARCBOUND  (0.02595)
R2 Gamma_40 case         q=(4/27,11/54,2/9,2/9,11/54)     bound 8/243   = ARCBOUND  (0.03292)
NOT pentagonal: Gamma_18 uniform, Gamma_20 uniform, Wagner V8 (both spacings)
```

Note that R1 and R2 — the two cases the root agent recorded as the residual hard ones, the ones
where `A`, `bound_0` and even `bound_1` fail — are pentagonal, hence **now proved**.

### A structural handle for the non-pentagonal case (verified, not yet exploited)

Lift by the 3-fold cover `u = 3x`.  Writing `x = (j+u)/3` with `j in Z_3` (the "sheet", defined
relative to a cut point `a`) and `u in [0,1)`:

* `x ~ y` iff the sheets are consecutive (`k = j+1` with `v > u`, or `k = j-1` with `v < u`).
  Points on the same sheet are never adjacent — this *is* triangle-freeness.
* Each oriented edge advances `u` by `delta in (0,1)` and `x` by `(1+delta)/3`.  A closed walk
  of `k` edges therefore satisfies `sum delta_i = 3 * (winding in x) - k`.  For `k = 3` this has
  no solution in `(0,3)` — triangle-freeness again; for `k = 5` it forces `sum delta = 1` or `4`.
  `C5` is exactly the 5-cycle whose `u`-displacements sum to `1` (winds once).
* `A = W/3 - (2/3) sum_e w_e delta_e` with `delta_e = 3(d_e - 1/3)`, and the 1/3-arc cut at the
  window `I_j` equals **the mass of edges avoiding `I_j`**; the three windows split the edges
  into three classes, so `B = min over cut positions and classes of the class mass`.

This makes the two failed bounds transparent: `A` is small only when the `u`-displacements are
large, `B` is small only when one of the three edge classes is light, and `V8` is precisely a
configuration that balances both.
