## (a) The Vega definition, verbatim, with all parameters

Brandt–Thomassé, *Dense triangle-free graphs are four-colorable: A solution to the
Erdős–Simonovits problem*, page 4 (file `round2/brandt_thomasse_vega11.pdf`, p.4 of 19).
The definition is quoted verbatim (the paper attributes the construction to Brandt–Pisanski [5]):

> Let us now turn to 4-chromatic graphs. A class of good weighted graphs, the Vega graphs
> were introduced by Brandt and Pisanski [5]. The construction is as follows: For some integer
> i ≥ 2, start with a graph Γᵢ on vertex set {1,...,3i−1} and add an edge xy and an induced 6-cycle
> (a, v, c, u, b, w) such that x is joined to a, b, c and y is joined to u, v, w. The set of neighbors
> of a, u on the Γᵢ graph is {1,...,i}. The set of neighbors of b, v on the Γᵢ graph is {i+1,...,2i}.
> The set of neighbors of c, w on the Γᵢ graph is {2i+1,...,3i−1}. This is the sole Vega graph
> on 3i+7 vertices. We denote it by Υᵢ.
>
> There are two Vega graphs on 3i+6 vertices, obtained from Υᵢ by a simple vertex deletion. The
> first one is Υᵢ − {y}, the second Υᵢ − {2i} (the latter sequence does not occur in [5], perhaps
> due to the fact that for i = 2 both graphs are isomorphic). Finally, the last Vega graph, on
> 3i+5 vertices, is Υᵢ − {y, 2i}. Observe that the Vega graph Υ₂ − {y, 4} is exactly the Grötzsch
> graph. Moreover, every vertex in a Vega graph is a neighbor of x, a, b or c, in other words,
> N_x ∪ N_a ∪ N_b ∪ N_c is the whole vertex set, this makes the Vega graphs 4-colorable, and even
> 4-chromatic since they contain the Grötzsch graph.

and the base family Γᵢ, from page 3:

> let us denote by Γᵢ, for every integer i ≥ 1, the graph on vertex set {1, 2, ..., 3i−1} where the
> vertex j has neighbors j+i, ..., j+2i−1, these values taken modulo 3i−1.

So **Γᵢ = And(i)**, the Andrásfai graph on 3i−1 vertices — exactly the circle graph of the root
agent's chain, step 2. Γ₂ = C₅, Γ₃ = Wagner/Möbius–Kantor V₈.

The **regular weight functions** (Theorem 3, p.4, verbatim, integer form; normalise by the total):

> Υᵢ: Assign weight 1 to the vertices x, y, 1, 2i, weight 3i−3 to c, w, weight 3i−2 to u, v, a, b
> and weight 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−6, and the total weight is 27i−19.
> Υᵢ − {y}: Assign weight 1 to the vertices 1 and 2i, 2 to x, 3i−4 to w, 3i−3 to u, v, c, 3i−2
> to a, b and 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−7, and the total weight is 27i−22.
> Υᵢ − {2i}: Assign weight 1 to x, y, 2 to 1, i, 3i−3 to b, v, c, w, 3i−2 to u, a and 3 to all the
> other vertices. The degree is 9i−7, and the total weight is 27i−22.
> Υᵢ − {y, 2i}: Assign weight 2 to x, 1, i, weight 3i−4 to v, w, 3i−3 to u, b, c, 3i−2 to a and
> 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−8, and the total weight is 27i−25.

The relevant structure theorem is **Corollary 4.1** (p.4):

> The twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with δ > 1/3 are the 3-colorable graphs
> Γᵢ for i ≥ 1 and the 4-chromatic Vega graphs.

### Answer to (a): the family is INFINITE — one integer parameter i ≥ 2, four graphs per i.

| name | order n | edges | comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Υᵢ | 3i+7 | — | the "full" Vega graph |
| Υᵢ − {y} | 3i+6 | — | |
| Υᵢ − {2i} | 3i+6 | — | ≅ Υᵢ − {y} **only** when i = 2 |
| Υᵢ − {y, 2i} | 3i+5 | — | Υ₂ − {y,4} = Grötzsch graph |

so the list of Vega graphs up to isomorphism is
`{Υᵢ, Υᵢ−{y}, Υᵢ−{2i}, Υᵢ−{y,2i} : i ≥ 2}` — 4 graphs for every i ≥ 3, and 3 for i = 2
(computationally confirmed below: Υ₂−{y} ≅ Υ₂−{4}; for 3 ≤ i ≤ 8 they are non-isomorphic,
already distinguishable by edge count, e.g. i = 3 gives 37 vs 36 edges).

Two structural facts I extracted (needed for (d), stated in the paper's §4, p.16):

- **X = N(a) ∩ N(u) = {1,…,i}, Y = N(b) ∩ N(v) = {i+1,…,2i}, Z = N(c) ∩ N(w) = {2i+1,…,3i−1}
  form a proper 3-colouring of Γᵢ** — three consecutive arcs of the circle of lengths i, i, i−1.
- In Γᵢ the neighbourhood of vertex j is the cyclic interval {j+i,…,j+2i−1}, hence
  **X = N_{Γᵢ}(2i)** and **Y = N_{Γᵢ}(1)**, while **Z has only i−1 elements and is not a
  neighbourhood**. That is exactly why 2i is the distinguished deletable vertex: deleting it
  makes Y short too. (The paper: "the possibly deleted vertex 2i has the property that its set of
  neighbors in Γᵢ is precisely one of the sets X, Y, Z".)

- triangle-free: **yes**, all 28.
- maximal triangle-free (diameter ≤ 2): **yes**, all 28.
- twin-free: **yes**, all 28.
- 4-chromatic: **yes**, χ = 4 exactly, all 28.
- minimum weighted degree with the paper's weights: **regular** (min = max degree) in every case,
  and equal to the paper's closed forms 9i−6 / 9i−7 / 9i−7 / 9i−8 over totals
  27i−19 / 27i−22 / 27i−22 / 27i−25, so δ = (9i−6)/(27i−19) etc., all **> 1/3** (the numerator
  times 3 exceeds the total by exactly 1 in every one of the four cases). δ ↓ 1/3 as i → ∞ and the
  largest is δ(Grötzsch) = 10/29 (Häggkvist's bound), attained at i = 2 by Υ₂−{y,4}.
- **odd girth 5** for all 28, and every one contains an **induced C₅**.

`P3_vega.g6` holds the graph6 strings (vertex order: Γᵢ-vertices 1,2,…,3i−1 in increasing order,
then x, y, a, b, c, u, v, w, omitting the deleted ones), one per line, tab-separated from a name
and the exact vertex order used.

**Immediate consequence for the conjecture.** By the induced-subgraph monotonicity of ψ proved
earlier in this campaign (max_x ψ(G) ≥ max_x ψ(H) whenever H is an *induced* subgraph of G,
because a cut of G restricts to a cut of H and the monochromatic mass inside an induced H is
exactly an H-monochromatic mass), every Vega graph has **max_x ψ ≥ 1/25**, attained by the
uniform 1/5 weighting on any induced C₅. So on the Vega side the conjecture is *equivalent* to
"max_x ψ ≤ 1/25", i.e. to the statement that the plateau is never exceeded.
