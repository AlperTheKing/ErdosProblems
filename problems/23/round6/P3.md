## (a) The Vega definition, verbatim, with all parameters

Brandt–Thomassé, *Dense triangle-free graphs are four-colorable: A solution to the
Erdős–Simonovits problem*, **page 4** (file `round2/brandt_thomasse_vega11.pdf`).  Quoted verbatim;
the construction is attributed to Brandt–Pisanski [5].

> Let us now turn to 4-chromatic graphs. A class of good weighted graphs, the Vega graphs
> were introduced by Brandt and Pisanski [5]. The construction is as follows: For some integer
> i ≥ 2, start with a graph Γᵢ on vertex set {1,...,3i−1} and add an edge xy and an induced 6-cycle
> (a, v, c, u, b, w) such that x is joined to a, b, c and y is joined to u, v, w. The set of neighbors
> of a, u on the Γᵢ graph is {1,...,i}. The set of neighbors of b, v on the Γᵢ graph is {i+1,...,2i}.
> The set of neighbors of c, w on the Γᵢ graph is {2i+1,...,3i−1}. This is the sole Vega graph
> on 3i+7 vertices. We denote it by Υᵢ.
>
> There are two Vega graphs on 3i+6 vertices, obtained from Υᵢ by a simple vertex deletion. The
> first one is Υᵢ − {y}, the second Υᵢ − {2i} (the latter sequence does not occur in [5], perhaps
> due to the fact that for i = 2 both graphs are isomorphic). Finally, the last Vega graph, on
> 3i+5 vertices, is Υᵢ − {y, 2i}. Observe that the Vega graph Υ₂ − {y, 4} is exactly the Grötzsch
> graph. Moreover, every vertex in a Vega graph is a neighbor of x, a, b or c, in other words,
> N_x ∪ N_a ∪ N_b ∪ N_c is the whole vertex set, this makes the Vega graphs 4-colorable, and even
> 4-chromatic since they contain the Grötzsch graph.

Base family, page 3, verbatim:

> let us denote by Γᵢ, for every integer i ≥ 1, the graph on vertex set {1, 2, ..., 3i−1} where the
> vertex j has neighbors j+i, ..., j+2i−1, these values taken modulo 3i−1.

so **Γᵢ = And(i)** = the circle graph Γ_{3i−1} of the root chain's step 2 (j ~ k iff the circular
distance of j, k in ℤ_{3i−1} lies in {i,…,2i−1}, i.e. iff normalised circular distance > 1/3).
Γ₁ = K₂, Γ₂ = C₅, Γ₃ = Wagner V₈.

Regular weight functions, Theorem 3, page 4, verbatim (integer form; normalise by the total):

> Υᵢ: Assign weight 1 to the vertices x, y, 1, 2i, weight 3i−3 to c, w, weight 3i−2 to u, v, a, b
> and weight 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−6, and the total weight is 27i−19.
> Υᵢ − {y}: Assign weight 1 to the vertices 1 and 2i, 2 to x, 3i−4 to w, 3i−3 to u, v, c, 3i−2
> to a, b and 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−7, and the total weight is 27i−22.
> Υᵢ − {2i}: Assign weight 1 to x, y, 2 to 1, i, 3i−3 to b, v, c, w, 3i−2 to u, a and 3 to all the
> other vertices. The degree is 9i−7, and the total weight is 27i−22.
> Υᵢ − {y, 2i}: Assign weight 2 to x, 1, i, weight 3i−4 to v, w, 3i−3 to u, b, c, 3i−2 to a and
> 3 to all the other vertices. The degree is 9i−8, and the total weight is 27i−25.

The structure theorem, **Corollary 4.1**, page 4, verbatim:

> The twin-free, maximal triangle-free, weighted graphs with δ > 1/3 are the 3-colorable graphs
> Γᵢ for i ≥ 1 and the 4-chromatic Vega graphs.

### Answer: the family is **INFINITE** — one integer parameter i ≥ 2, four graphs for each i.

`{ Υᵢ, Υᵢ−{y}, Υᵢ−{2i}, Υᵢ−{y,2i} : i ≥ 2 }`, of orders 3i+7, 3i+6, 3i+6, 3i+5.
Four pairwise non-isomorphic graphs for every i ≥ 3, three for i = 2 (Υ₂−{y} ≅ Υ₂−{4}).
There is no second parameter and no sporadic member.  Υ₂−{y,4} = Grötzsch.

Two structural facts I extracted, needed below (they are the paper's §4, p.16, made explicit):

* **X = N(a)∩N(u) = {1,…,i}, Y = N(b)∩N(v) = {i+1,…,2i}, Z = N(c)∩N(w) = {2i+1,…,3i−1}
  is a proper 3-colouring of Γᵢ** by three *consecutive arcs* of the circle, of lengths i, i, i−1.
* In Γᵢ the neighbourhood of j is the cyclic interval {j+i,…,j+2i−1}, hence **X = N_{Γᵢ}(2i)**,
  **Y = N_{Γᵢ}(1)**, and **Z (length i−1) is not a neighbourhood**.  That is exactly why 2i is the
  distinguished deletable vertex, matching the paper: *"the possibly deleted vertex 2i has the
  property that its set of neighbors in Γᵢ is precisely one of the sets X, Y, Z"*.
* Consequently a, u sit at circle position 2i; b, v at position 1; c, w at the **half-integer**
  position i+½ (their Γᵢ-neighbourhood Z is exactly the far-arc of i+½).  The circle rule
  reproduces all Γᵢ-attachments of the six, but **not** their mutual adjacencies: the circle rule
  would give K_{{a,u},{b,v}} and no edge to {c,w}, whereas the Vega graph puts the induced
  6-cycle a–v–c–u–b–w–a there.  The 6-cycle is invariant under the position-preserving involution
  a↔u, b↔v, c↔w, x↔y (which is an automorphism of Υᵢ).

## (d) The Vega analogue of the arc-cut mechanism: **ARCPLUS**

### The skeleton

A Vega graph is `Γᵢ (possibly minus the point 2i)` — the circle of the root chain — plus at most
**eight** further vertices x, y, a, b, c, u, v, w, and *that number 8 does not grow with i*.
So the natural analogue of "cut whose side is a cyclic interval" is:

> **ARCPLUS(H) = { A ∪ T : A a cyclic interval of the circle ℤ_{3i−1} (intersected with V(H)),
>                          T ⊆ {x, y, a, b, c, u, v, w} ∩ V(H) }.**

Size: `((3i−1)(3i−2)+2) · 2^{|specials|}` ≤ 508 · 256 at i = 8 — i.e. **O(n²) with an absolute
constant 2⁸**, versus 2ⁿ for the full cut set.  ARCPLUS contains every **neighbourhood cut**:
N(j) = (an arc) ∪ (the two specials of j's colour class), N(a) = X ∪ {v,w,x}, N(x) = ∅ ∪ {y,a,b,c},
etc., so it is a genuine enlargement of the m(b)-family and a genuine restriction of the full family.

## The sharpening: the reduction only needs ψ on the δ > 1/3 **polytope**, and there the margin is 13.8 %

Unwinding Brandt–Thomassé for Erdős 23 gives something strictly stronger than "max over the whole
simplex".  Let G be triangle-free with δ(G) > n/3.  Then

1. `bip(G)/n² = ψ(G, uniform)`;
2. G is a spanning subgraph of a maximal triangle-free G′ on the same vertex set with δ(G′) ≥ δ(G);
   adding edges only enlarges every mono_S, so `ψ(G, uniform) ≤ ψ(G′, uniform)`;
3. contracting the twin classes of G′ gives a twin-free maximal triangle-free weighted (H, ω) with
   `ψ(G′, uniform) = ψ(H, ω)` (the objective is multilinear in the split fractions of a twin class,
   so the minimum is at an extreme point where each class is undivided) and `δ(H, ω) > 1/3`;
4. by Corollary 4.1, H = Γᵢ or a Vega graph.

So the δ > n/3 case of Erdős 23 is **exactly**

> `ψ(H, ω) ≤ 1/25` for every H ∈ {Γᵢ} ∪ {Vega} and every ω in
> `P(H) = { ω ≥ 0 : Σω = 1, ω(N(v)) > 1/3 for all v }`

— *not* for all ω.  `P3_delta.cpp` computes `max ψ` over the rational points of P(H) of denominator
q, exactly (exhaustive, Aut-reduced, with a min-degree DFS prune, exact bip over all support cuts):
