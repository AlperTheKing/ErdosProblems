Triangle-free Graphs with Large Minimum Common Degree
Jian Wang∗
Weihua Yang†
Fan Zhao‡
Abstract
Let G be a graph. For x ∈V (G), let N(x) = {y ∈V (G): xy ∈E(G)}. The
minimum common degree of G, denoted by δ2(G), is defined as the minimum of |N(x)∩
N(y)| over all non-edges xy of G. In 1982, H¨aggkvist showed that every triangle-free
graph with minimum degree greater than ⌊3n
8 ⌋is homomorphic to a cycle of length 5.
In this paper, we prove that every triangle-free graph with minimum common degree
greater than ⌊n
8 ⌋is homomorphic to a cycle of length 5, which implies H¨aggkvist’s
result. The balanced blow-up of the M¨obius ladder graph shows that it is best possible.
Keywords: triangle-free; minimum common degree; the blow-up of C5.

Let G, H be graphs. We say that G is homomorphic to H if there is a map σ from
V (G) to V (H) such that σ(u)σ(v) ∈E(H) for every uv ∈E(G). Note that if G is a
subgraph of an H-blow-up then G is homomorphic to H.
In 1982, H¨aggkvist [2] extended the Andr´asfai-Erd˝os-S´os Theorem to the following
form.
Theorem 1.3 ([2]). If G is a triangle-free graph on n vertices with δ(G) > ⌊3n
8 ⌋, then G
is homomorphic to C5.
Define the M¨obius ladder HM to be the graph obtained from a cycle of length 8 by
adding 4 chords joining vertices of distance 4 on the cycle. A balanced HM-blow-up on n
vertices shows that Theorem 1.3 is sharp (as shown in Figure 1 (b)).
The minimum common degree of G, denoted by δ2(G), is defined as the minimum
of |N(x) ∩N(y)| over all non-edges xy of G. We say that G is maximal triangle-free if
G is triangle-free and any addition of an extra edge to G creates a triangle. Recently,
Liu, Shangguan, Skokan and Xu [4] proved that every maximal triangle-free graph with
δ2(G) ≥εn is a blow-up of some triangle-free graph with at most 2O( 3
ε log 1
ε ) vertices.
Motivated by H¨aggkvist’s result and Liu, Shangguan, Skokan and Xu’s result, we
consider triangle-free graphs with large minimum common degree. Our main result is the
following.
Theorem 1.4. Let G be a triangle-free graph on n vertices. Then (i) and (ii) hold.
(i) If δ2(G) > ⌊n
5 ⌋and n ≥5, then G is bipartite.
(ii) If δ2(G) > ⌊n
8 ⌋and n ≥8, then G is homomorphic to C5.

Some useful lemmas
In this section, we prove some lemmas that are needed in the proofs.
Lemma 2.1. If G is a graph on n vertices with δ2(G) ≥1, then either G is bipartite or
the shortest odd cycle of G has length 3 or 5.
Proof. Suppose that G is non-bipartite. Let C = v1v2 . . . vℓv1 be a shortest odd cycle of
G. Note that C is an induced cycle. If ℓ≥7, then N(v1)∩N(v4) ̸= ∅. It follows that there
is some v ∈N(v1) ∩N(v4). Since C is chord-free, v ∈V (G) \ V (C). Then vv1v2v3v4v is a
C5, contradicting the minimality of ℓ. Thus the lemma is proven.

Proof of Theorem 1.6. Let G be a C5-free graph on n vertices with δ2(G) ≥3. By
Lemma 2.1, either G is bipartite or contains a triangle. In the former case, there is nothing
to prove. Thus we may assume that G contains a triangle v1v2v3v1.
For any vertex x ∈V (G)\{v1, v2, v3}, we claim |N(x)∩{v1, v2, v3}| ≥2. Otherwise, by
symmetry assume that xv1, xv2 /∈E(G). Then δ2(G) ≥3 implies |N(x) ∩N(v1)| ≥3 and
|N(x) ∩N(v2)| ≥3. It follows that there exist distinct vertices y, z ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3, x}
such that y ∈N(x) ∩N(v1) and z ∈N(x) ∩N(v2).
Then xyv1v2zx forms a C5, a
contradiction. Thus |N(x) ∩{v1, v2, v3}| ≥2 for all x ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3}.
Now we distinguish two cases.
Case 1.
There exists x ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3} such that |N(x) ∩{v1, v2, v3}| = 2.
Without loss of generality, assume xv1, xv2 ∈E(G). Then δ2(G) ≥3 implies |N(x) ∩
N(v3)| ≥3. Note that v1, v2 are in N(x) ∩N(v3). Since |N(x) ∩N(v3)| ≥3, there exists
u ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3, x} such that u ∈N(x) ∩N(v3). Therefore v1v2v3uxv1 is a C5, a
contradiction.
Case 2. |N(x) ∩{v1, v2, v3}| = 3 for all x ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3}. Since n ≥5, there
exists two vertices x, y ∈V (G) \ {v1, v2, v3} such that x, y are both connected to v1, v2, v3.
Therefore v1xv2yv3v1 is a C5, a contradiction.
Thus we conclude that G is bipartite.
□
Remark. If G is a C5-free graph on 3 vertices and δ2(G) ≥3, then G is a triangle. If G
is a C5-free graph on 4 vertices and δ2(G) ≥3, then G is a K4.
