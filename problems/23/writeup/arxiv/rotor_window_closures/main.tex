\begin{abstract}
Fix a maximum cut of a triangle-free graph, write \(B\) for its
crossing edges and \(M\) for its monochromatic edges, and associate to
each \(xy\in M\) with \(d_B(x,y)=4\) the crossing edges on its shortest
geodesics. The companion note \cite{Ferudun26supports} showed that the
resulting supports can violate Hall's condition by an unbounded factor
and classified the minimal violating footprints with at most ten atoms;
the unique nine-atom footprint found there is the smallest object
studied here. This paper treats the dynamic side of the same system.
We prove an abstract circuit identity for inclusion-minimal Hall
violations (support union one edge smaller than the atom set, with a
perfect system of distinct representatives after deleting any atom), a
star lemma at cut-tight vertices whose arithmetic core is
kernel-checked in Lean~4, and an eight-vertex example, the neutral
square rotor, on which geodesic-collision repair provably never
terminates even though Hall expansion holds with room to spare. We then
close the two smallest rotor windows of the associated transport
strategy: nine-atom support circuits carry no balanced rotor (two
independent proofs, with kernel-checked finite cores), and at sixteen
atoms no covered two-owner window exists (an exhaustive, dually
verified census of \(153{,}978\) support graphs). At twenty-five atoms
the results are partial: support orders \(15\) and \(16\) are excluded
by machine certificates, the admissible order window is
\(15\)--\(21\), and two eighteen-vertex circuits that pass every local
test are excluded only by explicit certificates; orders \(17\) and
\(19\)--\(21\) remain open. All computer-assisted claims carry named
verifiers and SHA-256 hashes. These results constrain one local proof
strategy; they yield no upper bound on \(\bip(G)\) and do not resolve
the Erd\H{o}s \(n^2/25\) conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a finite graph \(G\), let \(\bip(G)=|E(G)|-\mc(G)\) be its
edge-deletion distance to bipartiteness. Erd\H{o}s, Faudree, Pach and
Spencer \cite{EFPS88} asked whether every triangle-free \(N\)-vertex
graph satisfies \(\bip(G)\le N^2/25\); the balanced blow-up of \(C_5\)
shows that the constant would be sharp. See \cite{EGS92,BCL21} for the
strongest general bounds, and \cite{Ferudun26} for an exact certificate
of the conjectured value at \(N=5n\), \(1\le n\le40\). Nothing in the
present paper improves any of these bounds.

The companion note \cite{Ferudun26supports} introduced the
shortest-geodesic support system of a fixed cut: each monochromatic
(\emph{bad}) edge \(xy\) with \(d_B(x,y)=4\) is charged to the crossing
edges on its shortest \(x\)--\(y\) paths, and one asks when these
supports admit a system of distinct representatives, i.e.\ satisfy
Hall's condition. The note constructed triangle-free graphs with unique
maximum cuts on which the Hall condition fails by an unbounded factor,
and classified the minimal violating footprints exactly: none with at
most eight atoms, a unique nine-atom footprint (a double star whose
atom set is a \(K_{3,3}\)), and three ten-atom classes.

This paper is the dynamic companion to that static classification. If
supports fail Hall expansion, a natural repair strategy selects one
geodesic per bad edge and locally re-routes colliding selections by
\emph{detour moves}; the terminal objects such a transport strategy
must exclude are configurations in which a locally deficient pattern
survives every move by merely rotating --- \emph{balanced rotors}. Our
results are of two kinds.

\emph{Structures.} Section~\ref{sec:support-circuits} isolates the
part of the companion's footprint lemma that depends on
inclusion-minimality alone: a support circuit on \(m\) atoms has
exactly \(m-1\) support edges, every edge lies in at least two
supports, the atom--edge incidence graph is connected, and deleting any
single atom leaves a perfect system of distinct representatives; in
classical terms, support circuits are circuits of a transversal
matroid. The nine-atom footprint of \cite{Ferudun26supports} is
exactly the \(t=3\) instance of these circuits, at \(t^2\) atoms on
\(t^2-1\) support edges. Section~\ref{sec:starlemma} proves an
independent local fact about maximum cuts of triangle-free graphs, the
star lemma at cut-tight vertices, whose arithmetic core and pair
manipulations are kernel-checked in Lean~4.
Section~\ref{sec:rotor8} constructs an eight-vertex \emph{neutral
square rotor}: a triangle-free graph with a maximum cut on which every
bad edge has two geodesics and Hall expansion holds strictly, yet no
selection is collision-free and the repair walk never terminates ---
its four states form a single orbit of a cut-preserving automorphism.

\emph{Closures.} Section~\ref{sec:t3closure} proves, in two
independent ways, that nine-atom support circuits carry no balanced
rotor: a shape-independent support-edge count, and a coverage-emptiness
argument on the unique nine-atom footprint of
\cite{Ferudun26supports}; both have kernel-checked Lean~4 cores.
Section~\ref{sec:t4} isolates the first surviving window beyond nine
atoms --- sixteen atoms with two rotating owners of width four, left a
margin of exactly one support edge by the by-hand crossover bounds ---
and closes it by an exhaustive census of all \(153{,}978\) connected
bipartite graphs with fifteen edges, verified along two acceptance
paths. Section~\ref{sec:t5partial} collects the partial state of the
twenty-five-atom window: an order window \(15\le|V(F)|\le21\) proved by
hand, machine exclusion of orders \(15\) and \(16\), two certified
eighteen-vertex near-misses, and an explicit open question. The
closures at \(t=3,4\) are complete; \(t=5\) is not.

Every computer-assisted claim names its verifier and the SHA-256 hash
of the artifact or source it rests on, with single-solver certificates
flagged as such; Section~\ref{sec:repro} aggregates the verification
record, and the ancillary files contain the verifier scripts and the
cited Lean~4 sources. We stress the scope honestly: the theorems below
constrain one local proof strategy for the \(N^2/25\) conjecture ---
they eliminate specific repair mechanisms, at specific window sizes ---
and they produce no new upper bound on \(\bip(G)\) and no progress on
the conjecture itself.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

The ancillary archive (\texttt{anc/}) contains every verifier script
named in this paper, the production sources of every cited Lean~4
module, a command-level \texttt{README}, and a \texttt{SHA256SUMS}
manifest; the hashes below are SHA-256 and match that manifest. The
Lean modules belong to the development \texttt{Erdos23Delta0}; the
declarations cited in
Sections~\ref{sec:starlemma},~\ref{sec:t3closure} and~\ref{sec:t4}
compile without \texttt{sorry} or \texttt{native\_decide}, with axioms
within \(\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Classical.choice},
\texttt{Quot.sound}\}\); for \texttt{LiveMiddleSwapCrossOuter.lean}
this axiom audit is attested twice: by the acceptance ledger of the
source archive and by a fresh rebuild of its five-module import chain
with a kernel \texttt{\#print axioms} probe, which reports axioms
exactly \(\{\texttt{propext},\texttt{Quot.sound}\}\)
(\texttt{anc/lean\_axiom\_probe/}, verdict \texttt{PASS\_AXIOM\_PROBE};
Proposition~\ref{prop:t4:swapgeometry}).
