# Lane L06: exact relation of Problems 840 and 864

## Decisive assessment

**NOVELTY GATE: PASS.** Problem 864 is a strict special case of Problem 840, so a
sharp affirmative solution of 840 would settle 864. However, no known upper
bound for 840 gives new information for 864. The best published 840 bound,
Pikhurko's constant

\[
C_P=\left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}=1.863\ldots,
\]

does apply to every 864-admissible set, including diagonals and an exceptional
sum of unbounded multiplicity, but it is strictly weaker than the already known
864-specific bound \(\sqrt2=1.414\ldots\). The two problems share the same
\(2/\sqrt3\) lower-bound construction; this is not a new consequence for 864.

## Exact statements and conventions

In the standard asymptotic formulation of 840, a family
\(A_N\subseteq [N]\), with \(k_N=|A_N|\to\infty\), is **quasi-Sidon** when

\[
 |A_N+A_N|=(1+o(1))\binom{k_N}{2}.
\]

Here \(A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}\). Pikhurko explicitly defines this full sumset
and defines a Sidon set by requiring all sums with \(a\le b\) to be distinct;
thus diagonal sums \(2a\) are included. The normalization by
\(\binom{k}{2}\), rather than \(\binom{k+1}{2}\), is harmless because the
\(k\) diagonal pairs are lower order. See Pikhurko, pp. 2098-2099,
[definitions and Theorems 2-3](https://opikhurko.warwick.ac.uk/E/Pikhurko06dm.pdf).

For 864, write

\[
r_A(t)=\#\{(a,b):a,b\in A,\ a\le b,\ a+b=t\}.
\]

The hypothesis is that at most one \(t\) has \(r_A(t)>1\), with no fixed bound
on that exceptional multiplicity. Diagonal pairs count. This is the exact
form stated by Erdos in 1992 immediately before his quasi-Sidon question; see
pp. 39-40 of [Some of my Forgotten Problems in Number Theory](https://hrj.episciences.org/125/pdf),
and the modern [Problem 864 statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/864).

## Exact inclusion: 864 implies 840

Let \(A\) be 864-admissible and \(k=|A|\). If the exceptional sum has
multiplicity \(R\) (put \(R=1\) when there is no exception), then counting all
unordered pairs, including diagonals, gives the exact identity

\[
 |A+A|=\binom{k+1}{2}-(R-1).
\]

Distinct representations of one fixed sum use disjoint elements, except that
one representation may be diagonal. Consequently \(R\le\lceil k/2\rceil\),
even though 864 imposes no constant bound on \(R\). Hence

\[
 |A+A|\ge \binom{k+1}{2}-\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+1
 =\binom{k}{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor+1
 =(1+O(k^{-1}))\binom{k}{2}.
\]

Thus every growing sequence of 864-admissible sets is quasi-Sidon in exactly
the sense used for 840. Pikhurko's theorem allows collisions of arbitrary
multiplicity and distribution subject only to the displayed sumset-size
hypothesis, so it certainly covers the single exceptional sum permitted by
864. Conversely, 840 permits losses \(o(k^2)\) spread among many different
sums, so 864 does not capture all quasi-Sidon sets. Therefore an affirmative
sharp upper bound for 840 implies the conjectured upper bound for 864, but an
864 solution would not settle 840.

## Historical relation and shared construction

The relation is not merely a later cross-reference. On p. 175 of Erdos's 1981
paper, he first gives the reflected construction in which all sums are distinct
unless they equal the reflection center, and immediately afterward asks for the
largest quasi-Sidon constant. See [Some Problems and Results on Additive and
Multiplicative Number Theory, p. 175](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1981-33.pdf).
Erdos again places the one-exception problem immediately before the quasi-Sidon
problem in the 1992 source cited above. The 1991 Erdos-Freud paper is the joint
primary source: [On sums of a Sidon-sequence, JNT 38 (1991), 196-205](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(91)90083-N).

In modern notation, choose a genuine Sidon set
\(B\subseteq[1,\lfloor N/3\rfloor]\) with
\(|B|=(1+o(1))\sqrt{N/3}\), and set

\[
 A=B\cup(N-B),\qquad N-B=\{N-b:b\in B\}.
\]

The sums within \(B\), the cross sums, and the sums within \(N-B\) occupy
disjoint ranges. Sidonicity makes every noncentral cross sum unique, while
\(N=b+(N-b)\) has exactly \(|B|\) unordered representations. Hence the only
exceptional sum is \(N\), its multiplicity tends to infinity, diagonals cause no
additional collision, and

\[
 |A|=2|B|=\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N.
\]

This single construction is both the established lower bound for 864 and the
established lower bound for 840.

## What the known 840 bounds transfer

Pikhurko defines

\[
s(k,n)=\max\{|A+A|:A\in\tbinom{[n]}{k}\}
\]

and proves, in the regime relevant here,

\[
s(k,n)\le n+k^2\left(\frac14-\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}+o(1)\right)
\quad\text{(Theorem 2)},
\]

followed by the exact corollary

\[
 |A|\le
 \left(\left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}+o(1)\right)n^{1/2}
 =(1.863\ldots+o(1))n^{1/2}
\quad\text{for quasi-Sidon }A\subseteq[n]
\]

(Theorem 3, p. 2099 of the paper linked above). Substituting the stronger exact
864 sumset count into Theorem 2 produces the same leading constant: its
additional \(O(k)\) distinct sums are swallowed by the theorem's \(o(k^2)\)
error. It yields no sharper 864 constant or lower-order term.

For comparison, if \(t\) is the exceptional sum, then
\(A\cap[1,t/2]\) and \(A\cap(t/2,N]\) are each genuine Sidon sets. Using the
classical \(h(x)=(1+o(1))\sqrt{x}\) bound on each interval gives

\[
 F(N)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt N.
\]

This argument and constant were recorded by Desmond Weisenberg on 11 August
2025 in the [Problem 864 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/864).
Since \(1.863\ldots>\sqrt2\), Pikhurko's 840 theorem is subsumed. The earlier
Erdos-Freud \((2+o(1))\sqrt N\) upper bound for 840 is weaker still. The
[current Problem 840 record](https://www.erdosproblems.com/840) lists no upper
bound better than Pikhurko's.

**Conclusion:** 840 creates no prior-art obstruction to a new improvement of
the \(\sqrt2\) upper bound for 864, and no known 840 result approaches the
target \(2/\sqrt3\) upper constant for the one-exception problem.
