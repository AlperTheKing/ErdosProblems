# Problem 864 novelty gate: stability/inverse theorems for dense interval Sidon sets

## Decisive assessment

**NOVELTY PASS for the proposed structural mechanism.** No proved stability or
inverse theorem was found that takes a near-extremal Sidon subset of an integer
interval and identifies it, even up to `o(sqrt(N))` edits, with a particular
algebraic model. In particular, no theorem forces two Sidon blocks to be
reflections of one another. The strongest proved interval results are
equidistribution, gap, order-statistic, and Fourier-uniformity estimates. The
closest genuine inverse statement is explicitly conjectural, and even its exact
`F_p^2` special case is open.

Consequently, a theorem for Problem 864 concluding, for some exceptional sum
`sigma` and Sidon set `B`,

\[
  |A\mathbin{\triangle}(B\cup(\sigma-B))|=o(\sqrt N)
\]

(with the appropriate two support intervals) would be new relative to the
sources below. None of those sources supplies the required cross-block
correlation.

## Exact conventions

Problem 864 uses unordered sums `a+b` with `a <= b`, including `a=b`. This is
exactly Cilleruelo's convention: a Sidon set is one for which all sums
`a+a'`, `a <= a'`, are different. Eberhard--Manners and Ding likewise define
Sidonicity through unordered pairs, including diagonals. Ortega--Prendiville use
the equivalent torsion-free formulation that every nonzero difference has at
most one ordered representation. Thus there is **no diagonal-convention gap**.

There is, however, a decisive hypothesis gap: every theorem below assumes a
genuine Sidon set, with no exceptional sum. None permits one sum to have
unbounded representation multiplicity.

## 1. Sharpest explicit interval theorem: Cilleruelo

[Cilleruelo, *Gaps in Dense Sidon Sets*, Integers 0 (2000), A11, Theorem 1.1](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/a11/a11.pdf)
states the following. Let `A subset [1,N]` be Sidon and write

\[
  |A|=N^{1/2}-L, \qquad L_+=\max(0,L).
\]

For every interval `I subset [1,N]` of length `cN`, write
`|A cap I|=c|A|+E_I`. Then

\[
 |E_I|\le 52N^{1/4}(1+c^{1/2}N^{1/8})
                 (1+L_+^{1/2}N^{-1/8}).                 \tag{C}
\]

The numerical constant is **52**; the statement is uniform in the location of
`I`. The [archival journal record](https://zenodo.org/records/7554449) confirms
the same formula and notation.

Consequences and sharpness, with exact scope:

- If `|A|=N^{1/2}+O(N^{1/4})`, Corollary 1.1 gives
  `g(A) << N^{3/4}`, where `g(A)` is the largest consecutive gap. The implied
  constant depends on the constant in the cardinality hypothesis.
- The exponent `3/4` is sharp **under that `O(N^{1/4})` cardinality error**.
  For `N=p^2+p+1`, the paper starts from a `p`-element Sidon set, partitions
  `[1,N]` into intervals of length `floor(N^{3/4})`, and deletes fewer than
  `2N^{1/4}` elements from one such interval. The remaining Sidon set still has
  `N^{1/2}+O(N^{1/4})` elements and has a gap `>> N^{3/4}`.
- The paper explicitly says that a better gap estimate was not known when the
  cardinality error is smaller than `N^{1/4}`, and even asks about maximal
  Sidon sets. Thus the sharpness construction is not an inverse classification.

For fixed `c>0`, (C) recovers the earlier Erdos--Freud asymptotic: if
`|A|~N^{1/2}`, every interval of length `cN` contains `~cN^{1/2}` elements.
This controls density, not the identity of the elements.

**Applicability to Problem 864:** exact for a genuine Sidon sub-block, including
its diagonal sums; inapplicable to the whole admissible set when the exceptional
sum has multiplicity at least two.

## 2. Strongest global uniformity theorem: Ortega--Prendiville

[Ortega--Prendiville, *Extremal Sidon Sets are Fourier Uniform*, JTNB 35
(2023), Theorem 6.3](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.1239.pdf)
proves, for every Sidon set `S subset [N]`,

\[
 \left\|\widehat{1_S}-{ |S|\over N}\widehat{1_{[N]}}\right\|_\infty
 \ll N^{1/2}
 \left(\left|1-{|S|\over N^{1/2}}\right|+N^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}. \tag{F}
\]

The implied constant is absolute but is not made numerical. The earlier
Theorem 1.2 has the same statement with `N^{-1/6}` in place of `N^{-1/4}`.
Corollary 1.5 gives interval equidistribution, and Corollaries 1.6, 1.7, and
1.11 give equidistribution in residue classes and (smooth or regular) Bohr
neighbourhoods. The hypotheses always require a genuine Sidon set.

The current local consequence is recorded explicitly by
[Ding, *Dense Finite Sidon Sets on Arithmetic Progressions*, arXiv:2606.15041v2
(24 June 2026), Lemma 9 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15041).
Define

\[
 \Phi(S,N)=N^{1/2}
 \left(\left|1-{|S|\over N^{1/2}}\right|+N^{-1/4}\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Uniformly for every finite arithmetic progression `P subset [N]`,

\[
 \left||S\cap P|-{|S|\over N}|P|\right|
 \ll \Phi(S,N)\log(2N),                              \tag{AP}
\]

with an absolute implied constant. More generally, for every fixed integer
`ell >= 0`,

\[
 \sum_{a\in S\cap P}a^\ell
 ={ |S|\over N}\sum_{a\in P}a^\ell
 +O_\ell\!\left(N^\ell\Phi(S,N)\log(2N)\right).      \tag{W}
\]

These are one-set pseudorandomness statements. They neither identify `S` with
a construction nor compare two sets `S_1` and `S_2` under reflection. In
particular, (F), (AP), and (W) give no lower bound for the reflection matching
correlation `sum_x 1_{S_1}(x)1_{S_2}(sigma-x)`.

**Applicability to Problem 864:** exact for each genuine one-sided piece;
inapplicable to the whole admissible set. The Fourier proof uses the genuine
Sidon correlation bound at every nonzero shift, so an unbounded exceptional
multiplicity is not an allowed error term.

## 3. Order statistics do not give structural stability

[Balasubramanian--Dutta, *The m-th Element of a Sidon Set*, J. Number Theory
279 (2026), 594--602, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2025.07.007; Theorem 3 in the author
version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01986) defines a **dense** Sidon set in
`[n]` to mean a Sidon set of maximum possible cardinality. If

\[
 A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_{|A|}\}\subset[n],\qquad
 |A|=n^{1/2}-L',\qquad L=\max(0,L'),
\]

is dense, then, uniformly for `1 <= m <= |A|`,

\[
 a_m=m n^{1/2}+O(n^{7/8})+O(L^{1/2}n^{3/4}).          \tag{O}
\]

No numerical constants are supplied in the two `O` terms. The body theorem is
for maximum-cardinality sets; its proof uses `L << n^{21/80}`. Formula (O) is a
quantitative order-statistic consequence of Cilleruelo's theorem. It does not
give edit distance to a model, uniqueness, reflection symmetry, or a pairing
between two Sidon blocks.

**Applicability to Problem 864:** only after isolating a genuine Sidon piece,
and only if that piece satisfies the theorem's near-maximum hypothesis in its
own ambient interval. It does not apply to arbitrary one-exception sets.

## 4. The closest inverse theorem is still a conjecture

[Eberhard--Manners, *The Apparent Structure of Dense Sidon Sets*, EJC 30(1)
(2023), P1.33](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v30i1p33/pdf/)
works in a finite abelian group `G` of order `n` and calls `S` dense when
`|S| >= (1-o(1))n^{1/2}` along an asymptotic family.

Their proved Proposition 2.1 says that the development `dev(S)`, with point and
line sets both equal to `G` and incidence `p-l in S`, is a partial linear space
if and only if `S` is Sidon. This is an exact reformulation, not a completion or
classification theorem.

The structural conclusions are explicitly unproved:

- **Conjecture 5.1.** For a dense Sidon set `S subset G`, the group `G` acts
  faithfully on a projective plane `P` with `|P|=(1+o(1))n`, and for some point
  `p` and line `l`, `S subset {g in G : p^g in l}`. Equivalently, `dev(S)` can
  be completed to a projective plane by adding `o(n)` points and lines.
- **Conjecture 5.3.** If `G=F_p^2` and `S subset G` is Sidon with exactly `p`
  elements, then `(G \ (S-S)) union {0}` is a subgroup of order `p`. The paper
  states that even this basic case is unresolved.
- **Conjecture 5.4.** For sufficiently large prime `p`, every `p`-element Sidon
  subset of `C_{p^2+p+1}` is contained in a perfect difference set of size
  `p+1`. Thus even one-point completion in this exact cyclic setting is posed
  as a conjecture.

The paper classifies only the Sidon sets already known to arise from its
Desarguesian projective-plane construction; it does **not** classify arbitrary
dense Sidon sets. As of 24 June 2026, Croot--Mao--Pohoata--Sheffer--Yip still
state that "many natural inverse and stability questions about dense Sidon sets
remain wide open" in
[*A Combinatorial Large Sieve for Sidon Sets, Distances, and Norm Forms*,
section 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17487).

Even Conjecture 5.1, if proved, concerns completion to a projective plane in a
finite group. It would not by itself imply a reflected two-interval structure
in the integers.

## 5. Exact failure under one exceptional sum

Let `sigma` be the exceptional sum for an admissible `A subset [N]`, and put

\[
 A_-:=\{a\in A:2a<\sigma\},\qquad
 A_+:=\{a\in A:2a>\sigma\}.
\]

Both `A_-` and `A_+` are genuine Sidon sets: an internal sum in the first is
strictly below `sigma`, and an internal sum in the second is strictly above it.
If `sigma/2` is an integer in `A`, adjoining that midpoint to either piece still
gives a genuine Sidon set. Therefore the interval and Fourier theorems above
may be applied **separately** to these pieces when their cardinalities meet the
relevant near-extremal hypotheses.

They cannot be applied to `A` itself. Suppose the exceptional sum has `r`
off-diagonal representations

\[
 \sigma=x_i+(\sigma-x_i),\qquad x_i<\sigma/2,\quad 1\le i\le r.
\]

For every `i != j`,

\[
 x_i-x_j=(\sigma-x_j)-(\sigma-x_i),
\]

so the same nonzero difference has two distinct ordered representations. Since
the `x_i` lie in the genuine Sidon piece `A_-`, their positive differences are
themselves distinct; hence there are `binom(r,2)` distinct shifts at which the
Sidon difference bound fails. Cilleruelo's Lemma 2.2 uses `d(h) <= 1` for every
positive `h`, and the Ortega--Prendiville argument uses the corresponding
one-pair correlation bound for every nonzero shift. Thus one exceptional **sum**
of arbitrary multiplicity is not one harmless exceptional term in either
proof; it can generate quadratically many bad difference shifts.

The optional diagonal representation `sigma/2+sigma/2` changes none of this.
All cited genuine-Sidon theorems already count diagonals correctly; what they
exclude is the second and subsequent representation of `sigma`.

## Bottom line for the reflected two-block route

1. Cilleruelo gives the strongest explicit spatial control and the only sharp
   statement located: the `N^{3/4}` maximum-gap exponent at cardinality error
   `O(N^{1/4})`.
2. Ortega--Prendiville and Ding strengthen spatial control to Fourier, Bohr,
   progression, and weighted equidistribution, still without model recovery.
3. Balasubramanian--Dutta control order statistics, again without uniqueness or
   reflection.
4. The only literature statements resembling inverse stability are the open
   Eberhard--Manners conjectures, even in exact finite-group cases.
5. For Problem 864 the proved results can constrain each side of the exceptional
   center separately, but no cited theorem couples the sides or tolerates the
   full arbitrary-multiplicity exception.

**Final novelty verdict: PASS.** A reflected two-block stability theorem for
near-extremal admissible sets in Problem 864 is not present in the located
primary literature and would cross an explicitly open inverse-theory barrier,
as well as a separate one-exception hypothesis barrier.
