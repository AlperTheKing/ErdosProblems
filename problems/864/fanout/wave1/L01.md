# L01: Erdős-Freud (1991) and Erdős (1992)

## Decisive novelty assessment

**PASS, but only for the sharp upper bound.** Neither assigned source proves

\[
F(N)\le (2/\sqrt 3+o(1))\sqrt N.
\]

The 1992 source states the matching equality only as a probability/conjecture. The lower bound with constant (2/\sqrt3), including a construction having one exceptional sum with unbounded multiplicity, is already proved prior art. The difference analogues and the broader quasi-Sidon lower bound are also prior art. Thus:

* a proof of the sharp upper bound for the exact Problem 864 class passes this lane of the novelty gate;
* the reflected-set lower construction, the constant (2/\sqrt3) lower bound, or either difference analogue fails novelty;
* the (1.98\sqrt N) quasi-Sidon bound mentioned in connection with the 1991 paper is **not a proved theorem there**: it was a promised future proof that was apparently never published.

## Counting convention and match to Problem 864

Write

\[
U_A(m)=\#\{(i,j):1\le i\le j\le k,\ a_i+a_j=m\}.
\]

This is the convention relevant to both sources. Their phrase that the sums (a_i+a_j) are all distinct cannot literally count both ordered pairs ((i,j)) and ((j,i)), since every off-diagonal sum would then repeat. It therefore means unordered index pairs, conventionally (i\le j). Pairs (i=j) are included, so diagonal sums (2a_i) count.

The 1992 one-exception statement imposes no bound on the number of representations of the exceptional integer. Its hypothesis is that there is only one (m) for which the representation count exceeds one. Hence the exception may have multiplicity tending to infinity. This is the same substantive condition as Problem 864, except that the paper says "one" while Problem 864 says "at most one." The zero-exception case is an ordinary Sidon set and has maximum size ((1+o(1))\sqrt N), so adjoining that case does not alter the conjectured maximum (2\sqrt{N/3}).

For differences the convention is positive differences (a_i-a_j) with (i>j); diagonal zero differences are not counted. Those results are analogues, not theorems directly about Problem 864.

## 1. Erdős-Freud, *On sums of a Sidon-sequence* (1991)

Primary citation: P. Erdős and R. Freud, *Journal of Number Theory* **38** (1991), 196-205, [doi:10.1016/0022-314X(91)90083-N](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(91)90083-N). The publisher's article page and abstract are the primary source for EF91.2 below. EF91.1 and the end-of-paper quasi-Sidon statements are cross-checked against Pikhurko's page-specific account of EF91 and O'Bryant's annotated bibliography because the publisher PDF endpoint did not expose the article text.

### Proved results

**EF91.1: Equidistribution lemma for dense genuine Sidon sets.** If (A\subseteq[1,n]) is a Sidon set and

\[
|A|=(1+o(1))\sqrt n,
\]

then for every interval (I\subseteq[1,n]),

\[
|A\cap I|=\frac{|I|}{\sqrt n}+o(\sqrt n).
\]

Equivalently, for fixed (0\le \alpha<\beta\le1),

\[
|A\cap[\alpha n,\beta n]|=(\beta-\alpha+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

O'Bryant identifies this as the opening lemma of EF91; Pikhurko identifies his residue-class-and-interval lemma as a common generalization of EF91 Lemma 1. **Applicability:** the hypothesis requires every unordered sum, including diagonals, to be unique. It does not apply directly to a Problem 864 set having an exceptional sum, but it applies to the Sidon set (B) used in the lower construction.

**EF91.2: Number of sums below (n).** Let (1\le a_1<\cdots<a_k\le n) be a genuine Sidon sequence, and let (S(n)) be the maximum possible number of its distinct sums (a_i+a_j) that are less than (n). For every (\varepsilon>0), for all sufficiently large (n),

\[
1-\frac1{\sqrt2}-\varepsilon
\le \frac{S(n)}n
\le \frac1\pi+\varepsilon.
\]

This is the theorem stated in the EF91 publisher abstract. **Applicability:** it concerns genuine Sidon sets and a count of sums below a threshold; it is not an upper bound for Problem 864.

**EF91.3: Quasi-Sidon construction.** EF91 calls (A\subseteq[1,n]), (|A|=k), quasi-Sidon when

\[
|A+A|=(1+o(1)){k\choose2}.
\]

It constructs such sets with

\[
k=\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n.
\]

The elementary range count also gives the proved coarse upper bound

\[
k\le(2+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

**Applicability:** every Problem 864 set is quasi-Sidon. Indeed, if an exceptional sum exists and has multiplicity (t), then (t\le\lceil k/2\rceil), and

\[
|A+A|={k+1\choose2}-(t-1)
      ={k\choose2}+O(k)
      =(1+o(1)){k\choose2}.
\]

Consequently any quasi-Sidon upper bound applies to Problem 864, but EF91.3 is far too weak to prove the target constant.

**EF91.4: The one-exception lower construction.** Take a Sidon set

\[
B\subseteq[1,\lfloor n/3\rfloor],\qquad
|B|=(1+o(1))\sqrt{n/3},
\]

and put

\[
A=B\cup(n-B).
\]

Then

\[
|A|=\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n.
\]

All unordered sums, including diagonal sums, are unique except (n). The exceptional sum (n) has exactly (|B|) unordered representations ({b,n-b}), so its multiplicity is $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, not bounded by any fixed constant. The intervals containing (B+B), (B+(n-B)), and ((n-B)+(n-B)) are disjoint; uniqueness of the nonzero cross sums follows from uniqueness of positive differences in (B). **Applicability:** this construction satisfies Problem 864 exactly and proves its stated lower bound. It is not novel.

### Posed or unproved statements

**EF91.P1: Quasi-Sidon extremal problem.** Under

\[
|A+A|=(1+o(1)){k\choose2},
\]

determine the maximum possible (k) for (A\subseteq[1,n]). This is a posed problem, not a theorem. It strictly contains Problem 864.

**EF91.P2: Announced (1.98) upper bound.** On p. 204 EF91 promised a later proof of

\[
k\le(1.98+o(1))\sqrt n
\]

for quasi-Sidon sets (also reported in rounded form as $\sqrt{3.93\,n}$). Pikhurko records that the promised proof was not published. This must be classified as an **unproved announcement**, not as a proved EF91 upper bound. In any event $1.98>2/\sqrt3$, so even the announced result would not settle Problem 864.

**EF91.P3: One-exception sharp value.** The construction EF91.4 motivates the special one-exception extremal question whose exact formulation is printed in the 1992 source below. The assigned sources provide no EF91 proof of the corresponding upper bound. The bibliographic caveat under "Source identity" prevents treating the wording in E92 as a verbatim quotation from the JNT article.

## 2. Erdős, *Some of my Forgotten Problems in Number Theory* (1992)

Primary citation: P. Erdős, *Hardy-Ramanujan Journal* **15** (1992), 34-50, [doi:10.46298/hrj.1992.125](https://doi.org/10.46298/hrj.1992.125); [primary PDF](https://hrj.episciences.org/125/pdf). The relevant text is on printed pp. 39-40 (PDF pp. 6-7).

### Proved results reported there

**E92.1: Globally bounded representation, (r=1).** For fixed (r\ge1), let (k^{(r)}(n)) be the maximum (k) for which (1\le a_1<\cdots<a_k\le n) and every sum has at most (r) unordered representations. The paper writes

\[
\max k^{(r)}(n)=(c_r+o(1))\sqrt n,
\]

and defines (c'_r) analogously for differences. It records the Erdős-Turan result

\[
c_1=c'_1=1.
\]

**Applicability:** (r=1) is the zero-exception Sidon case. No fixed (r) models Problem 864, since its single exceptional sum may have $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ representations.

**E92.2: One exceptional sum, lower bound.** If there is only one integer (m) for which (a_i+a_j=m) has more than one unordered solution, then the paper says it is possible to attain

\[
k\ge \frac2{\sqrt3}\sqrt n
\]

in the asymptotic sense supplied by the EF construction, namely

\[
k=\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n.
\]

There is no cap on the exceptional multiplicity. This is the exact lower-bound side of Problem 864, with diagonals included.

**E92.3: One exceptional difference.** Under the analogous hypothesis that only one value of (a_i-a_j), (i>j), has more than one representation, E92 reports

\[
\max k=(1+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

The scan has a faint/defective comparison sign in this sentence, but its parallel syntax and mathematical context identify the condition as "more than one." This is stated as an obtained equality, not as a question. It does not count diagonal zero differences and does not prove the sum analogue.

**E92.4: Quasi-Sidon sum lower bound.** If

\[
\#\{a_i+a_j:1\le i\le j\le k\}
   =(1+o(1)){k\choose2},
\]

then E92 says only that the authors could show

\[
\max k\ge\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n.
\]

This is a proved lower bound, not an asymptotic evaluation of the maximum.

**E92.5: Quasi-Sidon difference equality.** Under the difference analogue

\[
\#\{a_i-a_j:i>j\}=(1+o(1)){k\choose2},
\]

E92 reports

\[
\max k=(1+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

Again this is a proved/reported equality for differences only.

### Posed questions and conjectures

**E92.P1: Constants for bounded multiplicity.** Determine (c_r) and (c'_r). For (r>1), E92 conjectures that

\[
c_r\ne c'_r,
\qquad\text{and more specifically}\qquad
c'_r<c_r.
\]

This is not a proved assertion and does not cover an exceptional sum of arbitrary multiplicity.

**E92.P2: Exact Problem 864 conjecture.** Under the hypothesis that only one sum has more than one unordered representation, E92 says the maximum is "probably" 

\[
\max k=(1+o(1))\frac2{\sqrt3}\sqrt n.
\]

The word "probably" is dispositive: the upper bound and equality are conjectural. The source proves/reports only the matching lower construction.

**E92.P3: Quasi-Sidon extremal question.** If the number of distinct sums is ((1+o(1)){k\choose2}), E92 asks what can be said about (max k). It follows this question by saying that only the (2/\sqrt3) lower bound was obtained. Thus no sharp quasi-Sidon upper theorem is asserted there.

## Source identity caveat

On printed p. 39 E92 introduces the target paragraph as further problems from work "with Freud." But E92 reference [4], printed on p. 49, is P. Erdős and R. Freud, *On Sidon-sequences and related problems*, Hungarian Mat. Lapok **2** (1991), 1-44, not the English JNT article. Modern catalogues cross-reference the JNT paper for Problems 840 and 864, and later literature specifically locates the quasi-Sidon announcement on EF91 p. 204. The safe attribution is therefore:

* EF91 directly supplies the genuine-Sidon theorems and the quasi-Sidon problem/results described above;
* E92 pp. 39-40 directly supplies the exact one-exception wording, the (2/\sqrt3) lower bound, and the explicit conjectural status;
* E92 alone does not justify claiming that every sentence in its paragraph occurs verbatim in the JNT article.
