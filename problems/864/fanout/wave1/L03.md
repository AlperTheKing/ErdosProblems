# L03: \(B_2[g]\), almost-Sidon, weak Sidon, and near-Sidon audit

**Novelty assessment: PASS (not preempted), as of 2026-07-12.**  None of the
standard notions below implies the hypothesis of Erdős Problem #864, except the
strict special case \(B_2[1]\) (a genuine Sidon set).  Conversely, #864 does not
fall under any fixed-\(g\) theorem, the modern additive-energy definition of
almost-Sidon, or weak Sidon.  The one published general theorem that applies to
every #864 set is Pikhurko's quasi-Sidon bound, with constant

\[
 C_{\rm P}:=\left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}
 =1.863\ldots,
\]

not the conjectured \(2/\sqrt3=1.154700\ldots\).  A public 2025 argument specific
to #864 gives the stronger \(\sqrt2=1.414213\ldots\) upper constant, but still
does not settle the conjecture.  The official problem page and discussion thread
remain marked **OPEN** and list no complete solution.

## 1. Exact target and two useful translations

For \(A\subseteq[1,N]\), put

\[
 \mu_A(s)=\#\{\{a,b\}:a,b\in A,\ a\le b,\ a+b=s\},\qquad k=|A|.
\]

Thus the domain of the sum map consists of the \(P=k(k+1)/2\) unordered pairs
**with repetition**.  The #864 hypothesis is

\[
 q(A):=\#\{s:\mu_A(s)\ge2\}\le1,
\]

with no bound on the exceptional value \(m=\mu_A(s_0)\).  Its representations
are disjoint complementary pairs, with at most one diagonal pair, so
\(m\le\lceil k/2\rceil\).  If there is no exception, set \(m=1\).  Then exactly

\[
 |A+A|=\frac{k(k+1)}2-(m-1)
       =\binom{k}{2}+O(k).                                      \tag{1}
\]

Consequently every asymptotic family satisfying #864 is quasi-Sidon in the
Erdős-Freud sense.

For the modern energy convention, let

\[
 R_A(s)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b=s\},\qquad
 E(A)=\sum_sR_A(s)^2.
\]

This is also the number of ordered solutions of \(x-x'=y-y'\).  A genuine
Sidon set has \(E(A)=2k^2-k\).  For a #864 set, exact diagonal bookkeeping gives

\[
 E(A)=
 \begin{cases}
  2k^2-k+4m(m-1),&s_0/2\notin A,\\
  2k^2-k+4(m-1)^2,&s_0/2\in A.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{2}
\]

Formula (2) is the key reason that #864 is quasi-Sidon but need not be
energy-almost-Sidon.

Two examples separate all of the definitions below.

* \(C=\{1,2,3,5\}\) is weak Sidon and \(B_2[2]\), but
  \(1+3=2+2=4\) and \(1+5=3+3=6\), so it has two exceptional sum values and
  is not #864.  For an asymptotic example with exactly the same two defects,
  take \(C\cup100S_j\), where \(S_j\) is any growing family of genuine Sidon
  sets.  All cross sums are separated by their residues modulo 100.  If its
  size is \(k\), this family has \(|A+A|=k(k+1)/2-2\) and
  \(E(A)=2k^2-k+8\).
* If \(B\subset[1,M/3)\) is Sidon and \(A=B\cup(M-B)\), then the only repeated
  sum is \(M\), represented \(|B|\) times.  Writing \(k=2|B|\), this gives
  \(m=k/2\) and \(E(A)=3k^2-3k\).  This is the Erdős-Freud construction behind
  the \((2/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt M\) lower bound.

## 2. \(B_2[g]\): the cap is on every fibre

### Standard unordered definition

Yu's definition is exactly aligned with #864's pair convention: for fixed
positive integers \(g,N\), \(A\subseteq[1,N]\) is a \(B_2[g]\) set when every
integer \(s\) has at most \(g\) representations \(s=a+b\) with
\(a,b\in A\) and \(a\le b\).  Diagonals are included.  If \(F(g,N)\) is the
maximum cardinality and

\[
 \sigma(g)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(g,N)}{\sqrt{gN}},
\]

Yu's Theorem 1 states

\[
 \sigma(g)<\sqrt{3.2}.                                         \tag{3}
\]

The quantifier is important: \(g\) is fixed before \(N\to\infty\).  Yu also
records Green's earlier bound
\(\sigma(g)\le\sqrt{3.5-1.75/g}\), useful for small fixed \(g\).

Nathanson's random-set theorem has the same fixed-\(g\) hypothesis.  If
\(B_2[g](k,N)\) counts \(k\)-element \(B_2[g]\) subsets of \([N]\), then

\[
 \frac{B_2[g](k,N)}{\binom Nk}\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{when}\quad k=o\!\left(N^{g/(2g+2)}\right).          \tag{4}
\]

This is a distributional statement below the square-root scale for every fixed
\(g\), not a one-exception result.

### Why it does not imply #864

For \(g=1\), \(B_2[1]\) is genuine Sidon and therefore implies #864 with no
exception.  For every \(g\ge2\), the implication fails: the set
\(\{1,2,3,5\}\) is \(B_2[2]\subseteq B_2[g]\), but the two values 4 and 6
both have multiplicity 2.  A \(B_2[g]\) hypothesis caps fibre *height* and says
nothing about how many fibres are non-singleton.

The reverse implication also fails for every fixed \(g\).  In the
Erdős-Freud family \(B\cup(M-B)\), the unique exceptional fibre has size
\(|B|\to\infty\).  Thus an individual #864 set is of course \(B_2[m]\), but
there is no uniform \(g\).  Taking \(g=m\asymp k\asymp\sqrt N\) is not allowed
in (3); even a hypothetical uniform \(O(\sqrt{gN})\) estimate would be of order
\(N^{3/4}\), not the required square-root bound.

### Ordered conventions do not repair the mismatch

Cilleruelo-Ruzsa-Vinuesa use

\[
 r_A(s)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b=s\},\qquad
 r'_A(s)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a\ne b,\ a+b=s\},
\]

and call \(r_A\le g\) a \(g\)-Sidon set and \(r'_A\le g\) a weak
\(g\)-Sidon set.  In \(\mathbb Z\),

\[
 r_A(s)=2\mu_A(s)-\mathbf1_{s/2\in A},\qquad
 r'_A(s)=2\bigl(\mu_A(s)-\mathbf1_{s/2\in A}\bigr).            \tag{5}
\]

Accordingly their 2-Sidon sets are ordinary Sidon sets, while their weak
2-Sidon sets are the classical weak Sidon sets discussed below.  Some authors
write \(B_2^*[g]\) for an ordered convention.  Formula (5) must be checked
before importing a theorem, but under either convention bounded multiplicity
does not localize the collisions at one sum.

## 3. “Almost Sidon” has two incompatible standard meanings

### 3.1 Erdős-Freud/Sárközy-Sós: quasi-Sidon or near-maximal sumset

Erdős and Freud call a family \(A\subseteq[n]\), \(|A|=k\), quasi-Sidon when

\[
 |A+A|=(1+o(1))\binom{k}{2}.                                  \tag{6}
\]

Sárközy and Sós call the same condition “almost Sidon” in their Problem 5.4.
Because the normalization is quadratic, the \(k\) diagonal pairs and any
\(O(k)\) loss of distinct sums are invisible.  Equation (1) proves

\[
 \text{#864}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{quasi-Sidon}.      \tag{7}
\]

The converse is false even with only two defects: the family
\(\{1,2,3,5\}\cup100S_j\) above satisfies (6), but both 4 and 6 repeat.
In general (6) allows \(o(k^2)\) lost sums distributed over \(o(k^2)\)
different values and places no cap on a fibre.

Pikhurko's exact relevant statements are as follows.  For

\[
 s(k,n)=\max\{|A+A|:A\subseteq[n],\ |A|=k\},
\]

his Theorem 2 gives

\[
 s(k,n)\le n+k^2\left(\frac14-\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}+o(1)\right).   \tag{8}
\]

His Theorem 3 therefore says that every quasi-Sidon \(A\subseteq[n]\) obeys

\[
 |A|\le\left(C_{\rm P}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n,
 \qquad
 C_{\rm P}=\left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}
 =1.863\ldots.                                                 \tag{9}
\]

By (7), (9) applies to #864, including diagonal pairs and an exceptional sum
of arbitrary multiplicity.  It is, however, far weaker than the desired
\(2/\sqrt3\) constant.  The official Problem #840 asks for the extremal size
under (6); it lists the same Erdős-Freud lower constant and Pikhurko upper
constant and remains open.  The official #864 page correctly calls #864's
conjecture a weaker assertion than #840's conjecture because #864 ranges over
a strictly smaller class.

### 3.2 Modern additive-combinatorics usage: near-minimal energy

Prendiville defines

\[
 E(S)=\#\{(x,x',y,y')\in S^4:x-x'=y-y'\}
\]

and notes that a finite \(S\) is Sidon exactly when
\(E(S)\le2|S|^2-|S|\).  In this literature “almost Sidon” means

\[
 E(S)\le(2+o(1))|S|^2.                                       \tag{10}
\]

One precise auxiliary result is Prendiville's Lemma 5.2: if
\(0\le\eta<1\), \(S\subseteq[N]\), and
\(E(S)\le(2+\eta)|S|^2\), then

\[
 |S|\le2\left(\frac{N}{1-\eta}\right)^{1/2}.                 \tag{11}
\]

This energy includes all ordered quadruples and hence includes diagonal
effects; it is not a restricted-sum convention.

Condition (10) does not imply #864.  The family
\(\{1,2,3,5\}\cup100S_j\) has energy \(2k^2-k+8=(2+o(1))k^2\)
but two repeated sum values.  Conversely #864 does not imply (10): the
Erdős-Freud family has \(E(A)=3k^2-3k\), by (2).  Thus the arbitrary
multiplicity at the sole exceptional sum is large enough to violate
near-minimal energy.

Energy-almost-Sidon does imply quasi-Sidon (small energy excess forces only
\(o(k^2)\) total pair identifications), but quasi-Sidon does not imply
energy-almost-Sidon; the Erdős-Freud family is again the exact counterexample.
None of the regularity, modelling, or linear-equation theorems for (10)
localizes the energy excess at one sum or yields the #864 constant.

## 4. Weak Sidon: restricted sums are unique and diagonals are omitted

The standard definition for a finite \(A\subset\mathbb R\) is

\[
 x+y\quad(x,y\in A,\ x<y)\quad\text{are all distinct}.         \tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, \(x+y=u+v\) is forbidden when the two off-diagonal unordered
pairs differ; such a collision necessarily uses four distinct elements.
Sárközy-Sós formulate this as forbidding the equation only when all four
variables are distinct and explicitly observe that a three-term progression
\(x+y=2u\) remains allowed.  They state that the maximum cardinalities of
Sidon and weak Sidon subsets of \([N]\) are asymptotically the same; hence if
\(W(N)\) denotes the weak maximum,

\[
 W(N)=(1+o(1))\sqrt N.                                       \tag{13}
\]

A recent theorem concerns Sidon subsets *inside* arbitrary weak Sidon sets.
For weak Sidon \(A\subset\mathbb R\), let \(h(A)\) be the largest size of a
genuine Sidon subset and

\[
 g(n)=\min\{h(A):A\subset\mathbb R,\ |A|=n,\ A\text{ weak Sidon}\}.
\]

Ma and Tang's 2026 Theorem (v2) determines this exactly:

\[
 g(n)=\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rceil\qquad(n\ge1).      \tag{14}
\]

Neither (13) nor (14) applies directly to #864:

* Weak Sidon does not imply #864 because diagonals are ignored.  The weak
  Sidon set \(\{1,2,3,5\}\) has the two diagonal/off-diagonal collisions
  \(1+3=2+2\) and \(1+5=3+3\).
* #864 does not imply weak Sidon because its exceptional sum may have several
  off-diagonal representations.  The set \(\{1,3,7,9\}\) has exactly one
  repeated unordered sum, \(1+9=3+7=10\), but is not weak Sidon.

Thus the classes are incomparable.  Eliminating all but one of the exceptional
off-diagonal pairs can cost about half the elements: in
\(B\cup(M-B)\), the \(|B|\) complementary pairs partition the set.  Any
argument that first forces weak Sidonicity therefore loses precisely the
constant-scale information #864 asks for.

There is also an ambient-group trap.  In groups of exponent two, every diagonal
has sum zero.  Binary-group papers consequently call a set Sidon when there is
no equality using four distinct elements; the single huge diagonal fibre at
zero is built into the ambient group.  Such theorems are not interval theorems
for ordinary integer addition and do not cover an exceptional sum containing
many distinct complementary pairs.

## 5. “Near-Sidon” is not a stable standard class

No canonical finite-integer definition of “near-Sidon” appears in the standard
Sidon bibliography or the primary sources above.  Three usages must be kept
separate.

1. **Deletion language.**  Cilleruelo describes an intermediate sequence in
   Ruzsa's infinite construction as “nearly a Sidon sequence,” meaning that
   deleting sufficiently few offending elements destroys the repeated sums.
   This is descriptive language in an infinite construction, not a finite
   extremal definition or theorem with a #864 constant.  Under the natural
   formalization “delete \(o(|A|)\) elements to become Sidon,” there is still no
   implication to #864: \(\{1,2,3,5\}\cup100S_j\) becomes Sidon after a bounded
   deletion but has two repeated sums.  Conversely, from
   \(B\cup(M-B)\) one must delete at least \(|B|-1=k/2-1\) elements to leave at
   most one complete complementary pair, so #864 need not be deletion-near.
2. **A recent fibre-profile label.**  Bado's unrefereed May 2026 preprint uses
   the heading “Near-Sidon sets” for the hypothesis that every non-singleton
   unordered sum fibre (pairs with repetition) has size exactly 2 and that
   there are \(q\) such fibres.  Corollary 12 computes an associated homology
   group as \(H^{\rm add}_1(A;\Bbbk)\cong\Bbbk^q\) and all other degrees as
   zero.  There is no bound on \(q\), no interval cardinality theorem, and no
   one-exception conclusion.  For \(q=2\), the example above already violates
   #864; in the other direction, #864 permits its sole fibre to have size
   greater than 2.  These two classes are therefore incomparable.
3. **Near-optimal rulers/sets.**  “Near-optimal Sidon set” or “near-optimal
   Golomb ruler” refers to objective value (length or cardinality) close to an
   optimum while retaining the genuine Sidon property.  It is not a relaxed
   collision condition and supplies no theorem about one exceptional sum.

The term “near-Sidon” therefore cannot be used as a prior-art match without
first quoting a definition.  None of the located usages includes the exact
profile \(q(A)\le1\) with an unbounded exceptional fibre.

## 6. Direct prior art and final gate decision

Erdős's 1992 primary source states the problem in exactly the relevant form:
there is only one integer \(m\) for which the number of solutions of
\(a_i+a_j=m\) exceeds one.  Erdős-Freud provide the reflected-Sidon lower
construction.  The current official formulation makes explicit that solutions
are unordered with \(a\le b\), diagonals count, and the exceptional
multiplicity is unrestricted.

The only target-specific public upper bound located is a 2025 comment on the
official discussion thread.  If \(s_0\) is the exceptional sum, then
\(A\cap[1,s_0/2]\) and \(A\cap(s_0/2,N]\) are genuine Sidon sets.  Using the
classical \(h(x)=(1+o(1))\sqrt x\) Sidon extremal estimate gives

\[
 F(N)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt N.                                 \tag{15}
\]

This is a public argument rather than a cited journal theorem, and its constant
is strictly above \(2/\sqrt3\).

**Decisive conclusion.**  The novelty gate passes.  A proof of
\(F(N)\le(2/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt N\), or a stronger target-specific asymptotic,
would not follow from the audited \(B_2[g]\), quasi/almost-Sidon, energy,
weak-Sidon, or near-Sidon results.  It would improve the public #864 upper
constant from \(\sqrt2\) to \(2/\sqrt3\) and settle the open Erdős-Freud
question.  The precise gap in all neighboring definitions is **localization of
all nontrivial unordered representations, including diagonal collisions, at a
single sum while allowing that one fibre arbitrary multiplicity**.
