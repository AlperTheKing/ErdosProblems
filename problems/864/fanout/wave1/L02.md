# L02: forward citations to Erdos-Freud (1991)

Search date: 2026-07-12

## Decisive novelty assessment

**PASS for the proposed upper-bound target.** I found no forward-citing work or exact-keyword source that proves, or claims a published proof of,

\[
F(N)\leq \left(\frac{2}{\sqrt 3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N
\]

for sets whose unordered sums, including diagonals, are uniquely represented except for at most one sum of unrestricted multiplicity.

The citation branch contains two results that must be treated as known:

1. The reflected-Sidon construction gives the exact lower constant \(2/\sqrt3\) and satisfies the problem's convention, including diagonals and an exceptional sum whose multiplicity tends to infinity.
2. Pikhurko's published quasi-Sidon theorem applies to every such set, but gives only the weaker constant
   \[
   \left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}=1.863\ldots .
   \]

The often-repeated \(1.98\) upper bound is not a published theorem. Pikhurko explicitly records it as an estimate that Erdos and Freud said would be proved later, but which was never published. It cannot be used to fail the novelty gate.

Thus the exact lower construction is prior art; a matching \(2/\sqrt3\) upper bound remains novel relative to this forward-citation and keyword search.

## Target convention and transfer to quasi-Sidon sets

The assigned statement defines

\[
r_A(s)=\#\{(a,b):a,b\in A,\ a\leq b,\ a+b=s\}.
\]

Let \(k=|A|\), and suppose \(e\) is the sole possible sum with multiplicity \(m=r_A(e)>1\). Distinct unordered pairs with sum \(e\) are disjoint, apart from the possibility of one diagonal pair. Consequently

\[
m\leq \left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
\]

even though the problem places no fixed bound on \(m\). There are \(\binom{k+1}{2}\) unordered pairs including diagonals, so exactly

\[
|A+A|=\binom{k+1}{2}-(m-1)=\frac{k^2}{2}+O(k).
\]

Therefore every asymptotic family admissible for Problem 864 is quasi-Sidon in the standard sense

\[
|A+A|=(1+o(1))\binom{k}{2}.
\]

This implication is why Pikhurko's quasi-Sidon upper bound applies despite the exceptional sum having arbitrary, nonconstant multiplicity. The converse is false: a quasi-Sidon set may have many different repeated sums.

## Primary-source findings

### 1. Erdos-Freud (1991) and Erdos's restatement

P. Erdos and R. Freud, ["On sums of a Sidon-sequence," *Journal of Number Theory* 38 (1991), 196-205](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(91)90083-N), is the cited source. Its published abstract concerns the proportion of pair sums below \(n\) for an ordinary Sidon sequence and states, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
1-\frac1{\sqrt2}-\varepsilon\leq \frac{S(n)}n\leq \frac1\pi+\varepsilon
\]

for sufficiently large \(n\), together with related questions. The one-exception problem occurs among those related questions rather than as a proved matching theorem.

Erdos restated the problem in ["Some of my Forgotten Problems in Number Theory," *Hardy-Ramanujan Journal* 15 (1992), 34-50, pp. 40-41](https://hrj.episciences.org/125/pdf): for \(1\leq a_1<\cdots<a_k\leq n\), there may be one integer \(m\) for which the number of solutions of \(a_i+a_j=m\), \(i\leq j\), exceeds one; all other sums are unique. He gives the reflected construction and conjectures its asymptotic size is best possible. The condition \(i\leq j\) confirms unordered sums with diagonals.

The current curated statement is also recorded as open at [Erdos Problems #864](https://www.erdosproblems.com/864), with the same \(a\leq b\) convention and the lower constant \(2/\sqrt3\).

### 2. Bollobas-Pikhurko (2005): exact lower construction

B. Bollobas and O. Pikhurko, ["Integer sets with prescribed pairwise differences being distinct," *European Journal of Combinatorics* 26 (2005), 607-616](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2004.04.008), [author PDF, pp. 608-609](https://opikhurko.warwick.ac.uk/E/BollobasPikhurko05ejc.pdf), explicitly define a Sidon set by requiring all sums \(a+b\), \(a\leq b\), to be distinct. They then reproduce the relevant Erdos-Freud construction:

* Take a Sidon set \(S\subseteq[1,t]\) of size \(s\), where \(t=(1+o(1))s^2\).
* Put \(S'=3t+1-S\subseteq[2t+1,3t]\) and \(X=S\cup S'\subseteq[1,3t]\).
* The three sum ranges are disjoint:
  \[
  S+S\subseteq[2,2t],\quad S+S'\subseteq[2t+2,4t],\quad
  S'+S'\subseteq[4t+2,6t].
  \]
* Every unordered sum in \(X\), including diagonals within \(S\) and \(S'\), is unique except
  \[
  3t+1=x+(3t+1-x)\qquad(x\in S),
  \]
  which has exactly \(s=|X|/2\) representations.
* Hence
  \[
  |X|=2s=\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{3t}.
  \]

**Applicability:** exact. The source uses unordered sums with \(a\leq b\), includes diagonals, has precisely one exceptional sum, and its exceptional multiplicity is unbounded. Any proof or construction rediscovering this lower bound is not novel.

The paper's own Theorem 3 instead concerns edge-magic graph labellings: there is an absolute \(c>0\) such that, when \(m\leq cn^2\), its graph extremal quantity satisfies \(S(n,m)\leq(1-c)n^2\). That theorem supplies no upper bound for Problem 864.

### 3. Pikhurko (2006): the only published applicable upper bound in the citation branch

O. Pikhurko, ["Dense edge-magic graphs and thin additive bases," *Discrete Mathematics* 306 (2006), 2097-2107](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2006.05.003), [author PDF](https://opikhurko.warwick.ac.uk/E/Pikhurko06dm.pdf), defines

\[
s(k,n)=\max\{|A+A|:A\in\tbinom{[n]}k\}
\]

and calls a family quasi-Sidon when

\[
|A+A|=(1+o(1))\binom{k}{2}.
\]

His Theorem 2 is

\[
s(k,n)\leq n+k^2\left(\frac14-\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}+o(1)\right).
\]

His Theorem 3 states that every quasi-Sidon \(A\subseteq[n]\) satisfies

\[
|A|\leq
\left(\left(\frac14+\frac1{(\pi+2)^2}\right)^{-1/2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n
= (1.863\ldots+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

The generating function used in the proof is

\[
g(x)=\frac{f(x)^2+f(x^2)}2,
\]

whose coefficient counts representations with the two summands ordered as \(s\leq t\). Thus the paper's sum convention includes diagonals rather than silently discarding them.

**Applicability:** yes, through the exact cardinality calculation above. The theorem tolerates the exceptional multiplicity growing as fast as \(\lceil k/2\rceil\), because this loses only \(O(k)\) of the \(\Theta(k^2)\) possible distinct sums. It does not exploit the fact that all collisions are concentrated at one sum, so its constant is much too large to settle Problem 864.

On pp. 2098-2099, Pikhurko states that Erdos and Freud gave the construction

\[
k=\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n

\]

and wrote that they would prove later that every quasi-Sidon set has

\[
k\leq(1.98+o(1))\sqrt n.
\]

Pikhurko immediately notes that this promised result was not published. His \(1.863\ldots\) theorem is the published replacement. Accordingly, \(1.98\) is an announcement, not established prior art.

### 4. Cilleruelo's exact-keyword manuscript

J. Cilleruelo, ["Quasi-Sidon Sets" (undated author manuscript)](https://matematicas.uam.es/~franciscojavier.cilleruelo/Papers/quasi-Sidon%20-%20greedy%20-%20solo%20A-A.pdf), was located through the `quasi-Sidon` variants. It cites Erdos's 1992 account and the Hungarian Erdos-Freud paper rather than the English 1991 article. For finite sum-quasi-Sidon sets it records:

* the same reflected construction with \(|A|\sim(2/\sqrt3)\sqrt n\);
* that it was unknown whether \(2/\sqrt3\) is the largest possible constant even for the broader quasi-Sidon class;
* the announced \(1.98\) and Pikhurko's published \(1.863\ldots\) upper bounds.

Its Theorem 2.1 is a difference-set result:

\[
|A|<\sqrt n+\sqrt{|A|^2-|A-A|}

\]

for \(A\subseteq[1,n]\), and its corollary gives \(|A|\leq(1+o(1))\sqrt n\) when \(|A-A|\sim|A|^2\). These conclusions concern differences, not sums, and do not settle Problem 864.

**Applicability:** the manuscript confirms the lower construction and the state of the broader quasi-Sidon problem, but supplies no matching sum upper bound. Its statement of broader uncertainty is strong negative evidence against a hidden published solution in this literature branch, not itself proof that the narrower problem is open.

### 5. O'Bryant's bibliography: secondary confirmation only

K. O'Bryant, ["A Complete Annotated Bibliography of Work Related to Sidon Sequences," *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* DS11 (2004)](https://doi.org/10.37236/32), [PDF, entry for Erdos-Freud](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/DS11/pdf/), summarizes the construction as a quasi-Sidon set of size asymptotic to \(\sqrt{4n/3}\) and repeats the claimed exclusion of size about \(\sqrt{3.93n}\). It gives no proof of the latter. Read together with Pikhurko's explicit publication-history note, this is secondary reporting of the unpublished \(1.98\) announcement, not an additional theorem.

## Exact keyword and terminology screen

The searches included the following quoted and unquoted variants, combined with `Sidon`, `Erdos Freud`, the paper title, and its DOI:

* `"one exceptional sum"`, `"exceptional sum"`, `"one exception"`
* `"all but one" sums`, `"one repeated sum"`
* `"at most one" "more than one representation" a+b`
* `almost Sidon`, `near-Sidon`, `quasi-Sidon`, `quasi Sidon`
* `weak Sidon`, `nearly Sidon`, `almost B2`, `B_2[g]`
* the exact constants `2/sqrt(3)`, `sqrt(4n/3)`, `1.1547`, `1.98`, `3.93`, and `1.863`

The terminology produces three important false friends:

1. **Weak Sidon:** conventionally requires distinct sums of distinct elements and omits diagonal sums. It still forbids two distinct off-diagonal representations of the exceptional sum, so it does not contain the Problem 864 class.
2. **Fixed \(B_2[g]\):** permits at most \(g\) representations for every sum. No fixed \(g\) contains the reflected construction, whose exceptional multiplicity is \(|A|/2\).
3. **Quasi-Sidon/almost Sidon:** requires only that asymptotically almost all pair sums be distinct. It contains every Problem 864 set but loses the decisive concentration of all collisions at one value. Published results for this broader class stop at Pikhurko's \(1.863\ldots\) constant.

Exact-phrase searches otherwise returned the current Problem 864 page, its sequence/index mirrors, the Bollobas-Pikhurko construction, and secondary descriptions already listed above. They did not reveal a paper proving the conjectured matching upper bound.

## Gate conclusion

* **Known and fatal to lower-bound novelty:** the construction of size \((2/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt N\), with diagonals included and exactly one sum represented \(|A|/2\) times.
* **Known applicable upper bound from this branch:** \((1.863\ldots+o(1))\sqrt N\) via quasi-Sidon theory.
* **Not a theorem:** the \((1.98+o(1))\sqrt N\) announcement.
* **Not applicable:** ordinary Sidon, weak Sidon, fixed-\(g\) \(B_2[g]\), and difference-set theorems listed above.
* **Novelty decision:** a proof of \(F(N)\leq(2/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt N\) for the exact one-exception, unordered-with-diagonals convention passes this lane's novelty gate.
