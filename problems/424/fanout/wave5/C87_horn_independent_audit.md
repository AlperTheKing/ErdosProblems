# C87 independent Horn-graph audit

## Verdict

The fixed-cutoff claim in `C87_horn_2000.json` is verified independently.
At `X=2000`, the reconstructed Horn graph has `83` hard roots, `207`
candidate nonhard targets, and `6032` edges.  It has a matching of size
`82`, while the reported Hall set has `80` hard roots and exactly `79`
neighbors.  Hence the maximum matching size is exactly `82` and the maximum
left Hall deficit is exactly `1`.

This is a finite obstruction to a root-by-root transport using the stated
universal Horn implications.  It is not a counterexample to the C23 image
inequality and is not a proof about all cutoffs.

## Independent reconstruction

The verifier is

```text
problems/424/compute/wave5/C87_horn_independent_verify.py
```

It imports neither C83 nor C87 code and uses no optimizer.  Its construction
differs from the original implementation in two material ways.

1. Rules are generated by enumerating every allowed ordered-by-size pair
   `2 <= a < b` with `ab-1 <= X`, rather than by factoring each output.
2. Least closures are computed by a factor-triggered worklist.  Each rule has
   two initially missing inputs; activation of its second distinct input
   activates its output.  The original ascending closure scan is not used.

The worklist activates only seeds or supported outputs, so induction on
activation time puts its result inside the least closure.  On termination it
checks every rule and is closed, so minimality gives the reverse inclusion.

## Horn characterization

Let `A_X` be the allowed values through `X`, let `G_X` be the least closure
of `{2,3}`, and let `F(S)` be the one-step image, with `{2,3}` included.  For
a root `v`, write `t(v)` for its highest seed-2-chain point at most `X`.
For a hard root `h` and nonhard target `r`, consider

```text
for every allowed forward-closed S containing G,
    t(h) not in F(S)  implies  r not in F(S) and t(r) in F(S).
```

The claimed characterization is exactly:

1. `t(r) in G`; and
2. for every legal distinct factor pair `{a,b}` with `ab-1=r`,
   `t(h) in F(cl(G union {a,b}))`.

There is no hidden assumption in this equivalence.

### Necessity

Every listed hard root has `t(h) not in G=F(G)`.  Taking `S=G` therefore
forces `t(r) in G`.  For a legal pair `{a,b}` of `r`, take the least
forward-closed source `C=cl(G union {a,b})`.  The pair puts `r` in `F(C)`.
If `t(h)` were absent from `F(C)`, this `C` would violate the implication.

### Sufficiency

Suppose `t(h) not in F(S)`.  Since `t(r) in G` and `G=F(G)`, monotonicity
gives `t(r) in F(S)`.  If `r` were in `F(S)`, one of its legal pairs
`{a,b}` would lie in `S`.  Forward closure and `G subset S` give
`cl(G union {a,b}) subset S`; monotonicity of `F` would then put `t(h)` in
`F(S)`, a contradiction.  If `r` has no legal pair, the second condition is
vacuous and `r` is absent from every image, as required.

### Distinct inputs and cutoff locality

The verifier admits only `a<b`; diagonal products are never rules.  The
seed-2 chain uses `2` with a chain value greater than `2`, so its nonseed
steps also respect distinct inputs.

For every legal rule with inputs at least `2`,

```text
ab-1 > max(a,b).
```

Consequently, closure and one-step support at values at most `X` depend only
on source membership at values at most `X`.  No value above the cutoff can
generate or support a value below it.  Thus the finite worklist closure is
the prefix of the global closure.  Conversely, a finite pair closure extends
to the global source `cl(G union {a,b})` without changing its prefix through
`X`.  This validates both directions of the finite iff proof.

Restricting to allowed values is also closed under the operation: allowed
residues are `0` and `2` modulo `3`, whose products minus one again have
residue `2` or `0`.  Hence the witness closures do not leave the stated
universe.

## Exact Hall obstruction

An independent Hopcroft-Karp implementation found a matching of size `82`.
The stored 82-edge matching was separately checked for distinct endpoints
and for membership in the independently reconstructed graph.

The stored Hall set contains `80` of the `83` hard roots; the omitted roots
are

```text
534, 954, 1134.
```

The exact union of the adjacency lists of those 80 roots is the stored set
of `79` neighbors.  Therefore every matching has size at most

```text
(83-80) + 79 = 82.
```

Together with the size-82 matching, this proves the optimum exactly.  The
independent matching's alternating-reachability computation recovered the
same 80-root and 79-neighbor Hall sets.  A size-82 matching also shows that
no hard subset can have deficit greater than one, so the maximum Hall
deficit is exactly one.

## Exact scope

Verified: at cutoff `X=2000`, the stated universal Horn implication graph is
characterized by grounded target tops and all minimal pair closures, and that
graph has exact maximum matching size `82` with a `80`-versus-`79` Hall
obstruction.

cut-adaptive or nonlocal transport, an obstruction at every cutoff, or any
