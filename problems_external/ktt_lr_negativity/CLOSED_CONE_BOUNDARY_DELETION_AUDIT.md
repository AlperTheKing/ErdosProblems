# Closed-cone boundary deletion: exact contract and obstruction

Date: 2026-07-22

## Verdict

A flat-rhombus picture, an ear label, and the abstract face lattice of a
normal cone do **not** determine a rank-reducing Berline--Vergne deletion.
There are two exact obstructions.

1. Restriction from a side-`r` hive to an inner side-`r-1` triangle does not
   land in one fixed-boundary hive polytope.  It exposes `r-2` formerly
   interior coordinates as new boundary values.  For a full-dimensional hive
   these coordinates vary independently, so a fixed target exists only after
   imposing an interface lattice of codimension `r-2` or retaining a family
   of target boundaries.
2. Even after an interface is frozen, the identical labelled boundary
   rhombus can have different primitive quotient-lattice multiplicities in
   two actual closed hive normal cones.  The exact pair below gives
   multiplicities two and one.

Thus the naive planar boundary-ear/strip induction fails the V2 input
contract.  A surviving deletion must carry the complete closed tight set,
the saturated intrinsic lattice, the Smith data of every retained facet, and
the BV complement/Gram data.  No nonnegative BV correction identity for that
enriched move is proved here.

This is a proof-route obstruction only.  Both displayed closed cones have
positive low-codimension BV values; no KTT counterexample is claimed.

## 1. Finite definition of an actual closed hive normal cone

Let `Delta_r` be the standard triangular grid and let `I_r` be its interior
vertices.  With a fixed boundary `p`, write

```text
H(p) = {x in R^{I_r} : A_r x <= D_r p}.
```

The ambient coordinate lattice is `Lambda_r = Z^{I_r}`.  If `H` is not full
dimensional, let `M_H` be the saturated intrinsic tangent lattice

```text
M_H = Lambda_r cap lin(H-H).
```

For a face `F` choose any rational point `x_F` in its relative interior and
define its exact tight set

```text
T(F) = {i : (A_r x_F)_i = (D_r p)_i}.
```

This is independent of the chosen relative-interior point.  It is **closed**
in the finite, checkable sense

```text
T(F) = {j : slack_j vanishes on
             H cap intersection_{i in T(F)} {slack_i=0}}.
```

Closure is certified either by exact rational linear programming or by one
exact Farkas identity for every forced slack.  Merely choosing some tight
rhombi, without this closure, does not define an actual normal cone.

Restrict every tight row to `M_H`.  Let `L_F=M_H cap lin(F-F)`.  The intrinsic
normal lattice and cone are

```text
N_F = (M_H/L_F)^*
    = M_H^* cap span_R{A_i|_{M_H} : i in T(F)},

C_F = cone{A_i|_{M_H} : i in T(F)} in N_F tensor R.
```

The equality on the right uses the saturated lattice, not the lattice merely
generated by the displayed tight rows.  The finite closed-cone data contract
is therefore

```text
(r, p, M_H, F, T(F), N_F, primitive extreme rays of C_F,
 all exact slack-closure certificates, BV complement/Gram data).
```

For the orthogonal Berline--Vergne complement map, the scalar product on
`M_H` and the induced rational Gram matrix on `N_F` are part of the input.
They are not recoverable from the planar adjacency graph.

## 2. What a lattice-compatible ear deletion must contain

Suppose a proposed ear is an extreme primitive ray `u` of `C_F`, and the
retained codimension-one cone is

```text
D = cone(u_1,...,u_{q-1}).
```

Set

```text
N_D = N_F cap span_R(D).
```

This is the saturated retained-facet lattice.  The canonical transverse map
is

```text
pi_D : N_F -> N_F/N_D.
```

When the quotient has rank one, choose its primitive orientation.  Equivalently,
choose a primitive covector `phi_D` with kernel `N_D`.  Then

```text
pi_D(u) = m(u,D) * 1,
m(u,D)  = |<phi_D,u>|.
```

The integer `m(u,D)` is an incidence/lattice correction.  For a simplicial
full-rank cone it also satisfies

```text
m(u,D) = [N_F : Z u_1+...+Z u_q]
         / [N_D : Z u_1+...+Z u_{q-1}].
```

An admissible BV deletion must specify this map, preserve the chosen
complement data, and give an exact valuation identity.  Deleting the same
planar rhombus does not fix `m(u,D)`.

## 3. Rank-uniform boundary restriction obstruction

Delete the unit strip adjacent to the side `x+y=r` and retain

```text
Delta_{r-1} = {(x,y) : x>=0, y>=0, x+y<=r-1}.
```

The newly exposed side contains the vertices

```text
(i,r-1-i),  0 <= i <= r-1.
```

Its endpoints were already on the old boundary.  The other `r-2` vertices

```text
(i,r-1-i),  1 <= i <= r-2,
```

belong to `I_r`, hence are coordinates of `Lambda_r`.  If `H` is
full-dimensional, its tangent lattice is all of `Lambda_r`, and projection
onto those `r-2` coordinates is surjective.  Therefore their values vary
independently on a relative open subset of `H`.

It follows that restriction sends `H` to a family

```text
{H_{r-1}(p') : p' ranges over an (r-2)-dimensional interface set},
```

not to one fixed `H_{r-1}(p')`.  To force a single target boundary one must
replace `M_H` by the sublattice on which all `r-2` exposed coordinates vanish,
or retain those coordinates as parameters.  This is an exact intrinsic
lattice issue, before any Todd calculation.

Consequently the operation "remove one boundary strip and apply the smaller
hive theorem" is not a map between the fixed-boundary polytopes or their
normal cones appearing in Route B.  A fibered/interface version is a new
theorem obligation; it cannot be silently treated as a local planar ear.

## 4. Exact same-ear pair with incompatible quotient corrections

Both examples are full-dimensional side-four hives, so their intrinsic
coordinate and dual lattices are `Z^3`.  In the standard row ordering, row
`8` is the rhombus

```text
A(1,1),
```

which meets the deleted hypotenuse unit strip.  It is an extreme normal ray
in both closed vertex cones.

### Multiplicity two

Take

```text
lambda = mu = (12,8,4,0),
nu = (18,14,10,6),
v = (26,32,38).
```

The exact active original rows are

```text
(2,3,7,8,9,10,12,13,15).
```

The primitive extreme normal rays are

```text
u8  = ( 1,-1,-1),
u9  = (-1, 1,-1),
u10 = (-1,-1, 1).
```

Their determinant has absolute value four.  The other distinct active
directions are exact nonnegative combinations

```text
(-1,0,0) = (u9+u10)/2,
(0,-1,0) = (u8+u10)/2,
(0,0,-1) = (u8+u9)/2,
```

so the displayed three rays generate the full closed cone.

Delete the boundary ear `u8`.  For the retained facet generated by `u9,u10`,

```text
u9 cross u10 = (0,2,2),
phi_D = (0,1,1),
|<phi_D,u8>| = 2.
```

Thus the retained row lattice has saturation index two and the primitive
quotient multiplicity is two.

### Multiplicity one

Take

```text
lambda = (12,9,3,0),
mu     = (10,8,3,0),
nu     = (18,14,8,5),
v      = (29,32,39).
```

Exactly rows

```text
(4,8,12)
```

are active.  Their primitive normals are

```text
w4  = (1,-1, 0),
w8  = (1,-1,-1),
w12 = (0,-1, 0).
```

They have determinant one, and `w8` is the identical labelled boundary ear
`A(1,1)`.  Delete it.  The retained facet satisfies

```text
w4 cross w12 = (0,0,-1),
phi_D = (0,0,-1),
|<phi_D,w8>| = 1.
```

Hence the quotient multiplicity is one.

The abstract move in both examples is "delete one distinguished extreme ray
from a simplicial three-cone", and the distinguished ray is the same labelled
rhombus at the same grid position.  Its primitive quotient correction is not
the same.  The surrounding closed cone and its saturated facet lattice are
load-bearing data.

## 5. Precise surviving theorem obligation

The planar statement "every closed coarsening has a boundary ear" is
insufficient even if true.  A viable `CLOSED-CONE-DELETION` theorem must
instead prove all of the following uniformly:

1. an ear/strip exists in the **complete finite closed-cone data contract** of
   Section 1;
2. restriction with its variable interface produces a specified map or
   fiber decomposition of intrinsic lattices;
3. every retained facet is saturated explicitly and all quotient
   multiplicities are included;
4. the BV complement/Gram data transform by a stated exact formula; and
5. the resulting valuation correction is a nonnegative combination of a
   rank-independent family.

Sections 3--4 show that items 2--4 do not follow from planar coarsening
combinatorics.  Until such a lattice-aware identity is supplied, the naive
boundary deletion branch is

```text
DEAD: local planar ear data do not define the intrinsic BV deletion.
```

This leaves V2 Route A (global complete-fan effectivity) open and also leaves
open a stronger Route B proof using a genuinely lattice-aware, possibly
fibered deletion.
